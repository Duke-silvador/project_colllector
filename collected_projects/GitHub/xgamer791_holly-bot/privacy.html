HOLLI BOT

Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 September 2026

Holli Bot is an AI assistant app where you create bots, chat with them and let them work for you, with Holli Bot's AI, which runs on DeepSeek. What you keep in Holli Bot is stored in your own account, which only you can open. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

Your account and what it holds

Holli Bot requires an account. Everything the app keeps for you is stored in it: your bots and their settings, your chats and messages, your bots' memories, files you or your bots save or attach, routines, tasks between bots, activity logs, usage counts, your profile (the name and details you choose to share with your bots) and your settings, including the keys you add for other services. For your AI credits we also keep how many credits your plan gives you, how many are left this month (on Free, today), and how many requests and tokens this month's (or today's) used, and we count how much space your files take.

That data is held for us by Convex, the hosting provider that runs our backend, in the United States. It is kept so that you can sign in on any device and find the same bots and chats. Each account can read and change only its own data; we enforce that on our server, not only in the app.

We use your data only to run the app for you. We do not read it, analyse it, sell it, or share it with anyone, and we do not use it to train AI models.

The app on your device also keeps your sign-in session and, until they reach our server, any changes made while you were offline. Signing out sends those changes and removes them from the device.

Payments and your subscription

Every account starts on Free, which takes no payment. Paid plans are a subscription, and Stripe handles the payments. You enter your card or other payment details on Stripe's checkout page, and Stripe keeps them under its own privacy policy. We never see or store your full card number.

When you first subscribe, we give Stripe your account's email address and name, so it can bill you and send your receipts. From Stripe we keep your customer and subscription identifiers, your plan, whether you pay monthly or yearly, and your subscription's status and when it renews or ends, so Holli Bot knows which plan you're on. Settings → Plan opens Stripe's billing portal, where you can see your invoices, update your payment details or cancel.

When you buy AI credits, you pay once on Stripe's checkout page in the same way. We keep how many credits you bought, what you paid and when, and Stripe's identifiers for the payment, so the credits are added to your account once.

Deleting your account also deletes your customer record at Stripe, which cancels your subscription straight away. Stripe keeps records of past payments as the law requires.

Your plan's computer

Each paid subscription comes with a dedicated server of your own at Vultr, a cloud provider, in Chicago in the United States. It runs Holli Bot Computer, linked to your account, so your bots can work there around the clock with the same access to your account that the app has. Files your bots make on it, its web browser (including sites your bots sign in to), and plugins and SSH servers you add there are kept on that server rather than in your account. We don't look at what's on it, and it isn't backed up.

The app reaches it over an encrypted connection, with an address and a key that only your account is given. When you move to a smaller plan, those files are copied to your new server and the old one is deleted. When your subscription ends or your account is deleted, the server and everything on it is deleted.

Signing in

You sign in with Apple or with Google. We store the email address they give us, the name they share and, with Google, your profile picture address, along with an identifier for your Apple or Google account, so we can recognise you the next time you sign in and show who is signed in.

Sign in with Apple shares your name only the first time you use it. If you choose Apple's Hide My Email, we receive a relay address instead of your real one, and that is the address we store. Apple and Google handle sign-in under their own privacy terms.

Holli Bot's AI (DeepSeek)

When a bot works, the app on your device, or your plan's computer, sends what the bot needs to Holli Bot's AI: the conversation, the bot's instructions and relevant memories, your profile, and any files or images involved. It goes to our server, which passes it on to DeepSeek under Holli Bot's own DeepSeek account and passes DeepSeek's answer back to your bot. Our server doesn't keep what's in these requests or answers, and we don't read them. For each request it keeps only how many tokens it used, which model answered and what that came to, to count your AI credits.

DeepSeek receives these requests from Holli Bot, not from your own DeepSeek account, and handles them under its own privacy policy. Its services are operated from the People's Republic of China. What it receives can include your name and other details from your profile and your chats, since they're part of what your bots work with.

Web search, reading pages and other tools

When a bot searches the web, the search is sent to the service you set up for it (Tavily, Exa or Jina) with your key. When a bot reads a web page, the page's address is sent to Jina's reader service to fetch it, or the page is fetched directly. The first time a bot runs Python code, the Python runtime is downloaded from the jsDelivr CDN; the code itself runs on your device. Icons shown beside the sources a bot cites are loaded from Google's website-icon service, which receives those websites' addresses.

Plugins (MCP servers) and other services you connect receive what a bot sends them while using them.

Connected accounts (plugins)

You can connect accounts in Settings → Plugins so your bots can use them when you ask: Gmail, Outlook, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Google Sheets, Google Analytics, Google Search Console, Firebase, Outlook Calendar, GitHub, Todoist, Asana, Figma, Stripe, Vercel and Higgsfield. Connecting takes you to the service (Google, Microsoft, GitHub, Todoist, Asana, Figma or Higgsfield), which shows what Holli Bot is asking for, and nothing is connected unless you agree there. Stripe and Vercel are connected with a key or access token you make in them and paste into Holli Bot, and GitHub, Todoist, Asana and Figma can be too; what that key or token allows is what bots can do.

What bots can do with them. With Gmail or Outlook, bots can search and read your email, send email from your address, and delete email. Deleted email goes to your Trash (Gmail) or Deleted Items (Outlook), where you can get it back, unless you ask for it to be deleted for good. For that, Holli Bot asks Google for full access to your Gmail, the only permission that allows deleting for good, and Microsoft for access to read and change your Outlook mail. Holli Bot doesn't change your mailbox settings. With Google Calendar or Outlook Calendar, bots can see your calendars and events and add, change and delete events, inviting only the people you name, who get the invitation from you. With Google Drive, bots can find, read, create, change, share and delete your files, for which Holli Bot asks Google for full access to your Drive. With Google Sheets, bots can read, change and create your spreadsheets, and see the names of the files in your Drive to find them. With Google Analytics, bots can read your reports and can't change anything. With Google Search Console, bots can read your sites' search performance and index status and submit sitemaps. With Firebase, bots can see your Firebase projects and use the Google Cloud access that comes with them (the same the Firebase command line tools ask for): read and change your Cloud Firestore data, look up your apps' users, and make the other requests to your projects you ask for. With GitHub, bots can list, create, change and delete your repositories and their files, and make the other GitHub requests you ask for. With Todoist or Asana, bots can read, add, change and complete your tasks and see your projects. With Stripe, bots can do what your key allows, such as reading your balance, payments and customers, or creating payment links. With Vercel, bots can see your projects, deployments and their logs, and make changes such as redeploying. With Figma, bots can read your files, export images of them, and read and post comments. With Higgsfield, bots make images and videos with your Higgsfield credits. Bots do this only when you ask them to, or in routines you set up. With Auto-review on, a bot asks you before sending or deleting email (showing you exactly which emails), making a repository public, inviting people to an event, sharing a file, deleting things, and making changes through Stripe, Vercel or Firebase. It always asks before deleting email or Drive files for good, before deleting a repository, a Firestore document or a Vercel project, and before Stripe refunds, payouts and charges.

What we store. For each connection we store the account's email address or username (for a key, the name of the account it belongs to), the permissions you granted, and the access tokens or key that let your bots use it. They are encrypted on our server, never sent to your devices, and used only when one of your bots acts for you.

Where your data goes. When a bot reads an email, a file, an event, a task, a report or a record, our server fetches it from the service and passes it to the app, which sends it to Holli Bot's AI as part of the conversation (see Holli Bot's AI above). What a bot reads is kept only as part of your chats in your account. We don't keep a separate copy of your mailbox, files, calendars or other data, we don't read them, and we don't use them for anything but the work you ask for.

Google user data. Holli Bot's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We use data from Google (Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Google Sheets, Google Analytics, Google Search Console and Firebase) only to provide the features you use in Holli Bot. We transfer it only as needed to provide them (to Holli Bot's AI provider, DeepSeek, at your direction), for security, or to comply with the law. We never use it for advertising, never sell it, and never use it to train AI models, and no person reads it unless you ask us to (for example for support), or where security or the law requires it.

Disconnecting. Disconnect a service at any time in Settings → Plugins. Its tokens are deleted, and Holli Bot asks the service to take back its access where it can: Google (once none of your other Google connections is left, since Google takes back all of Holli Bot's access at once), GitHub, Todoist, Asana and Higgsfield. Microsoft and Figma offer no way to do that for a single app: the access simply stops being renewed, and you can also remove Holli Bot at account.microsoft.com or in your Figma settings. A key or access token you made yourself stays valid until you delete it in the service. Deleting your Holli Bot account disconnects everything the same way. You can also remove Holli Bot's access directly in each service's account settings.

Holli Bot Computer

Holli Bot Computer is an optional companion you can run on your own PC or Mac, so your bots can work around the clock and use that computer with the permissions you give them. When you link it to your account, it keeps your bots, chats, memories, files and keys in your account, like the app does, and signs in with a session of its own that you can end from Settings. Anything it kept before is moved into your account, and its old data folder is set aside on the computer for you to delete. Until it's linked, it keeps them on the computer.

SSH servers you add in Settings → Plugins, with the password or private key each one signs in with, are kept on the Holli Bot Computer your bots use, never in your account: the app is only shown their names, addresses and users, and your bots connect to them from that computer when you ask. Removing a server deletes its password or key there.

Your phone reaches it through a Cloudflare tunnel or your Wi-Fi network, protected by a pairing token that stays on your devices. Once it's linked, it also keeps in your account the address your devices can reach it at, an access key of its own for them, and when it last ran, so that Holli Bot on each device signed in to your account can connect to it. The access key stops working when you unlink the computer, and restarting Holli Bot Computer with --new-token replaces it.

Voice

Dictation uses your device's or browser's speech recognition, which may send your voice to Apple or Google to turn it into text. Read-aloud uses the voices built into your device. Holli Bot does not record or store audio.

Notifications

Notifications, if you turn them on, are shown by the app on your device. We do not run a push notification service.

What we never do

We do not run analytics, telemetry or advertising. We do not sell or rent personal information or share it beyond what this policy describes, we load no third-party trackers, and we do not build a profile of you. The app itself is served by GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, processes the network information needed to deliver it.

Deleting your data

To remove your account and everything in it, email us at chris@mangomarketeers.com from the address you sign in with. We erase your bots, chats, memories, files, routines, settings, keys and AI credits, your connected accounts, and your sign-in details, and cancel your subscription. It cannot be undone.

To erase what's in your account but keep the account, open Settings, then Data & Backup, then Erase all data. Signing out deletes nothing; your account is there when you sign in again.

Deleting your account also unlinks any Holli Bot Computer. A Holli Bot Computer that was never linked keeps its bots on that computer; delete them there.

Children

Holli Bot is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top of this page changes with it. Material changes will be noted in the app.

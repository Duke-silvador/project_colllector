RadioPod Privacy Policy

Effective 30 September 2026 · Togaware Pty Ltd

RadioPod does not collect your data. There is no account to create, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. Nothing about what you listen to is sent to us or to anyone else.

What RadioPod stores

RadioPod keeps a list of the radio stations you have saved and the playlists you have organised them into. That is the whole of the app's data. It is stored in one of two places, and you choose which.

  • Local-only mode. Tap Continue on the login screen and everything stays on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere. This is a fully supported way to use RadioPod, not a limited one.
  • Your Solid Pod. If you log in, your stations and playlists are encrypted before they leave your device and written to your own Pod on a Solid server. The server holding them, and its administrators, cannot read them. A server will collect the usual meta-data logs, depending on which Solid server is hosting your Pod.

Your security key and your WebID never leave your device. The key is used on the device to decrypt your data.

The unencrypted copy on your device

Android Auto starts the app by itself, before any login can happen, and will not wait for one. So RadioPod keeps a copy of your station names and stream addresses, unencrypted, in the app's private storage, so the car has something to show.

It holds no more than an ordinary playlist file would. It never contains your security key or your WebID, and it never leaves the device. Settings has a button to clear it.

The one third party

The only third party RadioPod contacts beyond a Solid server (if you are logged in), is the community-run Radio-Browser database, and only when you use Search.

  • It is sent your search text and the app's name and version, for example RadioPod 1.1.21, which their terms of use require.
  • It is not sent your WebID, your Pod address or your station library.
  • Radio-Browser offers an endpoint that apps call to report which station a listener picked, feeding its popularity ranking. RadioPod does not call it. Nothing about what you listen to leaves your device.

All of this is one short source file, lib/services/radio_browser.dart, and you are welcome to read it.

Playing a station

Playing a station connects directly to that station's stream, so the broadcaster sees a connection from your network, exactly as it would from any other radio player or a web browser. RadioPod adds nothing to that request. What the broadcaster does with that connection is covered by their privacy policy, not ours.

Children

RadioPod is suitable for all ages and collects no personal information from anyone, including children. Station streams are provided by third-party broadcasters and their content is not filtered or controlled by RadioPod.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version will be published at this address with a new effective date. RadioPod is free software and its full history is public, so any change to what the app does is visible in the repository.

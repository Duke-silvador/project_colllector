A quick checkpoint

What goes in? What comes out?

Imagine a model that predicts tomorrow’s temperature for your city.

  1. What information could you give it today?
  2. What output should the model produce?
  3. How would you check the prediction?
Reveal a suggested answer

Inputs: recent temperatures, today’s humidity and wind, the date and the location. Use information available when the prediction is made.

Expected output: one number, such as tomorrow’s maximum temperature in degrees Celsius. Define the place, time period and unit clearly.

Check: compare the prediction with tomorrow’s measured maximum temperature. Over many days, calculate how far the predictions are from the measurements and compare with a simple baseline, such as using today’s maximum.

Notice: tomorrow’s actual temperature must not be used as an input when predicting it today.

Make the ideas stick

A simple rhythm for each lesson.

01 /

Watch or listen

Focus on one idea at a time. Pause whenever you want to work through an example.

02 /

Explain it simply

Close the lesson and describe the idea in your own words, without relying on technical terms.

03 /

Try it yourself

Answer the checkpoint or complete a small exercise. Later, change one thing in the code and inspect the result.

04 /

Return and connect

Revisit the audio or transcript, correct misunderstandings and connect the idea to a new example.

Start today: complete the temperature checkpoint, then invent one example of predicting a number and one of predicting a category. For each, identify the inputs, output and a way to check success. A flexible 20–40 minute study session is a suggestion, not a deadline.

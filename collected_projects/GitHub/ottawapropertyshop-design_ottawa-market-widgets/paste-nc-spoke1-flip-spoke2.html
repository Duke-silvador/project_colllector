The HST Rebate Snapshot: Up to $130,000 Back

For a limited window, Ontario's enhanced new housing rebate returns the full 13% HST on a new home — the 8% provincial part plus the 5% federal part, cost-shared by the two governments under CRA Notice 346 and Ontario Regulation 196/26. On a home at $1,000,000 that is up to $130,000 — the single largest number in most buyers' math, and the reason builder incentive marketing suddenly sounds so generous. First-time buyers have a second program: the federal GST rebate of up to $50,000 on a first new home. The snapshot:

Three cautions. First, there is no separate "federal 13% rebate" — anyone selling you one is describing the Ontario rebate's federal cost-share as if it were a second cheque. Second, the enhanced rebate is usually assigned to the builder at signing, which is why prices are quoted "net of HST" (next section) — if you turn out not to qualify, the amount comes back onto your closing bill. Third, the window is real: purchase agreements signed April 1, 2026 through March 31, 2027 qualify; after that date the enhanced rebate ends unless the province extends it.

Net of HST: Why Two Identical Houses Have Two Different Prices

"Net of HST" means the sticker price already assumes the rebate has been assigned to the builder — the price you see is the price after the government money is applied. The catch is that not every builder presents it the same way, and comparing stickers across presentation styles is how buyers overpay or walk away from the better deal:

• Before-rebate pricing — Minto and eQ Homes show prices before the rebate (eQ shows them without HST entirely). The sticker looks high; the closing number is lower.

• After-rebate pricing — Mattamy and Cardel show prices with the rebate already in. The sticker looks sharp; there is no second rebate waiting at closing.

• Both columns — HN Homes shows the before and after figures side by side, lot by lot. This is the honest format; ask every other builder for the same two numbers.

Then add new construction closing costs Ontario buyers forget: development charges, utility connection fees, the Tarion enrolment fee and land transfer tax all sit on top of any sticker, whichever style it is. A before-rebate sticker plus closing costs can still beat an after-rebate sticker — but only if somebody does the full math on the same lot, the same day. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a realtor to buy new construction homes in Ottawa?

No law requires one — but the builder's sales staff work for the builder, the price already includes the cost of agent representation, and the first-visit rule means hiring an agent after a solo first visit can be too late. Representation costs you nothing extra; going without it protects nobody but the builder.

What builder incentives are Ottawa builders offering right now?

The usual levers: design studio bonuses, closing-cost assistance, mortgage rate buydowns and discounts on standing inventory. They change weekly and are lot-specific — the gist by builder is in the comparison panel above; the current detail is in the free Ottawa Builder Deals guide.

How does the HST rebate on a new home in Ontario work?

Under CRA Notice 346 and Ontario Regulation 196/26, Ontario's enhanced rebate returns the full 13% HST — the 8% provincial part plus the 5% federal part — on new homes up to $1,000,000, worth up to $130,000, for purchase agreements signed April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027. The benefit holds to $1.5M and phases down above it. Most buyers assign the rebate to the builder at signing, which is why prices are quoted net of HST.

What does "net of HST" pricing mean?

The sticker already has the rebate applied — you are looking at the after-rebate price. Some builders (Minto, eQ Homes) quote before the rebate instead, so never compare stickers across builders without asking which style each one uses.

Can first-time buyers combine the federal GST rebate with the Ontario rebate?

Carefully: the Ontario rebate already includes a 5% federal part — that is the cost-share. The separate federal first-time buyer GST rebate (up to $50,000, purchase agreements on or after March 20, 2025) has its own qualification rules, and the two programs interact. Have the math done on your specific purchase before you sign — it is a short calculation that can be worth tens of thousands of dollars.

How much are new construction closing costs in Ontario?

On top of the price: development charges, utility connection fees, the Tarion enrolment fee, land transfer tax and legal fees. The total surprises most buyers, so budget for it from day one — the closing-costs guide in this series itemizes every line.

How much deposit do I need on a new build?

It varies by builder and home type — from roughly 3% staged with some builders to considerably more on detached homes with others. The schedule matters as much as the total: when each instalment falls due is what squeezes buyers. The free guide carries the deposit structures builder by builder.

I already visited a sales centre alone. Is it too late?

Maybe not — it depends on the builder and whether you signed the guest sheet. Call me at 613-314-1199 before you sign anything else or visit again; in many cases there is still a route to proper representation.

Is there a cooling-off period on a new home purchase?

For new condos, yes — Ontario law gives buyers 10 days to rescind the agreement. For freehold homes there is no general cooling-off period, which is why the agreement review has to happen before signing, not after.

What does the Tarion warranty cover?

New homes in Ontario carry the Tarion 1-2-7 warranty: one year on workmanship and materials, two years on building systems and water penetration, seven years on major structural defects. The claim deadlines are strict — the Tarion guide in this series walks through them.

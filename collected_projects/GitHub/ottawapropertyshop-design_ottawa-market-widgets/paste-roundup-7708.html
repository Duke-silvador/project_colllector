The golf course at the heart of it

Most suburbs have a park at their centre; Stonebridge has a golf course. Stonebridge Golf Club on Hawktree Ridge is a public 18-hole course designed by Ted Baker and later redesigned by Doug Carrick, a certified Audubon Cooperative Sanctuary with a clubhouse, bistro and patio overlooking the final hole. The course winds through the community in a figure-eight, so a large share of lots back directly onto fairways — no rear neighbours, open green views, and a quiet that a standard subdivision cannot copy. The community that grew around it since the early 2000s — built by Minto, Mattamy, Monarch, Uniform, Tamarack, Cardel and Phoenix — holds roughly three thousand four hundred homes, including a bungalow bench, with main-floor living built in, that no other Barrhaven pocket matches for downsizers. One Stonebridge-only detail worth knowing before you buy: homes here carry a special area levy that runs until October 2029, on top of standard municipal taxes — small, dated, and exactly the kind of line item to confirm on any offer.

Schools in Stonebridge

Adrienne Clarkson Elementary School (JK–6) sits inside Stonebridge itself, on Stoneway Drive beside Stonecrest Park — triple-track, with English, Early French Immersion and Middle French Immersion streams, and an extended-day program run inside the school. St. Cecilia School, the Catholic elementary option, stands on Cambrian Road at the community’s southwest edge, beside the Minto Recreation Complex. Secondary options named for the area are John McCrae Secondary School, St. Mother Teresa High School and St. Joseph High School. One caution that matters: catchments split by street, so your exact school follows your exact address — always confirm on the board locator before you buy.

Parks, the river and the bridge

The Stonebridge Trail runs along the Jock River on the community’s north edge — a green, quiet walking corridor that feels a world away from the fairways. Kilbirnie Park and W. C. Levesque Park carry the big fields and play structures, Stonecrest Park adds a splash pad beside the school, and the Minto Recreation Complex — Barrhaven on Cambrian Road brings the pools, two NHL-sized arenas and a premium turf field to Stonebridge’s doorstep. To the east, the Vimy Memorial Bridge — the white arches in both photographs on this page, which I took myself this fall — carries Strandherd Drive across the Rideau River to Riverside South, giving Stonebridge a second way out of Barrhaven that did not exist a generation ago.

The Vimy Memorial Bridge from the parkland on the Barrhaven side — the arches that mark Stonebridge’s eastern gate. My own photograph.

Shopping and daily life

There is no large-format retail inside Stonebridge itself — it is a purely residential golf-course pocket, and that is the point of it. Daily life runs a few minutes north and west: the Barrhaven Marketplace district and the Strandherd Drive corridor carry the big grocery runs, big-box stores, banks, dentists, clinics and pharmacies, and Greenbank Road is the spine that ties Stonebridge to the rest of Barrhaven — north to Longfields, the Transitway stations and, eventually, the Greenbelt. Most weeks, life here runs on the course, the trail and the crescents, with the errands a short drive away.

Saturday, November 21: the Barrhaven Winter Craft Event at Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School on Berrigan Drive — Barrhaven’s own winter craft stop, one neighbourhood north of Stonebridge.

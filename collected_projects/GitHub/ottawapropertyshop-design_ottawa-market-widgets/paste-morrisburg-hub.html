Living in Morrisburg — the Town and the Waterfront

Daily life in Morrisburg runs between two lines: the commercial spine the town rebuilt for itself after the flood — the plaza and the services along Ottawa Street and County Road 31 as it climbs to the 401 — and the water. Lakeshore Drive, the old river road, carries the town’s finest older homes along the north shore, past the waterfront park and the beach, with the river doing what it has always done here: setting the town’s mood. This is a complete small town — its own elementary school and Catholic school, an arena, a full-service main street by Seaway standards — that also happens to live on one of the great rivers of the continent, with ocean-going ships passing the foot of the street.

Iroquois — the Planned Town at the Lock

Twelve kilometres west, Iroquois is Morrisburg’s sister and mirror. Where Morrisburg was half-flooded and rebuilt in place, Iroquois was moved whole — the old waterfront town was demolished for the Seaway and a new, planned town was laid out about a kilometre and a half north, all at once, in the 1950s. The result reads in its streets: a coherent postwar town of bungalows and family homes around its schools, its own elementary school, and the building that anchors the whole hub’s school path — Seaway District High School. A Short History — West Williamsburg to the Seaway Town

The settlement began as West Williamsburg, a Loyalist-era community on the St. Lawrence in old Williamsburgh Township, in the path of the Williamsburg Canals built between 1843 and 1856 to carry shipping past the river’s rapids. In 1851 it was renamed Morrisburg for James Morris of Brockville — canal promoter and the first Postmaster General of the Province of Canada — and the Grand Trunk Railway arrived in 1855. The village incorporated in 1860 with mills and manufacturing on the river, and grew into the commercial centre of South Dundas. Just east of town, the ground holds older history still: the Battle of Crysler’s Farm, fought on November 11, 1813, where a small British and Canadian force turned back an American invasion — its battlefield now lies under the Seaway’s water, and its monument stands at Upper Canada Village.

Then came the project that remade the whole shore. The St. Lawrence Seaway and the Moses-Saunders power dam required a new lake, and on July 1, 1958, the cofferdam was blasted and Lake St. Lawrence rose. About a third of Morrisburg — its waterfront, its business district, its main street — went under permanently. More than 80 homes and buildings were moved to higher ground first; the downtown was demolished and rebuilt as a shopping plaza; the railway shifted about 1.1 kilometres north. Iroquois was moved entire. Ten villages east of here were lost completely — the Lost Villages — and some of their rescued buildings were reassembled just east of Morrisburg as Upper Canada Village. On January 1, 1998, Morrisburg and Iroquois amalgamated with Matilda and Williamsburgh Townships into the Municipality of South Dundas — the same four districts this hub covers today.

Upper Canada Village — the Village the Seaway Saved

The entrance to Upper Canada Village, in Williamsburgh Township east of Morrisburg — the 1860s village assembled from buildings rescued ahead of the 1958 flood. Photo: Dennis G. Jarvis, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Upper Canada Village is the Seaway’s great act of salvage. As the waters rose, heritage buildings from the doomed communities — houses, mills, a tavern, churches’ worth of history — were moved to high ground east of Morrisburg and reassembled as a living 1860s village, opened to the public in 1961 and run ever since by the St. Lawrence Parks Commission. More than 40 heritage buildings stand here now: working woollen, grist and saw mills, a bakery, a cheese factory, trades shops and costumed interpreters working the year 1866. For the hub it is three things at once: the region’s signature attraction, a major seasonal employer, and the standing proof of what this shore lost in 1958 — and saved.

The Townships — Matilda and Williamsburgh Country

Wrapped around the two towns sit the two township districts, and they are not filler between the towns — for many buyers they are the point. Matilda Township (703) is Iroquois’s ring: concession roads running north from the river through hamlets like Brinston and Dundela, farm properties, country homes on acreage, and the Lakeshore Drive corridor east and west of the town. Williamsburgh Township (704) is Morrisburg’s ring: the same concession grid north and east of town, the hamlets of Williamsburg, Dunbar, Elma and Glen Becker, the Mariatown pocket on the river immediately west of Morrisburg, and Upper Canada Village itself at its eastern edge. The stock is farmhouses, country homes, hobby farms and land — bigger lots, longer driveways, and the full rural homework list in the Servicing section below. Buyers cross-shop the towns and the townships constantly: the towns sell services at the door, the townships sell space.

Servicing — the Morrisburg Hub Homework

Morrisburg and Iroquois are serviced towns wrapped in rural townships, and the line between the two is a lot line, not a guess. Before any purchase in the hub:

  • Confirm the services for the exact lot with the Municipality of South Dundas — whether the property connects to municipal water and sewer or runs on a private system, and what connection charges apply. Never assume from the street name or the town boundary on a map.

  • On a well and septic property — the norm across both townships — budget for a potability and bacteria water test, a flow check, and a septic inspection with the tank pumped and the bed assessed. A replacement bed is one of the largest cheques a rural buyer can inherit.

  • Heating history matters — oil, propane, electric and wood heat are all common out here, and each carries a very different monthly reality through a St. Lawrence winter.

  • For pre-flood-age homes, look hard at foundation, wiring and insulation before conditions come off. Charm and knob-and-tube are frequent roommates in nineteenth-century stock.

  • On riverfront lots, ask about shoreline and flood history specifically — this shore was remade by a flood once, on purpose, and shoreline allowances and erosion are standard questions on any Lakeshore or County Road 2 property.

None of this is a reason to avoid the hub — half the Seaway shore lives happily on some version of it. It is a reason to buy with your eyes open, and it is standard practice in every offer I write out here.

Schools — One Path for the Whole Hub

This is the fact that organizes the whole hub, and the reason this ring of guides is built the way it is: whichever of the four districts you buy in — Morrisburg, Iroquois, Matilda or Williamsburgh — your family traces one public path. The younger child starts at one of two elementary schools, and the older one finishes at the hub’s single high school, Seaway District High School in Iroquois. District by district:

  • Morrisburg (701): Morrisburg Public School (Upper Canada District School Board), 16 Second Street — Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6. Morrisburg’s own elementary school, on the high ground in the middle of town, feeding Seaway District High School for Grades 7 to 12.

  • Iroquois (702): Iroquois Public School (UCDSB), 66 Lakeview Drive — Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6, English and French Immersion both offered. Then the shortest high-school trip in the hub: Seaway is in the same town.

  • The high school: Seaway District High School (UCDSB), 2 Beach Street, Iroquois — Grades 7 to 12, about 400 students. The school opened in 1957 in the rebuilt town, and Morrisburg’s high-school students joined it in 1967 when Morrisburg Collegiate closed — the two towns have shared one high school ever since.

  • Matilda Township (703): the township has no school of its own — its rural addresses feed Iroquois Public School or Morrisburg Public School by address, and then Seaway District High School. Which elementary school applies is address-specific: run the exact address through the UCDSB locator before you buy.

  • Williamsburgh Township (704): the township’s designated public elementary school is Morrisburg Public School, feeding Seaway District High School — the township’s children ride into Morrisburg for elementary and on to Iroquois for secondary.

  • Catholic: St. Mary-St. Cecilia Catholic School (Catholic District School Board of Eastern Ontario), 28 Shea Drive, Morrisburg — Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8, Core French program, about 250 students. Its name joins the two towns it serves. There is no Catholic secondary school inside the hub — Catholic secondary assignment for South Dundas addresses is address-specific, so confirm it with the CDSBEO locator directly.

  • French-language boards: assignments for hub addresses are address-specific — run your exact address through the French boards’ locators rather than relying on any summary, including this one.

Read the path the way a family lives it: two elementary doors, one high school, four districts. That is why the guides in this program are organized by school catchments first — where your little one goes to primary school and where their older sister goes to secondary is the question every buying family actually asks, and in South Dundas the answer is one path, shared by the whole shore.

Getting to Ottawa — and Reading the Map

Morrisburg sits where the hub’s three roads meet: Highway 401 across the north side of both towns, County Road 2 along the water through every riverfront street, and County Road 31 climbing north from Morrisburg through Williamsburgh Township and Winchester toward Ottawa. Iroquois is 12 straight-line kilometres west; Williamsburg village is 10 kilometres north; Cornwall is about half an hour east on the 401, Prescott about 35 minutes west, and downtown Ottawa about an hour and a quarter by the 401 and 416.

Questions Buyers Ask About Morrisburg

Is Morrisburg, Ontario a good place to live?
For the right buyer, it is one of the most complete small towns on the St. Lawrence. You get a real town — schools, an arena, a waterfront, a main street rebuilt after the Seaway flood — at prices far below Ottawa, with Highway 401 at the door and Cornwall about half an hour east. The honest trade-offs: bigger shopping and the hospital are drives, there is no public transit, and daily life runs on a car. Buyers who choose Morrisburg are usually choosing the river, the space and the value on purpose — and the town rewards the choice with a community that has quite literally rebuilt itself once already and kept its character doing it.

Which schools serve Morrisburg and Iroquois?
One public path serves the whole hub. In Morrisburg, children start at Morrisburg Public School (Upper Canada District School Board, 16 Second Street, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6). In Iroquois, they start at Iroquois Public School (UCDSB, 66 Lakeview Drive, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6, English and French Immersion). Both feed Seaway District High School in Iroquois (UCDSB, 2 Beach Street, Grades 7 to 12) — the hub’s one high school. Matilda Township’s rural addresses feed Iroquois Public School or Morrisburg Public School by address, and Williamsburgh Township feeds Morrisburg Public School. Catholic elementary is St. Mary-St. Cecilia Catholic School in Morrisburg (CDSBEO, 28 Shea Drive, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8). Always confirm the school assigned to an exact address with the board before you buy.

How far is Morrisburg from Ottawa?
About 71 kilometres straight-line from downtown — roughly an hour and a quarter by road in normal conditions. The run is simple: Highway 401 west from the Morrisburg or Iroquois interchange to Highway 416, then straight north into the city; or County Road 31 north through Winchester for the scenic version. Cornwall is about half an hour east, Prescott about 35 minutes west along the river, and the US border crossing at Ogdensburg is just past Prescott. Winter adds its opinion, as it does everywhere along the river, but there is no route-finding in this commute.

What happened to Morrisburg in 1958?
The St. Lawrence Seaway happened. On July 1, 1958, a cofferdam was blasted open and the new Lake St. Lawrence rose over about a third of the town — the old waterfront, the business district and the main street went under permanently. Before the water came, more than 80 homes and buildings were moved to higher ground, the downtown was demolished and rebuilt as a modern shopping plaza, and the railway was shifted about a kilometre north. Whole villages to the east were lost entirely — they are remembered today as the Lost Villages — and some of their rescued buildings became Upper Canada Village, just east of town. Morrisburg is the town that was half-drowned and rebuilt itself.

What is Upper Canada Village?
One of Canada’s great heritage parks, sitting in Williamsburgh Township just east of Morrisburg. When the Seaway flooding came, historic buildings from the doomed villages were moved here instead of being lost, and the park opened in 1961 as a living 1860s village: more than 40 heritage buildings, working mills, tradespeople, a bakery, a cheese factory and costumed interpreters. For buyers it matters beyond tourism — it is the hub’s biggest employer of summer students, its signature day out, and the reason half the province has driven past Morrisburg’s door at least once.

Morrisburg or Iroquois — what is the difference?
They are sister towns twelve kilometres apart, rebuilt in the same Seaway decade, and buyers cross-shop them constantly. Morrisburg is the bigger town: the main commercial centre of South Dundas, the elementary school and the Catholic school, the waterfront and the plaza. Iroquois is the planned town: moved whole about a kilometre and a half north in the 1950s and laid out fresh, it holds the Seaway lock and control dam, its own elementary school and the hub’s high school, Seaway District High School. Morrisburg sells town services and river frontage; Iroquois sells the high school at the door and the ships passing the lock. The townships around them — Matilda around Iroquois, Williamsburgh around Morrisburg — sell the country version of both.

Are homes outside the towns on well and septic?
Almost always, yes — that is the honest answer for the township concessions, and it is one of the first checks on any purchase here. Morrisburg and Iroquois themselves are serviced towns, but the line between town services and private systems is a lot line, not a guess. On a well and septic property, budget for a potability and bacteria water test, a flow check, and a septic inspection with the tank pumped and the bed assessed. Before you buy anywhere in the hub, confirm the services for the exact lot with the Municipality of South Dundas.

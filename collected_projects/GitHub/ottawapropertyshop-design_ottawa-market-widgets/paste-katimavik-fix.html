The Short Answer

Katimavik Elementary School is an Ottawa-Carleton District School Board (OCDSB) elementary school at 64 Chimo Drive in Kanata (K2L 1Y9 · 613-592-5462), teaching Grades 4 to 8 — the junior and intermediate half of its shared catchment, with a school day that runs 08:45–15:15. The board publishes its boundary as a single entry — the Katimavik Elementary School Grades 4 to 8 English Program with Core French and Early French Immersion Attendance Boundary on its official elementary boundary index, updated January 27, 2025 — covering the English Program with Core French and Early French Immersion together, and answers for one exact address at a time through its School Locator, listed as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. The path through this catchment is one clear line: Castlefrank Elementary School (Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3) → Katimavik Elementary School (Grades 4 to 8) → Earl of March Secondary School (Grades 9 to 12).

One honest note on ratings, because families look for the number: Katimavik does not appear in the Fraser Institute’s 2025 elementary report card, so this guide prints no Fraser rating for it. That absence is a gap in the report’s coverage, not a verdict on the school — the Institute’s rating is built from Grade 3 and Grade 6 EQAO indicators, and Katimavik’s students arrive in Grade 4, after the Grade 3 assessments, so no rating is published for a school whose cohort begins at Grade 4. First, the board decides, not the map: the school at the end of the street is not evidence, and proximity is a hint, not an answer — the locator result on the exact address is. Second, this school’s two tracks share one line: the board publishes Katimavik’s English and Early French Immersion boundary as a single entry, so there is one line to check, not two. One Catchment, Two Schools: Why Katimavik Starts at Grade 4

Families comparing this side of Kanata always ask the same question from the other direction: why does Katimavik Elementary start at Grade 4? It looks like a mistake. It is deliberate — one shared catchment split across two buildings by age, and the history is documented in the board’s own papers. Katimavik Elementary School, on Chimo Drive, opened in 1981 as a full Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8 school, expanded twice as the neighbourhood grew — the second expansion, in 1984, added room for intermediate students — and then outgrew itself anyway: by about 2010 it held more than 800 students, roughly 200 percent over capacity, with a dozen portables in the yard. Castlefrank Elementary School, on McCurdy Drive, was built in 1988 as the community grew — it officially opened on May 9, 1991 — and on September 7, 2004 it too became a full Kindergarten to Grade 8 school.

The split came out of the board’s Kanata South Accommodation Review: OCDSB Report 12-004 of January 31, 2012, and the Board’s decision of April 24, 2012. From September 2013, Katimavik became the junior and intermediate school (Grades 4 to 8, English and Early French Immersion) and Castlefrank the primary school (Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3), phased in so no child had to bounce between schools. Read from this building’s side, the design is the point: children arrive at Katimavik in Grade 4 from Castlefrank Elementary, already neighbours, and spend five years in the bigger building before high school. Same streets, same neighbourhood, start to finish — and that is the feeder story in one line: Castlefrank’s children continue to Katimavik for Grades 4 to 8, and then on to Earl of March Secondary School for high school.

One program note belongs here, stated plainly because families ask about it at exactly this point: Middle French Immersion is an entry-point program, and it is not offered at Katimavik — a student in this catchment entering Middle French Immersion goes to Bridlewood Community Elementary School for that track, the destination Castlefrank’s own site names for its students continuing in Middle French Immersion. The English Program and Early French Immersion tracks run at Katimavik itself. Questions Families Ask First

Does buying a home inside the Katimavik catchment guarantee my child a place at the school?
No. School assignment is always the school board’s decision, boundaries can change, and programs fill. A home inside the line is a strong starting point for a search — never a guarantee. Confirm the schools assigned to the exact address with the board’s school locator, and directly with any other school you are considering, before you firm up an offer.

How do I check which school a Kanata address belongs to?
Use the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board’s official School Locator, which the board lists as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. Enter the exact address — not just the street or the neighbourhood — because a boundary can run along lot lines and put one side of a street in a different catchment from the other.

Why does Katimavik start at Grade 4?
Because the board designed it that way. Katimavik and Castlefrank are one catchment split across two buildings by age: the Kanata South Accommodation Review (OCDSB Report 12-004, decided April 24, 2012, effective September 2013) made Katimavik the Grades 4 to 8 school and Castlefrank the primary school, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3, to relieve a Katimavik that had passed 800 students at roughly 200 percent of capacity. The young children get a small school of about 300; the older children get the larger building — same neighbourhood throughout.

Where do Katimavik students go after Grade 8?
Castlefrank Elementary School (Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3) → Katimavik Elementary School (Grades 4 to 8) → Earl of March Secondary School (Grades 9 to 12). That is the designated path for this catchment — the same path both schools’ own websites describe, and the one the board’s School Locator returns for addresses across the catchment.

What if my child is in Middle French Immersion?
That track has its own destination: Middle French Immersion is an entry-point program, and it is not offered at Katimavik — a student in this catchment entering Middle French Immersion goes to Bridlewood Community Elementary School, the destination Castlefrank’s own site names for its students continuing in the program. The English Program with Core French and the Early French Immersion program both run at Katimavik. If your child’s program is a specific track, check that track’s entry with the board before you rely on the line.

Does Katimavik have a Fraser Institute rating?
No — and it is worth understanding why. Katimavik does not appear in the Fraser Institute’s 2025 elementary report card. The Institute’s rating is built from nine academic indicators drawn from Grade 3 and Grade 6 EQAO results, and Katimavik’s students arrive in Grade 4 — they write their Grade 3 assessments at their primary school — so the Institute publishes no rating for a school whose cohort begins at Grade 4. The absence of a rating is a coverage gap in the report, not a judgement on the school — weigh the school on its programs, its size and your own visit, the way this guide weighs its boundary on the board’s documents.

If my child needs a bus, does living in the catchment mean they get one?
Not automatically. Transportation in Ottawa is run by OSTA, and eligibility is a distance test on top of the boundary: a student’s address must be inside the designated attendance boundary and beyond the board’s walk-zone distance from the school. Inside the walk zone, students walk. OSTA’s “Do I Qualify?” check answers for one address at a time, and the board’s transportation page sets the rules out.

Rideau Township was Ottawa's rural southwest before amalgamation folded it into the city in 2001 — two survey townships, North Gower and Marlborough, joined in 1975 and still farming country at heart: concession roads, century farmhouses, estate acreages, river lots and one of the largest protected forests in the region, all of it inside the City of Ottawa and inside one school-and-services orbit around Manotick, North Gower and Kemptville.

Rideau Township Homes for Sale Along the Kars Corridor — Area 8007

South of Roger Stevens Drive and east of McCordick Road, the township runs down to the Rideau River at Kars — the village first called Wellington, renamed Kars, and built on the river trade: A. J. Eastman's steam sawmill stood at the mouth of Stevens Creek from 1852, and the wharves at Garlick's and Lindsay's landings shipped what the farms and forests produced. Rideau Valley Drive South still reads like a museum row — the stone Lindsay House of 1850, the Martin House, the Bryan House, Trinity United Church and St. John's Anglican, moved on skids across Stevens Creek in the winter of 1904 — most of them designated heritage properties today.

The housing stock follows the corridor: village homes in Kars itself, riverfront and river-view properties along the shore roads, and concession homes on the roads running south. Baxter Conservation Area, at 7498 Carter Road, puts protected river shore, trails and picnic ground at the district's edge — the trailhead and the swimming area are what matter day to day. This is the district for buyers who want the river without leaving the township.

Rideau Township Homes for Sale in the Malakoff and Forest Country — Area 8008

The southwest quadrant is old Marlborough Township — the deepest country in the guide. Pierce's Corners, where John Pierce settled in 1826, was the crossroads that mattered: taverns, the first council meetings, and the Malakoff post office, opened in 1856 under a Crimean War battle name and famous locally as 'the Windfall'. The former Marlborough Township Hall of 1855 still stands on Pierce Road, still working as a recreation centre with its rink and ball field. Malakoff itself stayed a name on the map and a farming locality — which is exactly the point of this district.

East of the crossroads the land rises into the Marlborough Forest — 8,149 hectares of City of Ottawa forest (the City-owned figure), trails, wetlands and hunting country under provincial regulation, the wild backyard of the whole township. Homes here are scattered country properties, hobby farms and the golf-course pockets around Merlyn Wilson Road and Golf Course Lane, and this district carries the township's deepest shelf of vacant land by a wide margin.

Burritts Rapids Homes for Sale — Area 8011

At the township's southwest corner the Rideau River splits around an island, and Burritts Rapids grew up on both banks — founded in 1793 by Loyalist Stephen Burritt, bridged from 1824, and locked into the Rideau Canal when Lock 17 was built in 1830 and the Oxford Snie channel cut the island free. Christ Church Anglican, begun in 1831 on the north bank, still anchors the Ottawa side; the lockstation, the dam and the community hall on Grenville Street anchor the rest. The village straddles the city boundary — the north bank is Ottawa, the south bank and island are North Grenville — and this guide's district covers the Ottawa-side search area, which catches village homes on both banks in practice.

The stock is small and characterful: older village homes, waterfront and water-view properties along River Road and Donnelly Drive, and the kind of lots that rarely turn over. Burritts Rapids prices like what it is — a canal village with a finite number of houses — and the live snapshot below is the honest way to read it.

Rideau Township — Area Profile and History

The township's bones are two survey townships. North Gower Township was surveyed in 1791 and named for John Leveson-Gower, an Admiralty lord; Marlborough Township, to its south and west, took the Duke of Marlborough's name. Roger Stevens, Marlborough's first settler, drowned in the creek that still carries his name in 1793 — Roger Stevens Drive, the township's east-west spine, is named for him. The two townships merged as Rideau Township in 1975 and joined the City of Ottawa in the 2001 amalgamation, which is why this countryside answers to city hall while looking like it never left the county.

Country infrastructure here has its own history. The North Gower Rural Telephone Company, formed in 1910, strung party lines between Carsonby, Pierce's Corners, Reeve Craig and Kars — the whole township on shared lines until dial service arrived in 1961. The crossroads hamlets were the service centres: Pierce's Corners with its taverns and council meetings, Malakoff with its post office, Kars with its mills, wharves and stores.

The Marlborough Forest is the district's giant fact: 8,149 hectares owned by the City of Ottawa — the figure the City itself publishes for its holding — of forest, wetland and Canadian Shield outcrop, threaded by the Rideau Trail and the Cedar Grove Nature Trail off Roger Stevens Drive, with parking lots along Roger Stevens Drive and Paden Road. It is working conservation country: deer, grouse, moose, beaver and herons in the wetlands, and hunting permitted in season under provincial rules — blaze orange is the local etiquette in the fall woods.

Burritts Rapids closes the township's story at the river. Stephen Burritt, a Loyalist from Connecticut by way of Vermont, settled the rapids in 1793; the first bridge crossed in 1824; the canal works of 1830 — a single lock, Lock 17, with a modern lift of 3.2 metres, and a dam still spanning 68 metres of river — made the island and the village together. No lockmaster's house survives (the 1914 demolition and its 1969 replacement saw to that), and that honesty is part of the village's charm: what stands is what work left behind — the lock, the church of 1831, the hall, and the houses.

Living in Rideau Township — Schools, Roads and Country Services

  • Schools. The public pathway is the same across the mainland districts: North Gower/Marlborough Public School (kindergarten to Grade 5), then Kars on the Rideau Public School (Grades 6–8, with French immersion streams), then South Carleton High School in Richmond (Grades 9–12). On the Catholic board the districts split: the northwest and southwest send younger students to St. Philip in Richmond and on to Sacred Heart in Stittsville, while the Kars corridor goes to St. Leonard and St. Mark in Manotick. French-language pathways exist on both French boards — verify your exact address on each board's school locator, since the French Catholic locator could not confirm designations for these rural districts and I will not guess them. Burritts Rapids families on the Ottawa bank follow the same North Gower pathway; the island and south bank belong to North Grenville and its boards.

  • Roads and the commute. Roger Stevens Drive runs the township east-west, Highway 16 (Prince of Wales Drive) runs north to Manotick and the city, and Highway 416 at the west side is the fast route downtown. Nothing in the township is more than a few concession roads from one of those three.

  • Services. Kars and North Gower carry the daily basics — stores, arenas, halls, the Kars Recreation Centre and the North Gower farmers' market tradition — with Manotick's full main street and Kemptville's larger retail both inside the map's 15 km ring.

  • Country systems. Almost every property here runs on a private well and septic system, and heating is propane, oil, wood or electric rather than city gas in most of the township. That is normal country living — and it makes the inspection homework below a real part of every purchase.

  • Recreation. The Rideau River and Canal for boating and fishing, Baxter Conservation Area on the Kars shore, the Marlborough Forest trails in the west, and village rinks, halls and ball fields in between — the township's recreation is outdoors and water-based.

Well, Septic and Zoning — The Country Homework

  • Water. Ask for the well record and a potability test, and run the taps long enough to judge recovery — a drilled well and a dug well are different purchases.

  • Septic. Get the septic permit and installation records where they exist, budget for a septic inspection, and confirm the bed's location before you plan an addition, a pool or a garage anywhere near it.

  • Zoning and severance. Rural zoning, agricultural zoning and conservation-authority regulation (the Rideau Valley and Mississippi Valley authorities both touch this township) decide what can be built, severed or added. Verify against the City's zoning map and the authority's regulation mapping for the exact lot — never from a listing's shorthand.

Rideau Township Questions Buyers Ask

Is Rideau Township still a real place, or just a name?
Both — it was a real township municipality from the 1975 merger of North Gower and Marlborough townships until Ottawa's 2001 amalgamation, and it survives as the name for this whole rural southwest of the city: its roads, its community associations, Rideau Township Public School's successor schools and the four real-estate districts this guide is built on. Addresses here are Ottawa addresses; the identity is the township's.

How much do homes cost in Rideau Township?
Read it live rather than from any printed number: each district section above carries a snapshot of the active asking prices — count, average, median, lowest and highest — refreshed daily, and the market read above them walks through what the combined shelf looks like today. Country shelves are thin, so a district median can move with a single listing; the live snapshot is always the honest answer.

Which part of Rideau Township has the most land for sale?
The west district — the Malakoff and Marlborough Forest country — carries the deepest land shelf in the township, and each district's land snapshot above shows its own active lots with asking prices. Land here ranges from single building lots to farm parcels in the hundreds of acres, and it is priced lot by lot on frontage, soil, servicing and zoning.

Which schools serve Rideau Township?
For the public board, one pathway covers the mainland districts: North Gower/Marlborough Public School to Kars on the Rideau Public School to South Carleton High School. Catholic and French pathways split by district — the Kars corridor feeds Manotick's Catholic schools while the west and northwest feed Richmond and Stittsville — and Burritts Rapids' south bank belongs to North Grenville. School assignment is always the board's decision for an exact address, so confirm on the board's locator before you buy.

Is Burritts Rapids in Ottawa?
Half of it. The village straddles the Rideau River: the north bank is in the City of Ottawa and the south bank and island are in North Grenville. The distinction matters for taxes, services and school boards, and it is one of the first things to pin down on any Burritts Rapids listing — this guide's district covers the Ottawa-side search area.

What should I check before buying a country property here?
The well (record, potability, recovery), the septic system (age, permits, bed location), the zoning and any conservation-authority regulation on the lot, and the heating fuel — most of the township has no natural gas. None of it is a reason to avoid country property; all of it belongs in the offer conditions and the inspection plan.

Can I build on vacant land in Rideau Township?
Often yes, within the rural and agricultural zoning rules — building lots and farm parcels sell here precisely for that — but frontage, setbacks, well and septic placement, and conservation-authority permits all have to line up on the specific lot. Verify the exact parcel on the City's zoning map and with the conservation authority before you buy, and be careful with any lot being sold on the promise of a future severance.

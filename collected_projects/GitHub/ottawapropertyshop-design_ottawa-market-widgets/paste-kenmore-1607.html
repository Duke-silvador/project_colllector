Living in Kenmore & East Osgoode

Hamlet life in Kenmore runs on its institutions, not its shops. The Kenmore Community Centre and Hall and Bicentennial Park are the gathering places; the Kenmore Community Association and the Women’s Institute keep the calendar; each November the Legion’s Remembrance service is held at the Kenmore cenotaph. A Short History of Kenmore — the Township Story, Told from Kenmore

The shared story first, because Kenmore’s only makes sense inside it. Osgoode Township was established in 1798 and named for William Osgoode, the first Chief Justice of Upper Canada. Settlement began around 1827 — and who arrived first is a genuinely contested piece of local history: the McDonell family’s claim and the Archibald York family’s claim both survive in the record, and honest local history reports both rather than picking a winner. The township’s first crossroads, Baker’s Corners, is today’s Metcalfe. The township was incorporated in 1850, and on January 1, 2001 it amalgamated into the City of Ottawa.

Kenmore’s own story starts with the Rideau Canal. The town-hall site was settled in 1830 by John McDonald, a former canal construction worker; the village itself was first settled in 1840 and laid out by Peter McLaren. A post office opened in 1853, and in 1857 Squire Peter McLaren chose the name “Kenmore” — over “Glen Lyon” — after his home in Perthshire, Scotland. On the Castor River, the Carkner Mill rose in 1857 and was enlarged in 1883 to manufacture cheese boxes, employing thirty people at its peak, with logs driven down the Castor to feed it. By 1866 Kenmore was a post village of about eighty people with three stores, flouring and saw mills, a pearl-ash factory, a tannery and a frame Baptist church raised in 1851.

The coda is the one every Kenmore telling earns: in 1901, former resident John McMaster carried the name west, and Kenmore, Washington was registered — named after this hamlet. The mills are gone now, and the village is quieter than its 1866 directory entry, but the shape of the place — hall, park, cenotaph, two clusters of homes in the farmland — is the shape the mill era left behind.

Homes & Housing Stock in Kenmore & East Osgoode

The hamlet holds older detached homes in two small residential clusters along the village roads — the housing of a mill village that stopped growing early and kept its character. The district around it carries the second East Osgoode: bungalows and two-storey homes on large rural lots along Yorks Corners, Marionville, Gregoire and Marvelville Roads, with working farms and hobby farms of ten acres and more beyond them. Wells & Septic — the Kenmore Homework

Kenmore and East Osgoode are not on municipal water or sewer, and neither is anywhere else in the township’s villages. Every purchase here should include:

  • A potability and bacteria water test on the well, plus a flow/recovery check — hamlet wells and farm wells behave very differently.

  • A septic inspection with the tank pumped and the bed assessed; ask for the system’s age, size and any permits on file. A replacement bed is one of the largest cheques a rural buyer can inherit.

  • Heating and services history — oil, propane and electric heat are all common out here, and each carries a different monthly reality.

  • For farm and acreage properties, a hard look at zoning, road frontage, drainage and any tile or easement records before conditions come off — the land is half the purchase.

None of this is a reason to avoid Kenmore — half the Ottawa valley lives happily on well and septic. It is a reason to buy with your eyes open, and it is standard practice in every offer I write out here.

Schools Serving Kenmore

First, the honest headline: there is no school in Kenmore. Children are bused, overwhelmingly to Metcalfe, a few minutes away. Here is the map as it stands:

  • Metcalfe Public School (OCDSB, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8) in Metcalfe — the east side’s public elementary school, and the default for Kenmore and East Osgoode addresses.

  • Castor Valley Elementary School (OCDSB) in Greely — the Early French Immersion centre for Grades 7 and 8 for the wider Osgoode area.

  • Osgoode Township High School (OCDSB, Grades 9 to 12) in Metcalfe — the township’s only public high school; its own published list of communities served explicitly includes Kenmore.

  • St. Catherine School (OCSB, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6) in Metcalfe — the Catholic elementary serving the Metcalfe side and Kenmore-area families.

  • St. Mark Catholic High School (OCSB, Grades 7 to 12) in Manotick — the Catholic secondary destination for this side of the township.

  • French-language boards (CECCE and CEPEO): catchments here are address-specific — run your exact address through each board’s school locator rather than relying on any summary, including this one.

School buses are run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority (OSTA) for the English boards and the Consortium de transport scolaire d’Ottawa for the French boards. In a hamlet where every school is a bus ride, the pickup time is part of the house — ask about it.

Community Life in Kenmore

Kenmore’s fabric is association-deep and storefront-thin, and residents will tell you that is the point. The Community Association and the Women’s Institute run the hamlet’s social life out of the Community Centre and Hall; Bicentennial Park is its green; the cenotaph anchors its November. For everything else, Kenmore plugs into the township’s shared furniture: the Metcalfe Fair (running since 1856) a few minutes away, the township museum in Vernon, Pioneer Day, and the churches of Metcalfe and Osgoode. Small place, long memory, full calendar for its size.

Getting Around & Who Runs the Hamlet

Yorks Corners Road is the hamlet’s spine, running north to Mitch Owens Road and the Greely side, and the village sits just northeast of Metcalfe — 8th Line Road is the direct tie to the high school and the fairgrounds. East, Marionville Road runs to Marionville and on toward Russell; Boundary Road and its Highway 417 interchange serve the district’s far eastern edge. Bank Street, the township’s main spine, is reached through Metcalfe. There is no regular OC Transpo service — Kenmore is a driving hamlet, full stop. Municipal services are the City of Ottawa’s: Ward 20 (Osgoode) is Kenmore’s seat at council, and rural services like waste collection, snow clearing and road maintenance run on the city’s rural schedules.

Questions Buyers Ask About Kenmore

Is Kenmore part of Ottawa?
Yes. Kenmore is a hamlet inside the City of Ottawa, in Ward 20 (Osgoode), in the old Osgoode Township in the city’s rural southeast. The township amalgamated into Ottawa on January 1, 2001. Is this Kenmore, Washington?
No — though the two are connected: Kenmore, Washington was named after this Kenmore, Ontario by a former resident, John McMaster, in 1901. This guide is about the original — the Ottawa hamlet on the Castor River, a few minutes northeast of Metcalfe.

Does Kenmore have municipal water and sewer?
No. Plan on a private well and a septic system — the norm across the whole township, in the hamlet and on the farms alike. Budget for a water test, a septic inspection, and the ongoing care both systems need, and verify the specifics lot by lot with the City and your inspector.

Which schools serve Kenmore?
There is no school in the hamlet. Most Kenmore children ride to Metcalfe Public School (OCDSB, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8), with Early French Immersion in Grades 7 and 8 at Castor Valley Elementary in Greely, and Osgoode Township High School in Metcalfe for Grades 9 to 12 — the high school’s own published list of communities served explicitly includes Kenmore. Catholic families: St. Catherine School in Metcalfe (OCSB, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6) serves the east side, then St. Mark Catholic High School in Manotick for Grades 7 to 12. Always confirm the schools assigned to an exact address in the board’s locator before you buy.

How far is Kenmore from downtown Ottawa?
About 23 kilometres southeast of the city centre as the road signs count it; by car, plan on roughly 35 to 40 minutes in normal conditions — an estimate, and winter has opinions. The neighbours are close: Metcalfe is only a few minutes southwest, Greely roughly 12 to 15 minutes, and the Highway 417 interchange at Boundary Road serves the district’s eastern edge.

What is actually in Kenmore?
A community hall, Bicentennial Park, an active community association and Women’s Institute, the cenotaph where the Legion’s Remembrance service is held each November, and a small Canada Post postal station — the hamlet’s only commercial presence. There is no church standing in the hamlet core today; residents use the Metcalfe and Osgoode churches a few minutes away. Daily shopping is Metcalfe, Greely or Russell.

What are the lots like in Kenmore and East Osgoode?
Two kinds. In the hamlet, older detached homes sit in two small residential clusters along the village roads. Across the wider district — Yorks Corners Road, Marionville Road, Gregoire Road, Marvelville Road, the south end of 8th Line Road — bungalows and two-storeys sit on large rural lots, with working farms and hobby farms of ten acres and more. There are no condos in district 1607; everything is freehold.

Why are there so few listings in Kenmore?
Because there is so little Kenmore: about 500 people in the hamlet, in a district of farms that change hands rarely. Seven sales in the last 360 days is this district’s true pace. Buyers here do not shop a market — they wait for a specific road, a specific farm, a specific house, and act when it appears.

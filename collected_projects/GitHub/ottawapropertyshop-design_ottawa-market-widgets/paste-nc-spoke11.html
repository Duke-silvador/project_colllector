The Short Answer

An assignment sale is the sale of a purchase agreement, not of a finished home. The original buyer — the assignor — transfers their agreement of purchase and sale to a new buyer — the assignee — who steps into the contract and closes with the builder when the home is ready. Three gates stand between the idea and the deal. First, the agreement itself: most builder contracts restrict assignment, many prohibit it without the builder's written consent, and the consent terms — including any fee — are whatever the signed agreement says. Second, the money: the assignee reimburses the deposits the assignor has already paid and takes over the remaining deposit schedule, and since May 7, 2022, the assignor's profit on the assignment carries HST. Third, the closing: the assignee faces the full new-home closing — the same statement itemized in the closing costs spoke — and pays the land transfer tax at final closing. An assignment done inside those gates is a clean transfer. An assignment attempted outside them can collapse at the builder's front desk.

What an Assignment Sale Actually Is

Start with what actually changes hands. In a new construction assignment, the assignor does not own a home; they own a contract: the right to buy a specific home, at a specific price, on a specific closing date, under the builder's agreement. The assignment sells that right. The assignee pays the assignor for the position, steps into the agreement in the assignor's place, and from that moment the builder's customer is the assignee: the remaining deposits, the design studio selections already made, the closing date and the price in the original agreement all carry on, now owed by and to a different person.

Why do contracts change hands? On the assignor's side, life moves faster than construction: a job relocation, a family change, a financing picture that looked different when the deposit was written, or a buyer who purchased as an investment and always intended the contract — not the home — to be the asset. On the assignee's side, the appeal is the mirror image: a home in a community whose release has already closed, a closing date that is now close instead of years away, or a price set in an earlier market. Both sides are trading the same thing — time and position — and both sides inherit the same document, which is why the document is where every assignment begins.

The Builder's Consent Gate

The first gate is not the market; it is the builder. Most builder agreements of purchase and sale restrict assignment: the standard position in Ontario builder contracts is that the buyer may not assign the agreement — or may assign it only with the builder's prior written consent, on the builder's conditions. Those conditions live in the agreement you signed, and they govern everything that follows. Many agreements attach a consent or administration fee to the process; the amount is not set by any market custom or any rule of thumb — it is set in the original agreement, and the only honest answer to the question of what consent will cost is: read the clause. Some agreements go further and restrict how an assignment may be marketed, prohibiting public advertising of the contract altogether.

Two consequences follow. First, an assignment negotiated before consent is read is a negotiation built on sand: the assignor may not have the right to sell what a buyer is trying to buy. Second, consent is a document, not a conversation — a sales office's verbal comfort is not the builder's written consent, and the transfer is not safe until the consent and the builder's assignment paperwork are executed. Read the assignment clause the way it will be enforced: before any price is agreed, not after.

The Tax Nobody Budgets For: HST on the Profit

The second gate is the one that ends the most assignment plans, because it is the one sellers forget exists. For years, assignment sales were treated in some quarters as a tax-free side door — buy the contract early, sell the position, keep the uplift. That door closed on May 7, 2022, when the federal rules changed: since that date, every assignment sale of new housing is subject to GST/HST. The tax is charged by the assignor — the seller of the contract — on the assignment consideration: in practical terms, on the profit, the amount the assignee pays above the original purchase price. The deposits the assignee reimburses are the assignor's own money coming back; the uplift above the original price is the taxable part, and the precise treatment of each amount follows the Canada Revenue Agency's rules for assignment sales.

What that means at the negotiating table is blunt: the headline assignment price is not the assignor's net. HST comes off the profit, and an assignment priced without the tax in the arithmetic is priced wrong. It also means the paperwork matters: the assignor is the one who charges and accounts for the tax on the assignment, which is one reason — there are several — that an assignment is a lawyer-and-accountant transaction, not a handshake with a deposit. Price the tax first, then negotiate the number.

Rebates at Risk

The third rail of the tax story is quieter, and it runs in both directions. The new-home rebates that return part of the HST on a purchase — the long-standing new housing rebate, the Ontario full-rebate window for agreements signed between April 1, 2026 and March 31, 2027, and the first-time-buyer pair that runs on its own track to 2030 — are built around a person who buys a home to live in. An assignor who sells the contract and never occupies the home may find their own rebate position gone: eligibility follows the person and the facts, and a buyer who never becomes the home's occupant is not the buyer the rebates describe.

The assignee's position is their own, and it is assessed on their own facts. An assignee who closes with the builder and moves in as their primary place of residence claims whatever their own status supports — first-time-buyer tests, residency and occupancy conditions included — on the same rules as any other new-home buyer. What an assignee cannot do is inherit the assignor's rebate expectations along with the contract. Two people, two tax positions, one home: settle both sides' rebate arithmetic before the assignment is priced, because a rebate one side assumed and the other side cannot claim becomes a dispute with a closing date attached.

The Deposit Handover

Underneath every assignment sits a deposit ledger. By the time a contract is assigned, the assignor has usually paid part of the deposit schedule decoded in New Construction Deposit Structures Ottawa Decoded — a signing deposit, staged installments, sometimes design studio deposits on top. The standard structure of the handover is simple: the assignee reimburses the assignor for the deposits paid to date, as part of the assignment price, and then steps into the schedule — every remaining installment, on its original dates, is now the assignee's to pay. The money the assignor already has with the builder stays with the builder; it is the right to its credit at closing that changes hands.

Simple structure, exact bookkeeping. The ledger has to be confirmed with the builder — what was paid, what was credited, what remains — because the assignment documents will recite those figures and both sides will rely on them. For condominiums, part of the deposit money sits in trust under the protection rules covered in Pre Construction Condos Ottawa Tarion Deposit Protection, which is one more reason the builder's own statement of the account, not the assignor's recollection of it, is the document the deal is written on.

Closing Day for the Assignee

When the home is finished, the assignee closes with the builder exactly as the original buyer would have — which means the closing is a full new-home closing, with every line that status carries: the balance of the original price, HST on the purchase subject to the assignee's own rebates, development levies and the builder's adjustments under the original agreement, and the enrollment and connection charges itemized in New Construction Closing Costs Ontario Itemized. The assignee also inherits the original agreement's design studio selections — the upgrades chosen at the appointment covered in Design Studio Upgrades Ottawa What Is Worth It are part of the contract being bought, priced into it, and not a menu that reopens.

One line deserves to be said plainly, because it is the one most often mis-stated at kitchen tables: the land transfer tax on the purchase is paid once, by the assignee, at final closing with the builder — the assignment itself is the transfer of a contract, and the tax event on the home lands where the home is transferred. And the home's warranty position is undisturbed by the change of buyer: Tarion coverage follows the home, the enrollment stands, and the clocks and coverage explained in Tarion Warranty Ottawa New Homes 1 2 7 Explained run for the assignee who closes. The contract changed hands; the home's protections did not change at all.

The Dates Move With the Contract

A new construction closing date is a target with a framework around it — occupancy dates for condominiums, firm and tentative dates for freehold, and a delay regime behind all of them. An assignment transfers the contract as it stands on the day it is signed: the assignee takes the current closing or occupancy date, the current state of any delay notices, and the remaining runway to closing, whatever those are. An assignment priced against a closing the parties imagine can meet a builder's notice that moves the date — and the notice binds the person holding the contract when it arrives.

So the dates are a diligence item, not a detail: before signing, establish the current firm or occupancy date from the builder's own notices, confirm whether delay notices have already been issued, and understand what compensation framework Tarion Warranty Ottawa New Homes 1 2 7 Explained describes if dates move again. The assignee's financing, rate hold and moving plans all hang off that date; the assignor's clean exit depends on the transfer being complete before the next notice lands. Both sides are reading the same calendar — make sure it is the builder's calendar, not the one in the listing conversation.

Six Checks Before You Sign

  • Read the assignment clause first. Before any price is discussed, find the clause in the original agreement: is assignment permitted, restricted, or prohibited — and on what conditions? The clause is the deal's constitution.

  • Get the builder's consent in writing. A verbal comfort is not consent. The transfer proceeds on the builder's executed consent and assignment documents, with the consent fee — whatever the signed agreement sets — accounted for in the price.

  • Confirm the deposit ledger with the builder. What has been paid, what is credited, and what remains on the schedule decoded in New Construction Deposit Structures Ottawa Decoded — from the builder's statement of account, not from memory. The assignment documents will recite these figures.

  • Price the HST on the profit. Since May 7, 2022 the uplift on an assignment of new housing is taxable, and the assignor charges it. Net numbers only: an assignment priced on the gross figure is priced wrong.

  • Fix the dates and the delay status. Current occupancy or closing date, delay notices already issued, and the remaining timeline — confirmed from the builder's notices before either side plans around a date.

  • Put a lawyer on both sides. Consent paperwork, the deposit ledger, rebate positions and the tax treatment all sit in documents. Independent legal review on each side is not a formality; it is where the assignment's surprises are cheapest.

Questions Buyers Ask Me

What exactly changes hands in an assignment sale?
The agreement of purchase and sale — not the home, which has not been built or has not yet closed. The assignor transfers their contract position to the assignee: the right to buy that home, at the original price, on the original closing date, under the original builder agreement. The assignee steps into the contract and closes with the builder when the home is ready.

Do I need the builder's permission to assign my contract?
In most cases, yes. Most builder agreements restrict assignment or prohibit it without the builder's prior written consent, on conditions set in the agreement — which can include a consent or administration fee in the amount the signed agreement specifies. The clause in your own agreement is the only version of the answer that counts, and consent should be in the builder's executed documents before the transfer proceeds.

Can I advertise my assignment publicly?
Not always. Some builder agreements prohibit marketing the assignment publicly — no listings, no advertising — as a condition of the contract. Breaching a marketing restriction can put the agreement itself at risk. Check what your agreement says about marketing before the assignment is offered anywhere.

Is there HST on an assignment sale?
Yes. Since May 7, 2022, every assignment sale of new housing is subject to GST/HST. The assignor charges the tax on the assignment consideration — in practical terms, on the profit above the original purchase price; the deposits the assignee reimburses are treated under the Canada Revenue Agency's rules for assignment sales. An assignment is not a tax-free route around the HST that a new home carries.

Who pays the land transfer tax when a contract is assigned?
The assignee, once, at final closing with the builder. The assignment transfers a contract; the land transfer tax event is the transfer of the home itself, and it lands on the buyer who closes — as part of the full new-home closing the assignee completes with the builder.

What happens to the deposits I have already paid the builder?
They stay with the builder and are credited at closing — what changes is who gets the credit. The assignee reimburses the assignor for the deposits paid to date as part of the assignment price, and takes over every remaining installment on the original schedule. Confirm the exact ledger with the builder before the assignment documents recite it.

If I assign my contract, do I lose the new home rebates?
You may. The new housing rebate, Ontario's full-rebate window and the first-time-buyer pair are built for a buyer who purchases a home as their own residence. An assignor who never occupies the home may not meet the conditions those rebates require. Treat your rebate position as a question to settle with your lawyer or accountant before you assign — not a surprise to meet afterward.

Can the assignee claim the rebates instead?
The assignee claims on their own facts, not on yours. An assignee who closes and occupies the home as their primary place of residence is assessed under the same rebate rules as any new-home buyer — including the first-time-buyer tests where they apply. Nothing about the assignor's eligibility transfers with the contract.

Does an assignment affect the home's Tarion warranty?
No. Tarion coverage follows the home, not the buyer. The enrollment stands through the assignment, and the assignee who closes holds the warranty position — the coverage periods and deadlines run from the home's own milestones, exactly as they would have for the original buyer.

Do both sides need their own lawyer on an assignment?
Yes. Between the builder's consent documents, the deposit ledger, the HST on the profit and each side's rebate position, an assignment has more paper per dollar than most purchases. Independent legal advice on both sides is where errors are caught while they are still cheap — before signatures, not after.

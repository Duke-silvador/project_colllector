The Short Answer

On an $800,000 home signed inside the enhanced window, new and resale tie on tax — $812,475 each before the other closing costs — and the decision moves to the things that are not tax: the new build's extra closing charges, its staged deposits and its wait, against the resale home's condition and repair risk. Sign the same new home after March 31, 2027 as a repeat buyer and it is not a tie at all: $80,000 of net HST lands on the new build alone. A first-time buyer is the exception that survives the window — that buyer's own programs still return the full 13% on agreements signed to 2030. Everything below is those three sentences, proven line by line.

The Tax Line: HST Lives on One Side Only

Start with the rule everything else hangs on. A brand-new home in Ontario is a taxable supply: it carries 13% HST — a 5% federal part and an 8% provincial part. The sale of a used, owner-occupied resale home is generally exempt from GST/HST. That single difference is why pre-construction homes Ottawa listings can never be compared to resale listings on sticker price alone: the new home's price has a tax layer to unwind, and the resale home's does not.

For agreements signed between April 1, 2026 and March 31, 2027, two programs together unwind the whole layer for a home bought as a primary place of residence. The Ontario Enhanced New Housing Rebate (ENHR) returns 100% of the 8% provincial part, up to $80,000. The Ontario New Home Affordability Payment (ONHAP) returns the 5% federal part, up to $50,000, as a top-up — and it is reduced by any federal first-time buyer rebate you are entitled to, so the federal side is covered once, never twice. Together: up to $130,000 of relief, and the full 13% back on a home up to $1,000,000. Read the grid slowly, because it contradicts the sales-centre version of this story. In the window, the rebate does not make the new home cheaper than resale — it makes it level. The $104,000 comes back, but it was only ever there because the new home was taxed in the first place. What the window really buys a new-home buyer is the removal of a penalty, not a discount.

Above $1,000,000 the tie breaks. Take a $1,200,000 purchase: the new build's HST is $156,000, the relief is capped at $130,000, and $26,000 of net HST stays on the new side of the ledger — subtotals of $1,246,475 against $1,220,475 for resale, before the new build's other closing extras. One more shared line before we leave tax: Ontario land transfer tax is the same both ways — the province's own guidance confirms the HST on a new home is not included in the value the tax is calculated on — $12,475 at $800,000 and $20,475 at $1,200,000 either way, with a first-time buyer refund of up to $4,000 available on both purchases.

When the Window Closes: Three Signing Dates, Three Answers

The enhanced relief is a window, not a rule of nature. Agreements signed on or before March 31, 2027 can carry it; agreements signed after cannot. What replaces it is the older, smaller standard rebate pair — a federal new housing rebate of 36% of the federal part, capped at $6,300 and fully phased out by $450,000 of value, and the Ontario new housing rebate of 75% of the provincial part, capped at $24,000. At Ottawa prices, the realistic standard figure for a repeat buyer is that $24,000.

The first-time buyer row is the one to plan around. The federal first-time home buyers' GST rebate — in effect since March 12, 2026, for agreements on or after March 20, 2025 — returns 100% of the 5% federal part on a new home up to $1,000,000, up to $50,000, then phases out to $1,500,000. Ontario's matching first-time buyer rebate returns the 8% provincial part on the same shape. Those programs run on agreements to 2030, long after the enhanced window closes — Federal First Time Home Buyer GST Rebate New Home 50000 is the full file. So the calendar divides buyers cleanly: anyone signing in the window is covered; after it, only first-time buyers still are. Deposits and Money at Risk

Tax is where the two routes differ on paper. Deposits are where they differ in your bank account. A pre-construction deposit is not one cheque: it is a staged schedule of cheques, paid over the construction period, that builds for years before there is a home to move into — the schedules themselves, builder by builder, are decoded in New Construction Deposit Structures Ottawa Decoded. For all of that time, your money is committed to a project, a builder and a completion date you do not control.

What stands under it is protection with exact limits — the Tarion file this series verified line by line in Pre Construction Condos Ottawa Tarion Deposit Protection:

  • Condominiums: trust first, Tarion second. Condo deposits sit in trust under the Condominium Act, and Tarion backstops the return of your deposit up to $20,000 — including money put towards upgrades and extras — if the builder does not return it.

  • Freehold: Tarion's caps are the protection. A freehold deposit has no trust rule, so Tarion's coverage does the work: up to $60,000 on a home priced at $600,000 or less, and 10% of the purchase price to a maximum of $100,000 above that.

  • Resale: a short exposure on a home that exists. A resale deposit runs from agreement to a closing that is measured in weeks or a few months, on a property you have walked through and can have inspected. The money is at risk for a fraction of the time, against an asset that is already standing.

Neither structure is reckless and neither is free. The pre-construction buyer lends patience and deposit money to a build, in exchange for protection caps that have to be checked, not assumed. The resale buyer's risk is concentrated and brief. Know which shape of risk you are actually buying.

Closing Costs Beyond the Tax

Both routes pay land transfer tax and legal costs. Only one route pays the new-build layer. A pre-construction closing statement adds items a resale buyer never sees: development levies and charges, utility connection and meter fees, and the Tarion enrolment fee — a fee the builder must pay by law, which many purchase agreements then pass through to the buyer in the closing adjustments. There is no honest universal dollar figure for that layer: it is set project by project, and the only version that matters is the one in your agreement — capped, if you negotiated caps, and open-ended if you did not. That layer is itemized in full in New Construction Closing Costs Ontario Itemized; on this page it enters the comparison as what it is — a real, variable cost that sits entirely on the new-build side, on top of the tax subtotals above.

The resale side has its own smaller layer, and it is paid in a different currency: the inspection you commission, the status certificate on a resale condominium, and — the large one — whatever the home's age is about to ask of you. A furnace, a roof or a foundation does not send an invoice in advance. The new home's extras are knowable in advance, in writing, if the agreement is read properly. The older home's extras announce themselves later. Budget for the shape you choose.

Warranty, Timelines and Price Risk

The new home's strongest non-tax argument is the warranty. A new build carries Tarion's 1-2-7 warranty — one year on most defects in workmanship and materials, two years on the building's envelope and systems, seven years on major structural defects — and any coverage still running runs with the home to its next owner, which is why a young resale home is a genuine middle option. The full warranty file, with its forms and deadlines, is Tarion Warranty Ottawa New Homes 1 2 7 Explained. An older resale home carries no warranty at all: your protection is the inspection, the seller's disclosure, and the price you negotiated knowing both.

Timelines cut the other way. A resale purchase closes on a date both sides sign for, usually weeks or a few months out, into a home whose rooms, street and neighbours can all be inspected today. A pre-construction purchase is a promise about a home, a price and a neighbourhood phase that will be delivered years from now, on a schedule the builder controls within the delay rules of the agreement. And price risk runs both ways, honestly stated:

  • Pre-construction price risk: you commit at today's price for delivery years out. If values rise, you gain on paper before you own anything. If they fall, you still close at the signed price — and if the appraisal at completion comes in under it, the shortfall is yours to cover.

  • Resale price risk: the price is tested by the market and an appraisal now, not years from now — but you pay today's full market price, and an older home's renovation and repair costs begin where the warranty would have ended.

  • The shared risk nobody prices: on both routes, the largest single variable is the same — buying the wrong home, in the wrong place, with the wrong representation, is expensive in either column. The tax math is the easy part of this decision.

  • First-time status second. A qualifying first-time buyer's route to the full 13% runs to 2030 either way. If that is you, the calendar pressure is real but smaller — and your four-year ownership history is the fact to confirm before you rely on it.

  • Read the closing layer before the floor plan. Levies, hookups, enrolment and the caps on each: the new-build extras live in the agreement's schedules, and they are negotiable before signing and fixed after it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is HST charged on a resale home in Ottawa?

 

Generally, no. The sale of a used, owner-occupied home is generally exempt from GST/HST in Canada. A brand-new home is a taxable supply: 13% HST in Ontario — a 5% federal part and an 8% provincial part — before any rebate is applied.

 

How much HST is on an $800,000 pre-construction home, after the enhanced rebate?

 

Gross HST is $104,000 — 13% of $800,000. For an agreement signed between April 1, 2026 and March 31, 2027 for a primary place of residence, the enhanced relief returns all of it: the Ontario Enhanced New Housing Rebate covers the 8% provincial part ($64,000 at this price) and the Ontario New Home Affordability Payment covers the 5% federal part ($40,000). Net HST: $0.

 

Can I stack the $50,000 first-time buyer GST rebate on top of the $130,000 enhanced relief?

 

No — the programs combine, but they never pay the same dollar twice. The Ontario top-up is reduced by whatever federal first-time buyer rebate you are entitled to, so the federal 5% is covered once. Total relief can never exceed the HST actually paid: up to $130,000 on a home at $1,000,000.

 

What happens if I sign a pre-construction agreement after March 31, 2027?

 

The enhanced window closes for new agreements. A repeat buyer falls back to the standard rebates — at an $800,000 price, the Ontario new housing rebate of $24,000, for net HST of $80,000. A qualifying first-time buyer's programs run on agreements to 2030, so that buyer can still reach the full 13%.

 

Is land transfer tax higher on a new build?

 

No. Ontario land transfer tax uses the same graduated brackets both ways, and the HST on a new home is not included in the value of the consideration the tax is calculated on. On an $800,000 purchase the tax is $12,475 either way — and an eligible first-time buyer can claim a refund of up to $4,000 on either purchase.

 

What closing costs does a pre-construction home have that resale does not?

 

The new-build-only layer: development levies and charges, utility connection and meter fees, and the Tarion enrolment fee — the builder pays that fee by law, and many agreements pass it through in the closing adjustments. These amounts are project-specific and are set, capped or left open in your purchase agreement; there is no honest universal figure, which is why the agreement has to be read before signing. Both purchases pay land transfer tax and legal costs.

 

How is my deposit protected on a pre-construction purchase?

 

By layers. Condominium deposits sit in trust under the Condominium Act, with Tarion backstopping the return of up to $20,000 if the builder does not return them. Freehold deposits carry Tarion caps of up to $60,000 on a home priced at $600,000 or less, and 10% of the price to a maximum of $100,000 above that. A resale deposit, by contrast, is exposed for the short run to a closing date on a home that already exists.

 

Does a resale home have any warranty?

 

Only what is left of one. A new home carries the Tarion 1-2-7 warranty, and any remaining Tarion coverage runs with the home to the next owner — so a young resale home can still carry warranty years. An older resale home carries none: its condition is what your inspection and the seller's disclosure show, and its repair costs are yours from closing day.

 

Which is cheaper at $1,200,000?

 

On tax, resale — once the price passes $1,000,000. A $1,200,000 new build signed in the window pays $156,000 of HST and receives the capped $130,000 of relief: net HST of $26,000. Land transfer tax is $20,475 on both. Before any other closing costs, that is $1,246,475 for the new build against $1,220,475 for the resale home — before the new build's project-specific closing extras are added.

This week in On the Green

Homes for sale: 5 active listings. The shelf held at five — the same count my On the Green guide recorded on October 10. In a pocket this small, a flat count is its own kind of news: five homes is one of the thinnest shelves in Barrhaven, and a single family's move changes the whole picture.

Median asking price: $549,000. The middle of the On the Green set stepped down: against the guide's median of $579,000, that is a drop of $30,000 in just over three weeks — just over five per cent.

Average asking price: $569,380. The average eased $7,200 from the guide's $576,580 — a little over one per cent. The average barely moved while the median stepped down, which says the change sits in the middle of this small set rather than at its edges.

The range: $499,000 to $699,000. The two ends moved the other way from the middle: the entry point eased down $30,000 from $529,000, while the top of the range stepped up $79,000 from $620,000. The shelf stretched wider even as its middle softened.

The Barrhaven picture: Barrhaven-wide, 247 freehold homes are listed today, and On the Green's five make it one of the smallest pockets in the count. Its $549,000 median sits $76,000 below the Barrhaven-wide median of $625,000 — and that gap is a mix story, not a verdict on value: On the Green is 1980s detached homes and townhomes on established streets, while the Barrhaven-wide figures blend in every pocket and product type, the newest subdivisions at the suburb's south end included.

What I make of it: a count that holds at five for over three weeks while the ends stretch says this pocket turns over one family move at a time. What the aggregates cannot tell us is whether individual listings sold, expired or were withdrawn along the way. If you are buying in On the Green, the whole shelf is five homes, so the work is in the inspection — this is 1980s stock, and roof, foundation, furnace and windows matter even on renovated homes. If you are selling here, yours would be one of a handful of choices in a built-out pocket with no new subdivision coming behind it.

1980s crescents in the middle of everything

On the Green was built out in the 1980s, and it still reads that way: detached two-storeys and freehold townhomes, many of them renovated, on quiet crescents and courts — Bartley Crescent, Exeter Drive, Benlark Road, Wessex Road, Fulham Court, Atoll Street, Avery Crescent, Chester Crescent, Danaher Drive and Starling Crescent, with Tartan Drive running down the west side. There is no condo stock inside the pocket and essentially no new construction; change here comes from infill and renovation, not subdivisions. What sets the pocket apart is position: the middle of everything Barrhaven offers, without the premium of the river or golf-course pockets.

And no other Barrhaven pocket has a namesake like this one. The On the Green Driving Range and Mini Golf on Jockvale Road — a family-run range with turf mats and grass tees plus an 18-hole mini-golf course, still operating in 2026 — is the reason for the name, and the pocket's signature leisure draw: a bucket of balls after work is a neighbourhood ritual here.

Getting around from On the Green

By car, Greenbank Road and Strandherd Drive are the pocket's arterials, Highway 416 access sits minutes west, and the off-peak drive downtown runs about 25 to 30 minutes. The pocket's north edge is the rail corridor — and just across the tracks sits Fallowfield Station, the Transitway and VIA Rail station in the banner photo above, with its park and ride and the Toronto–Ottawa corridor trains.

On transit, one route does the local work, and it runs the pocket's own streets: route 70 travels Tartan Drive, Exeter Drive and Wessex Drive on its way between Fallowfield Station and Limebank Station on Line 2 of the O-Train, passing the Marketplace and Citigate corridors on the way. Most addresses in On the Green have the bus on their own street, not just at the boundary. For drivers who go partway, the Strandherd Park and Ride just south of the pocket holds more than 300 spaces.

Schools around On the Green

No school building sits inside the pocket itself — the bench that serves it rings it on every side, across all four boards:

  • Public English (OCDSB): Jockvale Elementary School (kindergarten to grade 6, English and Early French Immersion) just north of the tracks, then Cedarview Middle School (grades 7 and 8), then John McCrae Secondary School (grades 9 to 12) — the schools themselves publish this as the pathway for the pocket's streets.

  • Catholic English (OCSB): St. Luke School in Nepean is the area's Catholic elementary school. Catholic secondary splits by street between St. Joseph High School (grades 7 to 12) on Greenbank Road and St. Mother Teresa High School (grades 7 to 12).

  • French Catholic (CECCE): École élémentaire catholique Jean-Robert-Gauthier (kindergarten to grade 6), then École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard (grades 7 to 12) — both serve the board's Barrhaven sector, which covers this pocket.

  • French public (CEPEO): École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean (kindergarten to grade 8), then École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois (grades 7 to 12), which runs the International Baccalaureate program.

  • Private: Ottawa Christian School (junior kindergarten to grade 8) on Tartan Drive at the pocket's west side — and the Sunday meeting place of Sequoia Community Church.

Catchments turn on the exact street, so check yours on each board's school-locator tool before you buy — my On the Green guide walks through the bench in more depth.

Parks, play and daily life

The pocket's own green is Moloughney Park — also signed as Wessex Park — on Wessex Road: a ball diamond, soccer fields, a basketball court, play structures, the Charlie Conacher Community Building, and a West Barrhaven Community Association outdoor rink in winter. Just north across the tracks, Malvern Park adds about seven acres of playgrounds and trails, and Greenbank Park on the east edge carries the splash pad that is the nearest summer cool-down for local kids. The big indoor draw is a short hop north: the Walter Baker Sports Centre on Malvern Drive, with two ice rinks, a 25-metre lap pool with a dive tank and teaching pools, a rock wall, gyms and squash courts — plus the Ruth E. Dickinson branch of the Ottawa Public Library under the same roof.

Churches, plazas and the daily run

Faith life sits at the pocket's edges: Sequoia Community Church meets on Sundays at Ottawa Christian School on Tartan Drive, inside the pocket, while Barrhaven United Church and Barrhaven Fellowship Christian Reformed Church stand on Jockvale Road at the western edge. The West Barrhaven Community Association covers the pocket — it manages the Charlie Conacher Community Building and runs the Moloughney Park outdoor rink. Childcare is close too: Educara Montessori Child Care on Tartan Drive sits inside the pocket, and Guide and Grow Daycare runs on the Jockvale Road edge.

The daily run is a five-minute drive, not a drawback: the pocket itself is purely residential, and directly south across Strandherd Drive sits the outdoor mall with Walmart, Loblaws, Winners, Sport Chek and Staples. The Barrhaven Town Centre and Marketplace corridors are minutes farther for everything else, from groceries to the cinema.

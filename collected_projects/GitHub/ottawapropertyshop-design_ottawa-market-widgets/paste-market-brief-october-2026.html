This Ottawa real estate market update — the October 2026 edition of the Ottawa Real Estate Market Brief — tells a two-sided story. Against September, the month improved: home sales edged higher, new listings eased, and the pressure on Ottawa sellers relaxed a little. Against last October, the Ottawa housing market stayed softer — fewer sales, lower prices, and more inventory than a year ago. OREB President Tami Eades called October “some welcome relief for sellers,” while noting that buyers “continue to have considerable choice.” Both halves of that sentence are true, and the tables below — pulled from the Ottawa Real Estate Board’s own October release — show exactly where each half applies.

Summary of Findings

  • Sales: 1,048 homes sold, down 10.7% from last October — but up 3.8% from September, and only the third-lowest October total since 2016.

  • New listings: 2,382, down 1.2% year over year and 18.6% from September — yet still the third-highest October count since 2016, 26.1% above the ten-year October average of 1,889.

  • Market balance: the sales-to-new-listings ratio jumped to 44.0% from September’s 34.5%, and months of inventory eased to 4.4 from 4.8 — relief that came mostly from fewer new listings, not a surge of buyers.

  • Prices: average $678,512 (down 4.5%) and median $622,000 (down 3.9%) year over year; the composite benchmark price is $616,900, down 1.3% — and after seasonal adjustment the index fell only about 0.5%, against 1.5% in September, so the pace of price declines slowed.

  • Inventory: 4,582 active listings, up 6.4% year over year — the second-highest October level since 2016, behind only October 2016 itself.

  • Speed and value: homes sold for 97.5% of asking, unchanged from September, at a median of 25.5 days on market.

  • The pressure point is apartments: 7.4 months of inventory and a benchmark of $377,500, down 5.3% year over year — the softest segment in the city, as it has been all year.

  • The suburbs carried the month: 753 sales, 71.9% of all Ottawa sales, with the firmest conditions in Ottawa Suburb East at 3.5 months of inventory.

Recommendations to Ottawa Buyers

  • Apartments offer real negotiation room. At 7.4 months of inventory, this is the deepest buyer’s leverage in the city right now.

  • Do not expect the same leverage on a well-priced suburban freehold. Those homes are still selling in roughly 25 days, at 97.5% of asking — and October’s easing came from fewer listings, which means the good ones have less competition sitting beside them, not more.

  • Refresh your pre-approval. The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate to 2.5% on October 28 — borrowing costs have moved since many pre-approvals were written.

  • Read the federal budget closely. The budget lands November 5, and its housing and staffing details matter more in Ottawa than almost anywhere else in Ontario.

Recommendations to Ottawa Sellers

  • Price to the 97.5% reality. The average Ottawa home sold for 97.5% of its list price in October. An asking price set above the recent comparable sales mostly buys extra days on market, not extra money.

  • Your property type sets your market. A suburban single-family home at 4.0 months of inventory plays by different rules than an apartment at 7.4 — price and plan for the segment you are actually in.

  • Apartment sellers need the sharpest pricing and the most patience. With 7.4 months of inventory and sales down 23.3% year over year, buyers have choices — and they know it.

  • October’s relief is real but narrow. Sales rose from September mainly because fewer competing listings arrived. A well-priced home now meets a thinner field of rivals — that window is worth using before winter.

For everyone, the citywide headline is the least useful number in this report. Recent sales and competing listings in the same neighbourhood and the same property type are what set a realistic price, a realistic offer and a realistic timeline — and as the market heads into winter, those local differences are what will decide outcomes. The full breakdown follows.

Ottawa Market Stats — the Citywide Picture

The headline tension is between the two comparisons. Month over month, October steadied and slightly improved: sales rose 3.8% from September, the sales-to-new-listings ratio climbed from 34.5% to 44.0%, the sale-to-list ratio held at 97.5%, and homes sold a day and a half faster than in September. Year over year, the gap is the story — sales down 10.7% on dollar volume of $711,080,780, months of inventory up from 3.7 to 4.4, and both the average and median price lower after holding in a narrow range from July through September.

October did not change the market’s direction; it confirmed a market that is cooler than last year without being stalled — homes priced to the current numbers are still selling in under a month. It is also worth noting what powered the improvement: new listings fell 18.6% from September, a bigger drop than the 14.6% median decline across the previous ten September-to-October periods, each of which also fell. Some of October’s relief is the usual fall slowdown arriving a little stronger than usual. Where the coolness sits, though, is anything but even, as the breakdown by property type shows next.

Sales and Inventory by Property Type

Sales remained below last year’s levels across all three major property types. Single-family sales fell 12.5% to 558. Townhouse sales declined 3.0% to 350. Apartment sales decreased 23.3% to 115. The flow of new listings split the same way: single-family new listings totalled 1,202, up 1.3% from last year and the second-highest October count since 2016; townhouse new listings reached 740, up 1.9% and the highest October total of the decade; apartment new listings fell 10.9% to 367, close to the ten-year October average of 362 — yet apartments still hold the deepest inventory, because so few of them are selling.

Read against the benchmark prices, single-family homes remain the firmest of the three types: a benchmark of $699,200, down just 0.8% year over year, on 4.0 months of inventory. Townhouses sit in the middle — a benchmark of $539,800, down 4.3%, with the tightest supply of any type at 3.6 months. Apartments are the clearest pressure point in this Ottawa real estate market update — a trend seen throughout 2026: months of inventory reached 7.4, up from 5.8 a year ago, and the apartment benchmark of $377,500 is down 5.3% year over year and 0.9% from September. Across all types, the composite benchmark is $616,900, down 1.1% from September and 1.3% year over year.

Eleven Octobers — Ottawa in the Long View

The Ottawa Real Estate Board’s October release carries its three headline measures back through eleven Octobers, and the long view explains this month better than any single comparison. October sales peaked at 1,859 in the pandemic autumn of 2020 and troughed at 811 in 2023; this year’s 1,048 sits below every pre-2022 October and above both post-rate-shock years. Inventory tells the opposite journey — squeezed to just 1,440 active listings in October 2021, it has rebuilt every year since, and October 2026’s 4,582 is the second-highest October shelf since 2016.

The chart is the decade in one picture: a pandemic spike, a rate-shock collapse, and a slow, uneven climb since. October 2026 continues the climb in count while prices soften — a combination that only makes sense alongside the inventory rebuild shown in the full table.

Read the three columns together and the decade resolves into three eras. From 2016 to 2019, Octobers were ordinary: roughly 1,100 to 1,400 sales against a shrinking shelf. In 2020 and 2021, demand overwhelmed supply — months of inventory fell below one month, a condition no Ottawa seller’s market had ever sustained. Since 2022, supply has rebuilt faster than sales: active listings have climbed from 1,440 to 4,582 while October sales recovered only partway. That gap — not any single month’s headline — is the structural fact Ottawa buyers and sellers are now negotiating inside.

Regional Market Comparison — Where Ottawa Actually Moved

Ottawa’s suburban markets continued to drive activity in October, accounting for 753 sales, or 71.9% of citywide transactions. However, sales were below last October’s levels in all three suburban areas — and with most transactions concentrated in these markets, their slower year-over-year pace helps explain why Ottawa’s overall sales remained soft despite the modest improvement from September.

The suburbs generally maintained firmer conditions than the central and rural markets. Ottawa Suburb East had the lowest months of inventory among the seven submarkets at 3.5, with a sales-to-new-listings ratio of 51.0%; months of inventory stood at 3.7 in Ottawa Suburb West and 4.1 in Ottawa Suburb South. Conditions were weaker in Ottawa Centre and Ottawa Rural East, with 6.6 and 7.3 months of inventory respectively, and months of inventory in Rural East also increased from September — October’s citywide easing did not extend to every area. Ottawa Rural South and Ottawa Rural West recorded 4.1 and 4.4 months of inventory, but their smaller sales totals of 57 and 66 mean monthly changes there should be interpreted cautiously.

The Year So Far — 2026 Year to Date

Through the first ten months of 2026, 11,335 properties sold in Ottawa, down 7.3% from the same period in 2025, for total dollar volume of approximately $7.9 billion, down 8.0%. New listings reached 25,352 over the same period, up 3.4% — ten months of supply arriving faster than demand, which is the whole year in one sentence. The year-to-date average price is $695,598, down 0.8%, and the median is $635,000, down 1.6%; homes have sold for 98.1% of list this year at a median of 24 days on market. October fits its year: cooler, slower, and split by segment.

Looking Ahead — Jobs, the Budget and the Rate Hike

Employment remains a key concern for Ottawa heading into winter. An analysis by the Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives, reported by the Ottawa Citizen, showed roughly 15,000 fewer people employed locally since March 2025 — a larger loss than in any other large Ontario municipality over the same period — and pointed to federal public-service reductions as an important contributor. The employment picture varies depending on the comparison period: September’s seasonally adjusted employment estimate for the Ontario side of the Ottawa–Gatineau census metropolitan area remained above its year-earlier level, although employment declined from August. Earlier periods of federal workforce reductions were accompanied by weaker Ottawa housing sales and higher inventory; that history adds to concerns about further cuts, although it does not tell us how much of today’s housing market softness is directly related to employment.

The federal budget, scheduled for November 5, will therefore be particularly important for Ottawa. Further details on staffing may help households assess their employment outlook, while housing measures could affect their ability to buy. The Canadian Real Estate Association’s pre-budget submission called for changes to housing finance and mortgage insurance, including support for ownership of individual units in small multiplexes — whether the government adopts those proposals will be another area to watch.

Households are also weighing an uneven national economy and a recent increase in borrowing costs. Statistics Canada reported essentially unchanged real GDP in August and an advance estimate of a 0.1% decline in September, while the Bank of Canada raised its policy rate to 2.5% on October 28, citing persistent inflation pressures — the first policy rate increase in three years. Affordability measures may also be influencing where buyers look: GOHBA reported 521 new-home sales in September, up 81.5% year over year, with sales from January to September up 53.8%, and expanded, limited-time HST relief for qualifying new homes may be temporarily strengthening their appeal relative to resale properties. While these figures do not establish that buyers are shifting from resale to new construction, the contrast makes new-home activity important to understanding Ottawa’s broader housing demand. As OREB’s president put it, the Board will be watching closely to see whether October’s improvement carries through into the coming months.

Questions Buyers and Sellers Ask About the Ottawa Housing Market

How many homes sold in Ottawa in October 2026?
1,048 residential properties sold through the Ottawa Real Estate Board’s MLS® system in October 2026, down 10.7% from October 2025 but up 3.8% from September — the third-lowest October total since 2016. For the year to date, sales stand at 11,335, down 7.3%.

What are Ottawa home prices doing?
The average sale price in October 2026 was $678,512, down 4.5% year over year, and the median price was $622,000, down 3.9%. The MLS® HPI composite benchmark — the cleaner like-for-like measure — was $616,900, down 1.3% year over year, and its seasonally adjusted decline of about 0.5% was smaller than September’s 1.5%, suggesting the pace of price declines had slowed.

Is Ottawa a buyer’s market or a seller’s market right now?
At 4.4 months of inventory citywide, Ottawa leans in buyers’ favour, modestly — but the honest answer depends on the segment. Suburban freeholds, at 3.5 to 4.1 months of inventory, remain comparatively firm, while apartments at 7.4 months of inventory are in clear buyer’s-market conditions.

How long does it take to sell a home in Ottawa?
The median home sold in 25.5 days in October 2026 — faster than September’s 27 days and close to last October’s 25 days. Single-family homes were a touch quicker, at a median of 24 days.

Why are Ottawa apartments under so much pressure?
Apartment sales fell 23.3% year over year to 115, while listings kept coming — pushing months of inventory to 7.4, up from 5.8 a year ago. The apartment benchmark price of $377,500 is down 5.3% year over year, and apartments have been the market’s pressure point throughout 2026.

How much do Ottawa homes sell for compared with their asking price?
In October 2026, homes sold for an average of 97.5% of their listing price — unchanged from September and slightly below the 97.9% recorded a year earlier. Pricing close to the market is what the current numbers reward.

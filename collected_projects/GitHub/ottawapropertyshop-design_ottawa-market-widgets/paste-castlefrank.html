The Short Answer

Castlefrank Elementary School is an Ottawa-Carleton District School Board (OCDSB) elementary school at 55 McCurdy Drive in Kanata (K2L 4A9 · 613-592-8071), teaching Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3 — a small primary school of about 300 students, with a school day that runs 08:30–15:00. The board publishes its boundary as a single entry — the Castlefrank Elementary School JK to 3 English Program with Core French and Early French Immersion Attendance Boundary on its official elementary boundary index, updated January 17, 2025 — covering the English Program with Core French and Early French Immersion together, and answers for one exact address at a time through its School Locator, listed as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. The path from this catchment is one clear line: Castlefrank Elementary School (Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3) → Katimavik Elementary School (Grades 4 to 8) → Earl of March Secondary School (Grades 9 to 12).

One honest note on ratings, because families look for the number: Castlefrank does not appear in the Fraser Institute’s 2025 elementary report card, so this guide prints no Fraser rating for it. That absence is a gap in the report’s coverage, not a verdict on the school — the Institute’s rating is built from Grade 3 and Grade 6 EQAO indicators, and Castlefrank’s students have moved on to Katimavik before Grade 6, so no rating exists for a school that ends at Grade 3. Nearby W. Erskine Johnston, a Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6 school, rates 9.7 out of 10 in the same report; the number simply cannot be computed for Castlefrank’s grade span.

The Catchment, From the Board’s Own Line

A catchment — the board calls it an attendance boundary — is the area a school draws its students from, and for Castlefrank three things about the line matter to a buyer. First, the board decides, not the map: the school at the end of the street is not evidence, and proximity is a hint, not an answer — the locator result on the exact address is. Second, this school’s two tracks share one line: the board publishes Castlefrank’s English and Early French Immersion boundary as a single entry, so there is one line to check, not two. Third, lines move: boards review boundaries as neighbourhoods grow and enrolment shifts, which is why this series names the board source and its update date on every guide, and re-checks its catchments every September.

One feature of this corner of Kanata deserves a plain statement, because buyers mix it up: Castlefrank’s line and Katimavik’s line are cousins, not twins. The board publishes each school’s boundary separately; the two overlap heavily but are not identical, so this guide runs on Castlefrank’s own line and the Katimavik guide runs on Katimavik’s. One Catchment, Two Schools: Why Castlefrank Stops at Grade 3

Families comparing this side of Kanata always ask the same question: why does Castlefrank Elementary stop at Grade 3? It looks like a mistake. It is deliberate — one shared catchment split across two buildings by age, and the history is documented in the board’s own papers. Katimavik Elementary School, on Chimo Drive, opened in 1981 as a full Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8 school, and the growing neighbourhood overwhelmed it: by about 2010 it held more than 800 students, roughly 200 percent over capacity, with a dozen portables in the yard. Castlefrank Elementary School, on McCurdy Drive, was built in 1988 as the community grew — it officially opened on May 9, 1991 — and on September 7, 2004 it too became a full Kindergarten to Grade 8 school.

The split came out of the board’s Kanata South Accommodation Review: OCDSB Report 12-004 of January 31, 2012, and the Board’s decision of April 24, 2012. From September 2013, Castlefrank became the primary school (Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3, English and Early French Immersion) and Katimavik the junior and intermediate school (Grades 4 to 8), phased in so no child had to bounce between schools. The little kids get a small school of about 300 students; the older kids get the big building. Same streets, same neighbourhood, start to finish — and that is the feeder story in one line: Castlefrank’s children continue to Katimavik for Grades 4 to 8, and then on to Earl of March Secondary School for high school.

One program note belongs here, stated plainly because families ask about it at exactly this point: a Castlefrank student who continues in Middle French Immersion goes to Bridlewood Community Elementary School for that track — the school’s own site names it. The English Program and Early French Immersion tracks run from Castlefrank to Katimavik. Read alongside the Homes for Sale in the Katimavik Elementary School Catchment guide, this page is the first half of one continuous path; the W. Questions Families Ask First

Does buying a home inside the Castlefrank catchment guarantee my child a place at the school?
No. School assignment is always the school board’s decision, boundaries can change, and programs fill. A home inside the line is a strong starting point for a search — never a guarantee. Confirm the schools assigned to the exact address with the board’s school locator, and directly with any other school you are considering, before you firm up an offer.

How do I check which school a Kanata address belongs to?
Use the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board’s official School Locator, which the board lists as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. Enter the exact address — not just the street or the neighbourhood — because a boundary can run along lot lines and put one side of a street in a different catchment from the other.

Why does Castlefrank stop at Grade 3?
Because the board designed it that way. Castlefrank and Katimavik are one catchment split across two buildings by age: the Kanata South Accommodation Review (OCDSB Report 12-004, decided April 24, 2012, effective September 2013) made Castlefrank the primary school, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3, and Katimavik the Grades 4 to 8 school, to relieve a Katimavik that had passed 800 students at roughly 200 percent of capacity. The young children get a small school of about 300; the older children get the larger building — same neighbourhood throughout.

Where do Castlefrank students go after Grade 3?
Castlefrank Elementary School (Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3) → Katimavik Elementary School (Grades 4 to 8) → Earl of March Secondary School (Grades 9 to 12). That is the designated path for this catchment — the same path both schools’ own websites describe, and the one the board’s School Locator returns for addresses across the catchment.

What if my child continues in Middle French Immersion?
That track has its own destination: a Castlefrank student continuing in Middle French Immersion goes to Bridlewood Community Elementary School, as Castlefrank’s own site states. The English Program with Core French and the Early French Immersion program both run from Castlefrank to Katimavik. If your child’s program is a specific track, check that track’s entry with the board before you rely on the line.

Does Castlefrank have a Fraser Institute rating?
No — and it is worth understanding why. Castlefrank does not appear in the Fraser Institute’s 2025 elementary report card. The Institute’s rating is built from nine academic indicators drawn from Grade 3 and Grade 6 EQAO results, and Castlefrank’s students write their Grade 6 assessments at Katimavik, after they have moved on. The absence of a rating is a coverage gap in the report, not a judgement on the school — weigh the school on its programs, its size and your own visit, the way this guide weighs its boundary on the board’s documents.

If my child needs a bus, does living in the catchment mean they get one?
Not automatically. Transportation in Ottawa is run by OSTA, and eligibility is a distance test on top of the boundary: for Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6, a student’s address must be inside the designated attendance boundary and 1.6 km or more from the school. Closer than that is the walk zone. OSTA’s “Do I Qualify?” check answers for one address at a time, and the board’s transportation page sets the rules out.

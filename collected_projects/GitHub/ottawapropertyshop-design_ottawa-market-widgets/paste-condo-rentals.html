What A Condo Lease Includes — And Who Pays For What

In a condo rental, the landlord pays the monthly condo fee — your rent is your rent. What that fee buys the building also serves you as the tenant:

  • Building insurance on the structure and common elements — you insure your own contents and liability.
  • Exterior upkeep: roofs, windows in many corporations, hallways, elevators, snow clearing, landscaping and garbage.
  • Amenities where they exist: gym, pool, party room, guest suite — as a tenant you use them under the same rules as owners.
  • Water is included in most Barrhaven corporations; heat and hydro vary by building and by lease — this is a lease question, answered in writing before you sign, never assumed.

Parking and locker are the two line items to pin down: whether the unit's spot is included, where it sits (underground vs. surface), and whether a locker comes with the unit. A lease that says "parking included" without naming the spot is a lease to clarify before the deposit, not after the move.

How Renting A Condo Works In Ontario

Most leases start with a one-year term, then continue month-to-month. Ontario's Standard Form of Lease is mandatory for most tenancies, and the only legal deposit is first and last month's rent — "key money" is not permitted. Landlords typically ask for a credit check, proof of income or an employment letter, and references from previous landlords; have the package ready before you tour, because the good Barrhaven condo leases do not sit.

A condominium adds one layer a freehold lease never has: the corporation's declaration and rules bind the tenant too. Minimum lease terms, move-in/move-out elevator bookings, pet rules and amenity conditions are set by the corporation, and the landlord is required to give you the rules with the lease. We read them together before you commit — a rule that bans the dog or the second car is a rule to learn on paper, not at the loading dock.

The Split and the Cap, in Canadian Dollars

You pay 20% of your commission to eXp until the brokerage's share reaches $16,000 CAD — that happens at $80,000 GCI — and after that you keep 100% minus a small per-deal fee.

Most eXp fee content online is American, in US dollars, and does not apply to a Canadian agent. This page is Canada only, and every figure below is CAD, drawn from eXp Realty Canada's published structure.

Your split is 80/20: you keep 80 percent of every commission, eXp keeps 20 percent. That 20 percent is counted toward your cap. When the total eXp has kept reaches $16,000, you are capped. Because 20 percent of $80,000 is $16,000, the cap is reached at $80,000 in gross commission income. The arithmetic is simple enough to check on your own statements, which is the point — there is no separate cap fee and no second schedule hiding behind the first one.

eXp Realty operates with over 82,000 agents across 29 countries — in Q2 2026 the reported total was 87,338 agents including approximately 4,900 NextHome agents — but the fee schedule you pay as a Canadian agent is the Canadian one on this page, regardless of those global numbers.

After the Cap: The $285 and $75 Steps

After capping, you keep 100% of commission minus $285 per deal for your next 20 deals — $5,700 at most — then just $75 per deal for the rest of your cap year.

Capped does not mean fee-free, and any explanation that stops at "100% commission" is incomplete. Here is the full step-down:

  • Deals 1 to 20 after you cap: 100% commission minus a $285 per-transaction fee — if you close all 20, that is $5,700 in total

  • Every deal after those 20, for the rest of your cap year: 100% commission minus $75 per transaction

  • A $29 broker review fee applies per deal throughout the year, before and after the cap — it is listed separately below so nothing is double-counted or hidden

For a high-producing agent, this is where the Canadian eXp structure separates most clearly from an uncapped split: once you are past the cap and the 20-deal step, your marginal brokerage cost on an additional deal is $75 plus the $29 review fee. Compare that with 20 or 30 percent of a full commission, every deal, all year, and the difference is arithmetic, not opinion.

Does the cap reset every calendar year?

No. Your cap resets on your anniversary date — the anniversary of the date you joined eXp — not on January 1. If you joined on March 15, your cap year runs March 15 to March 14. This matters for timing a move, and it is covered in detail in the January Reset post linked from the Agent Attraction hub.

The Monthly and One-Time Fees

Budget $139 per month ($1,668 per year) in cloud brokerage fees and a $199 one-time startup fee — there is no desk fee, because there is no desk you are required to pay for.

The recurring fees are short enough to list completely:

  • $139 per month cloud brokerage fee — $1,668 over a full year

  • $199 one-time startup fee when you join

  • $29 per deal broker review fee, charged on each transaction

Any other Canadian charge not listed here — for example board, association or licensing fees — is not an eXp fee and verify it against your own board's current schedule before you budget it, because those costs follow you to any brokerage.

Are there royalty or franchise fees on top of this?

No separate royalty or franchise line appears in the Canadian fee structure set out on this page — the split and cap, the per-deal fees after cap, the monthly cloud fee, the startup fee and the broker review fee are the brokerage charges. That absence is one of the clearest differences from traditional franchise models, where a royalty percentage is commonly charged off the top in addition to the split.

What a Full Year Looks Like at Three Production Levels

The cap means your brokerage cost stops scaling with your income — the examples below use only the verified figures on this page, with the monthly fee counted for a full year.

At $80,000 GCI: you reach the cap exactly. Brokerage split cost: $16,000. Plus $1,668 in monthly fees and $29 per deal in review fees. Your keep before review fees: $64,000 of commission, minus $1,668.

At $150,000 GCI: the split cost is still $16,000 — the cap does not move. Deals closed after capping attract the $285 fee for the first 20 and $75 after that. On an uncapped 80/20 split, $150,000 GCI would cost $30,000 in split alone; on an uncapped 70/30, $45,000. The cap is worth more the more you produce, and worth nothing extra if you never reach $80,000 — both halves of that sentence are true, and your own production tells you which half applies to you.

Below the cap: if your GCI is $40,000, your split cost is $8,000 (20 percent), plus the same $1,668 monthly total. eXp's structure does not punish lower production, but the cap advantage only starts at $80,000 GCI, so an honest comparison at your level should be run on your real numbers — the Fee Audit Worksheet paired with the companion post on the hub does exactly that.

Amounts vary and are not guaranteed — see eXp's income disclosure. It is not a fee refund and should never be counted as one in your budgeting.

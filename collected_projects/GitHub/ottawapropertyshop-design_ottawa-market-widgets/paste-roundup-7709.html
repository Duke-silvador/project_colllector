This week in Strandherd

Homes for sale: 17 active listings. That is down from the 29 in my Strandherd guide's October 6 snapshot — twelve fewer homes in four weeks, in a pocket where the shelf normally turns over one family move at a time.

Median asking price: $449,500. The middle of the Strandherd set has reset: against the guide's median of $549,900, that is a drop of $100,400 in four weeks — just over eighteen per cent.

Average asking price: $532,999. The average is down $20,922 from the guide's $553,921 — just under four per cent — and the two measures have pulled apart. In the guide's snapshot the average and the median sat within a few thousand dollars of each other; today the average rides $83,499 above the median, carried by the detached homes at the top of the shelf while the shelf's weight sits in its lower half.

The range: $359,900 to $809,900. The shape of the change is in the two ends: the entry point did not move at all — $359,900 in the guide's snapshot and $359,900 today — while the ceiling came down $123,100 from $933,000.

The Barrhaven picture: Barrhaven-wide, 242 freehold homes are listed today, and Strandherd's 17 make it one of the smaller pockets in the count. Its $449,500 median sits $175,400 below the Barrhaven-wide median of $624,900 — and that gap is a mix story, not a verdict on value: Strandherd's shelf is weighted to townhomes and attached homes around its station and the Chapman Mills streets, while the Barrhaven-wide figures blend in every pocket and product type, the newest subdivisions at the suburb's south end included.

What I make of it: a median that falls $100,400 while the average eases only $20,922 says the shelf re-centred — half of everything listed in Strandherd now asks $449,500 or less, and the homes above the middle are the ones holding the average up. What the aggregates cannot tell us is whether the listings that left sold, expired or were withdrawn; twelve net departures also means new listings did join along the way. If you are buying in Strandherd, the choice sits in the townhome tier around the station, and these are homes from the late 1990s and 2000s, so budget for the inspection to matter — roof, foundation and mechanicals. If you are selling a detached home here, yours is the scarcer half of a small shelf.

Family streets, five schools and a landmark bridge

Strandherd was built mostly through the late 1990s and 2000s, and it still reads that way: Strandherd Meadows and the Chapman Mills streets by Richcraft Homes and Phoenix Homes — quiet crescents and courts like Garrity Crescent, Serena Way, Tacom Circle and Akita Walk branching off the collectors — plus Minto's condominium townhomes at the east end. One edge is still growing: The Conservancy by Caivan keeps building out along the Jock River at the pocket's southwest corner, new construction joining a pocket that was finished a generation ago.

Getting around from Strandherd

Road access is this pocket's edge: it is the closest of Barrhaven's northern pockets to Highway 416, whose interchange sits at its own west end, and the off-peak drive downtown runs about 25 to 30 minutes. At the east end, the Vimy Memorial Bridge carries Strandherd Drive over the Rideau River to Prince of Wales Drive and Riverside South — opened on July 12, 2014 as the Strandherd-Armstrong Bridge, renamed for Vimy Ridge that October, and an award-winning piece of engineering: the Gustav Lindenthal Medal in 2015, three vehicle lanes plus a dedicated bus lane and a bike lane in each direction.

On transit, the local routes work the pocket's own streets. Route 99 runs through the middle of it — along Chapman Mills Drive and Strandherd Drive between Barrhaven Centre Station and Limebank Station on Line 2 of the O-Train. Route 110 runs the Strandherd Drive corridor, and route 173 serves the west edge along Weybridge Drive, Fable Street and Kennevale Drive, between Barrhaven Centre and the Citigate business area.

The fast ride is the pocket's own: Strandherd Station, on the Southwest Transitway at Strandherd Drive and Greenbank Road, pairs the frequent route 75 — around the clock to Tunney's Pasture and the Line 1 connection downtown — with a park and ride of 336 spaces. Routes 70 and 80 stop there too — the 70 tying the pocket to Fallowfield and Limebank through the Marketplace and the Tartan corridor, the 80 running to Tunney's Pasture — plus peak-period Connexion routes 275 (from Half Moon Bay) and 279 (from Manotick), the 305 shopper service, and school routes 671, 672, 677 and 690 at bell times.

Schools around Strandherd

The school bench is the headline here — five schools sit inside the pocket itself, one of them a French secondary, which is rare on Ottawa's west side. The full bench serving Strandherd addresses, board by board:

  • Public English (OCDSB): Chapman Mills Public School (kindergarten to grade 6) is the pocket's own elementary school, dual-track with an English program and Early French Immersion, opened in 2012. Its graduates move to Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School (grades 7 to 12) — the board redirected the Chapman Mills pathway there, fully phased in by 2023. At the pocket's west side, Mary Honeywell Elementary School (kindergarten to grade 6) feeds Cedarview Middle School (grades 7 and 8) and then John McCrae Secondary School (grades 9 to 12).

  • Catholic English (OCSB): two elementary schools inside the pocket — St. Elizabeth Ann Seton School on Weybridge Drive and St. Emily Catholic School on Chapman Mills Drive. Catholic secondary splits by street between St. Mother Teresa High School (grades 7 to 12) and St. Joseph High School (grades 7 to 12).

  • French Catholic (CECCE): École élémentaire catholique Jean-Robert-Gauthier (kindergarten to grade 6) on Chapman Mills Drive, inside the pocket, then École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard (grades 7 to 12).

  • French public (CEPEO): École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois (grades 7 to 12) stands inside the pocket on Chapman Mills Drive and runs the International Baccalaureate program; French public elementary runs through École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean (kindergarten to grade 8) in Davidson Heights, just north of the pocket.

  • Private: Ottawa Christian School (junior kindergarten to grade 8) on Tartan Drive, just northwest of the pocket — and the Sunday meeting place of Sequoia Community Church.

Catchments turn on the exact street, so check yours on each board's school-locator tool before you buy — my Strandherd guide walks through the bench in more depth, board by board.

Parks, play and daily life

The green network runs on two rivers. At the northeast edge, just across the arterial, the Chapman Mills Conservation Area protects 23 acres on the Rideau River: a 1.5-kilometre boardwalk trail, a fishing platform, an accessible dock for canoes and kayaks, a covered picnic shelter and free parking — with Chapman Mills Park, the playground park, at the same edge. Inside the pocket, the neighbourhood parks carry the daily play: Water Dragon Park on Chapman Mills Drive, and Panda Park and Tierney Park in the Chapman Mills streets. Along the south, the Jock River is the pocket's green edge, with walking paths along the river streets and The Conservancy lands at the southwest adding a builder-stated 140 acres of greenspace with over 3 kilometres of river frontage. The big indoor draw is a short drive south: the Minto Recreation Complex—Barrhaven on Cambrian Road, with its 25-metre pool, two NHL-size rinks, gymnasium and fitness centre.

Churches, plazas and the daily run

Faith life gathers at the pocket's west end, on the Strandherd Drive corridor itself: Haven Baptist Church and the Nepean Seventh-day Adventist Church stand side by side there, with Harvest Mission Bible Church as their neighbour on the same stretch. Barrhaven Fellowship Christian Reformed Church serves the area from Jockvale Road at the west edge, and Sequoia Community Church meets on Sundays at Ottawa Christian School on Tartan Drive. Between them, most of the congregations a Strandherd family would look for are within a few minutes of the front door.

The daily run splits two ways. The big shop is at the pocket's northeast edge: Chapman Mills Marketplace, at Strandherd Drive and Woodroffe Avenue, packs 66 stores and services — Loblaws, the Walmart Supercentre, Home Depot, Shoppers Drug Mart, Winners and the Cineplex. The small shop stays closer: the plaza node at Strandherd Drive and Greenbank Road covers the grocery-and-services run at the west end, the Costco sits minutes farther west toward Highway 416, and childcare runs along the corridor, BrightPath Strandherd among the centres.

If you want Barrhaven's most established streets — mature trees, larger lots, and 1970s–80s Minto-built homes wrapped around the Walter Baker Sports Centre — start with the Pheasant Run Neighbourhood. This is one of the earliest pockets of Barrhaven to be developed (AMP area 7701), and it still feels like it: quiet crescents, renovated family homes, and a rare cluster where the sports centre, John McCrae Secondary School, and the Ruth E. Dickinson library branch all sit together at 100 Malvern Drive.

What sets Pheasant Run apart from newer Barrhaven subdivisions is simple: you trade brand-new construction for location and value. The homes here are older and often updated, the lots are generous, and daily life — school, swimming, skating, the library, the park — happens within walking distance. If you are browsing Ottawa houses for sale and want an affordable entry point close to real amenities rather than a far-flung new build, Pheasant Run deserves a serious look.

Schools & Catchments

Families move to Pheasant Run for the schools. Within about 3 km you will find public English schools (Barrhaven Public School JK–6, Jockvale Elementary JK–6, Mary Honeywell Elementary JK–6, Cedarview Middle School 5–8, John McCrae Secondary School 9–12, Longfields Davidson Heights School 7–12), Catholic English schools (St. Elizabeth Ann Seton Elementary JK–6, St. Patrick Elementary JK–6, Mother Teresa School 7–12), and École élémentaire catholique Pierre-Elliott-Trudeau (JK–6, French Catholic). Listings on Fable Street and Vanessa Terrace specifically advertise a walk to Jockvale Elementary and John McCrae High School. School catchments follow street-level boundaries, so before you buy, confirm your address in the OCDSB, OCSB, CECCE, and CEPEO school finders.

Commute & Transportation

OC Transpo routes 70 and 75 serve the streets around Pheasant Run Drive, with the nearest stop at Jockvale / Jennifer roughly a five-minute walk away; school routes 677, 696, 682, and 683 also pass nearby. For commuters, the Strandherd Park & Ride (3680 Strandherd Drive) is about 2.7 km away, with the Fallowfield Park & Ride as a second option, and the Fallowfield VIA Rail station connects Barrhaven to the Toronto–Ottawa corridor. By car, Greenbank Road and Fallowfield Road are the main arterials, and the drive to downtown Ottawa typically runs 25–30 minutes off-peak. Bus routing changes with OC Transpo's seasonal schedules, so check the current timetable when you plan your commute.

Parks, Trails & Recreation

Pheasant Run Park (18 Pheasant Run Drive) is the neighbourhood's green heart: playgrounds, walking trails, and picnic areas, with dogs welcome off-leash. For water access, Jock River Landing at 3691 Prince of Wales is a short drive away and also permits dogs. This is a pocket where kids bike to the park and neighbours walk the trails — the mature tree canopy is one of the first things buyers notice.

Walter Baker Sports Centre — The Neighbourhood Anchor

No other Barrhaven sub-area has anything quite like this. The Walter Baker Sports Centre at 100 Malvern Drive is a full City of Ottawa recreation complex: an 8-lane 25-metre lap pool, dive tank with 1m and 3m boards, warm teaching pool, preschool pool, hot tub, and steam room, plus two full-size NHL ice rinks that stay open through summer, an upper gym for cardio and weights, a lower free-weights gym, and four squash courts. It has been attached to John McCrae Secondary School since the school was built in 1999, and it houses the Ruth E. Dickinson branch of the Ottawa Public Library. Opened in 1980 as the South Nepean Centre and renamed in 1983 for MP Walter Baker, it is served by OC Transpo route 173. For a buyer, this is the single biggest lifestyle differentiator of Pheasant Run.

Entertainment & Leisure

Between the sports centre, the library, and the park, day-to-day recreation is covered inside the pocket. What you will not find inside Pheasant Run itself is a cinema, bowling alley, or arcade — the nearest options are in wider Barrhaven, a short drive away. Shopping, Groceries & Restaurants

Pheasant Run is a residential pocket, not a retail district: there is no large-format shopping inside its streets. That is a five-minute drive, not a drawback — Barrhaven Town Centre, Chapman Mills Marketplace, and Marketplace Avenue cover groceries, retail, restaurants, and services. Housing Stock & Price Context

Expect 1970s and 1980s construction, predominantly built by Minto: single-family detached homes, townhouses and row units, and low-rise condo communities on mature, tree-lined streets with larger lots — many homes have been renovated. Five named Minto condo complexes sit inside the pocket: 2–54 Sherway Drive (built 1974, 112 units, playground), 1–305 Thistledown Court (built 1978, 88 units, playground), 2–22 Sweetbriar Circle (built 1985, 128 units, daycare centre, playground, tennis courts), 1-3-5-7 Vanessa Terrace (built 1985, indoor pool, racquet courts, exercise centre, daycare centre, playground), and 41–43 Fable Street (built 1987, marketed on its walk to Jockvale Elementary, John McCrae High School, transit, and the Walter Baker Sports Centre). Price context, shown here only as aggregate bands: townhouse condos have recently listed in the low-to-mid $300s, while detached homes have listed in the mid-$500s. Pricing moves with the market — ask me for current comparable sales before you write an offer.

Services, Healthcare & Daycare

There is no hospital or major medical clinic inside Pheasant Run; residents use Barrhaven-area clinics, dental offices, and pharmacies a short drive away. The standout local feature is childcare: two condo complexes — Sweetbriar Circle and Vanessa Terrace — have daycare centres right on site, a genuinely rare amenity for young families.

Churches & Community

No place of worship has been confirmed inside Pheasant Run's boundaries; nearby options include Barrhaven United Church (3013 Jockvale Road) and the Salvation Army Barrhaven Church (102 Bill Leathem Drive). No Pheasant Run–specific community association was found — Barrhaven-wide representation applies. Both points will be re-verified against the final area boundary before this page publishes.

Safety & Street Character

Pheasant Run reads as a settled, family-oriented pocket: quiet crescents and courts, kids walking to Jockvale and John McCrae, and steady daytime activity around the sports centre and library. The housing stock's age means buyers should budget for the usual inspections — roof, windows, furnace, and foundation — on 1970s–80s homes, even renovated ones.

Future Development

Pheasant Run is essentially built out. Change here comes from infill and renovation, not new subdivisions — which is exactly why it appeals to buyers who want an established streetscape rather than a construction zone. (New-build Barrhaven is a different story, and it will be told on the Stonebridge and Half Moon Bay pages.)

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is Pheasant Run in Barrhaven?
Pheasant Run (AMP area 7701) sits in the established north-central part of Barrhaven, near Greenbank Road and Fallowfield Road, wrapped around the Walter Baker Sports Centre at 100 Malvern Drive.

What schools are near Pheasant Run, Ottawa?
Ten schools sit within about 3 km, including John McCrae Secondary School, Jockvale Elementary, Cedarview Middle School, and Catholic and French-Catholic options. Always confirm your exact catchment with the school board finders before buying.

How long is the commute from Pheasant Run to downtown Ottawa?
Typically 25–30 minutes by car off-peak via Greenbank and Fallowfield Roads. Transit riders use OC Transpo routes 70 and 75, with the Strandherd Park & Ride about 2.7 km away.

What is there to do near Pheasant Run?
The Walter Baker Sports Centre — pools, two NHL rinks, gyms, squash courts, and the Ruth E. Dickinson library — plus Pheasant Run Park's playgrounds, trails, and off-leash dog area.

What types of homes are in Pheasant Run?
1970s–80s Minto-built detached homes, townhouses, and five condo complexes, several with unusual amenities like an indoor pool (Vanessa Terrace) and on-site daycares (Sweetbriar Circle, Vanessa Terrace).

Is Pheasant Run a good place to buy a home?
For buyers who value mature streets, walkable amenities, and value over new construction, yes — it is one of Barrhaven's most established and affordable pockets. Get current comparable sales before offering; asking bands run from the low-to-mid $300s for townhouse condos to the mid-$500s for detached homes.

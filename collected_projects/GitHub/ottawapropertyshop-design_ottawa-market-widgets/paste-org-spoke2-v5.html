Who Owns PropTx — A Toronto Board’s Company Running Ottawa’s Listings

PropTx Innovations Inc. (proptx.ca) is a wholly owned subsidiary of TRREB — the Toronto Regional Real Estate Board is its only shareholder. TRREB created the company to hold its technology and data operations as a for-profit business, separate from the board’s not-for-profit association work.

That ownership surprises people, so let me be precise about what it does and does not mean. TRREB does not run Ottawa’s board, and it does not publish Ottawa’s statistics — that is still OREB, the Ottawa Real Estate Board (oreb.ca), with its more than 4,000 Ottawa-area members. What TRREB’s company runs is the platform: the database those members’ listings live in. Ottawa governance stayed in Ottawa; the technology moved to a shared, province-scale system built by the Toronto board’s subsidiary. PropTx now serves a growing list of Ontario boards on the same model.

The November 25, 2024 Move — What Changed For Ottawa

On November 25, 2024, OREB transitioned its members to the PropTx MLS® System, replacing the previous stand-alone Ottawa environment. For agents, the change was a new building behind familiar doors: the same work — entering listings, searching, setting up client alerts — now happens on the shared PropTx database, through front ends of choice including Matrix and REALM™, a mobile-first platform that combines MLS® data with Teranet’s GeoWarehouse parcel data — lot, assessment and sales information in the same view an agent uses to price your home.

For buyers and sellers, three things actually changed:

  • One database, most of Ontario. Your Ottawa agent and a Toronto agent can now be looking at the same underlying data for the same home. A client’s saved search, showing feedback and saved listings draw on a near-province-wide pool instead of a patchwork of separate board systems.

  • Less friction between boards. The old interboarding seams — listings delayed or reformatted as they crossed from one board’s system to another — matter less when neighbouring boards share one platform.

  • Newer tools on the same data. The PropTx system was introduced with AI-assisted search and enhanced data security, and integrated tools boards announce, such as forms and offer tools and neighbourhood-data products.

Matrix Is A Window — PropTx Is The Building

The most common confusion I hear: “Matrix is the MLS.” It is not. The MLS® System is the co-operative listing database and its rules — the shared record every member brokerage contributes to and draws from. Matrix and REALM™ are software front ends that display that record. When Ottawa moved to PropTx, Matrix did not vanish — it became one of the windows agents open onto the PropTx database. If the window changes, your listing does not move; the building underneath is what matters, and the building is PropTx.

Where The Data Goes Next — REALTOR.ca And Agent Websites

A listing entered once into PropTx travels. The board systems feed CREA’s national showcase, REALTOR.ca — where most Ottawa buyers actually browse — and the same data powers the listing searches and property pages on agent and brokerage websites, including the searches on this site. That is why the same home, with the same photos and details, appears everywhere at once: there is one source record, distributed. It is also why accuracy at the source matters so much — an error entered into the system is an error distributed everywhere, which is why board MLS® rules govern listing data so closely.

What Wider Exposure Does — And Does Not — Promise A Seller

Sellers hear “your listing is now visible across most of Ontario” and reasonably ask what that buys them. Honestly stated: it buys reach and consistency — a relocating buyer’s agent in Toronto sees your Ottawa home in the same system, with the same data, as a local agent does, and GTA-to-Ottawa moves are a real corridor for this city. It does not buy a price. Exposure puts your home in front of more qualified eyes; pricing, condition and presentation still decide what those eyes do next. No platform — PropTx included — sells a house. It makes sure the house can be found, completely and correctly, by everyone looking.

The one-sentence version: OREB is Ottawa’s board and publishes Ottawa’s numbers; PropTx — TRREB’s wholly owned data company — is the platform those numbers and listings now live on; Matrix and REALM™ are the windows agents open to work in it; REALTOR.ca is where buyers meet the same data.

Questions Buyers And Sellers Ask About What PropTx Is In Ottawa

What is PropTx, in one line? A for-profit data and technology company, wholly owned by the Toronto Regional Real Estate Board, that operates the shared MLS® system Ottawa’s agents have worked in since November 25, 2024.

Did Toronto take over Ottawa real estate? No. OREB remains Ottawa’s board — it sets local board rules for its members and publishes Ottawa’s official statistics. PropTx supplies the technology platform underneath, the way one company might supply the software many organizations run on.

Is Matrix gone? No. Matrix is one of the front ends — alongside REALM™ — that agents use to work in the PropTx database. The window stayed familiar; the building behind it changed.

Where do the listings on REALTOR.ca and agent websites come from? From the board MLS® systems — in Ottawa, that now means data entered into PropTx and distributed onward to REALTOR.ca and to agent and brokerage sites.

Does PropTx regulate agents or set commissions? Neither. PropTx regulates nobody.

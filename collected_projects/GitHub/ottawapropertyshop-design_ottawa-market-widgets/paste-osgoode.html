Osgoode at a Glance

Why do buyers choose a village 35 minutes south of downtown over a closer suburb? In Osgoode the answer is the combination: a real main street that still works — grocery store, pharmacy, LCBO, café, arena, library — with village lots in the core and rural acreages at the edges, all inside city limits. Greely, up Bank Street, grows faster and builds bigger; Metcalfe, east on Victoria Street, keeps the fair and the high school. Osgoode is the township’s service centre: the place the surrounding concessions drive to for hockey, groceries and the trail. Downtown is roughly a 35-minute drive, Greely about 15 minutes north, Manotick a short hop west across the Rideau.

Living in Osgoode

Daily life in Osgoode runs on Osgoode Main Street. The village core holds the Foodland grocery, the pharmacy, the LCBO, gas and the Red Dot Café; the Stuart Holmes Arena and Osgoode Community Centre anchor the west end of the street; the Ottawa Public Library’s Osgoode branch sits between them. Around the core, the village’s older streets carry its 19th- and early-20th-century housing stock — the frame and stone houses the railway era built — and beyond them the district opens into the concession country: River Road and Lorne Bridge Road toward the Rideau River, Nixon Drive and Stagecoach Road south, and working farms in every direction. Moving to Osgoode means choosing that mix on purpose: a village small enough to stay itself, with the services a village actually needs, inside a city big enough to need it.

A Short History — Osgoode Station to Ottawa Village

Osgoode’s story starts with the township itself. Osgoode Township was established in 1798 on lands acquired from the Mississaugas and named for William Osgoode, first Chief Justice of Upper Canada; its first settler families — the McDonells and the Yorks — arrived in 1827, drawn by farmland and the white pine and oak, and built the township’s first two roads, converging at Baker’s Corners, now Metcalfe. (The two families’ tombstones each claim their man was the township’s first settler; the 1879 atlas says they settled the same day, on adjacent concessions — this guide reports both claims and picks neither.) The township was incorporated in 1850 and governed itself for 150 years.

What made Osgoode village was the railway. The Ottawa–Prescott line crossed the township’s western concessions while bypassing stagecoach Metcalfe, and the village that grew at the crossing was known as Osgoode Station until 1962. It was incorporated as a Police Village in 1910, absorbed into the township in 1974, and the rail-era main street is why Osgoode ended up with the arena, the grocery store and the densest services of the township’s villages. The line itself is gone, but its bed survives as the Osgoode Link Pathway — 21 kilometres of paved trail from Buckles Street in the village north through Greely’s west side to Leitrim Road by the airport, officially opened in 2010. On January 1, 2001, Osgoode Township amalgamated into the City of Ottawa, and Osgoode Station’s village became what it is now: an Ottawa village with a railway memory. The shared township story — and the fair, museum and high school all five villages hold in common — is told in full in the Metcalfe guide and the Greely guide.

Homes & Housing Stock

Osgoode’s stock follows its shape. In the village core: older detached homes — including 19th- and early-20th-century stock from the railway decades — on compact lots of roughly half an acre. On the village fringe and the concessions: detached bungalows and two-storeys on lots from about a third of an acre to two acres and more, with hobby farms and working farms of ten acres and up beyond. There is essentially no condo stock here; this is detached-house country. Prices sit in the same broad band as the township’s other villages — below Greely’s estate-lot band at the top end, with the village’s older stock the value play for buyers who want a main-street address and are ready to do the well-and-septic homework that comes with it.

Wells & Septic — the Buyer-Critical Section

Every Osgoode purchase starts with one question: where do the water and the wastewater come from? Across the village and the district, the answer is a private well and a private septic system — wells and septic are the norm in old Osgoode Township, and city limits do not mean city pipes here. That is normal, workable, and non-negotiable homework. A well is a quantity-and-quality question — flow rate, potability, treatment equipment, records. A septic system is an age-and-capacity question — what was installed, when, and whether it suits the household you are bringing. A building lot is a feasibility question — what can be drilled, dug and approved on that specific land. Get the answers in writing, before closing, not after — and budget the inspections before you firm up, on every Osgoode purchase.

Schools — the Village School, the Township High School

Schools are the organising spine for most Osgoode families. The standing warning applies — catchments are set by your exact home address and program, so confirm any specific property in the board’s school locator before you buy.

  • Osgoode Public School (OCDSB, JK–Grade 6) — 5590 Osgoode Main Street, the village’s own elementary school, English and Early French Immersion, serving the village and the surrounding rural and riverfront lots.

  • Metcalfe Public School (OCDSB, JK–Grade 8) — in Metcalfe: where Osgoode’s English-program students continue for Grades 7 and 8.

  • Castor Valley Elementary School (OCDSB) — in Greely: the Early French Immersion centre for the wider Osgoode area in Grades 7 and 8, so immersion families bus north the way high-schoolers bus east.

  • Greely Elementary School (OCDSB) — serves the Greely-adjacent fringe at the district’s north edge; check your address in the locator.

  • Osgoode Township High School (OCDSB, Grades 9–12) — 2800 8th Line Road, Metcalfe. The shared public high school for the whole township — its own list of served communities reads Metcalfe, Greely, Osgoode, Carlsbad Springs, Vernon, Edwards, Kenmore and Manotick.

  • St. Catherine School (OCSB, JK–Grade 6) in Metcalfe and St. Mary School (OCSB, JK–Grade 6) in Gloucester — Catholic elementary for the area, split by address; confirm yours in the OCSB locator. Catholic secondary students attend St. Mark Catholic High School in Manotick for Grades 7–12.

  • French-language boards (CEPEO / CECCE) — no French-language school sits in Osgoode itself; confirm your exact address in the French boards’ locators, as rural French catchments follow their own sector maps.

Churches & Community Life

The faith map is a village map. St. Paul’s Anglican Church, 5462 Osgoode Main Street — the first church on the site was built in 1886 and the current building replaced it in 1924 — shares the street with St. James United Church, Trinity Bible Church and the Osgoode Bible Chapel. Community life runs through the Stuart Holmes Arena and Osgoode Community Centre at 5660 Osgoode Main Street, the Ottawa Public Library Osgoode branch at 5630 Main Street, the Osgoode Youth Association (O-YA), the Legion and the Lions — and out of town, the W.A. Taylor Conservation Area on the Rideau River and the Link Pathway trailhead carry the recreation.

One parish story belongs in this guide by name, because it is historically Osgoode’s own. A few minutes southeast of the village, at 2540 Stagecoach Road in the hamlet of Enniskerry, stands St. John the Evangelist Roman Catholic Church — the parish church of the township’s Irish Catholic farm families, founded in 1854 and served at first from Our Lady of the Visitation in South Gloucester. Fr. Thomas O’Boyle, who held St. John’s among his missions from 1855, became its first resident priest in 1860 — with St. Brigid’s in Manotick as St. John’s mission, a pairing that survives today, run the other way: the parish office now sits at St. Brigid’s, and St. John’s still celebrates Saturday vigil and Sunday Masses. In the 1890s and 1900s the settlement around the church was known as Dawson, and under Fr. McCauley the parish raised a Temperance Hall there. The church the Journal recorded was damaged by fire on an Armistice Day and solemnly reopened; in a second fire, it was Metcalfe’s Fr. Harris who carried the Host and the sacred vessels to safety through the flames. Fr. O’Boyle lies in the parish cemetery beside the church, among the families he served. The church itself sits in Enniskerry, in the township’s rural north — its full landmark treatment, with the photo, comes in the Osgoode Township hub guide in the set below.

Getting Around & Who Runs the Village

Osgoode is a car village, and this guide will not pretend otherwise. Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) is the spine — north through Metcalfe and Greely to downtown in roughly 35 minutes; Stagecoach Road (Ottawa Road 25) carries the drive south through Enniskerry toward Manotick Station; River Road and Lorne Bridge Road serve the Rideau riverfront. There is no regular OC Transpo route — school busing, through the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority, is the exception that matters to families. Governance is the City of Ottawa’s Ward 20, Osgoode. The old township identity persists where it counts — in the ward name, the high school’s name, the museum in Vernon and the fair in Metcalfe.

Questions Buyers Ask About Osgoode

Is Osgoode part of Ottawa?

Yes — Osgoode has been a village inside the City of Ottawa since January 1, 2001, when Osgoode Township amalgamated into the city. It sits in Ward 20, Osgoode, on Osgoode Main Street just west of Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) and about two kilometres east of the Rideau River. City limits, village life: that is the whole Osgoode proposition.

Why was Osgoode called Osgoode Station?

Because the railway made it. Osgoode grew where the Ottawa–Prescott railway crossed the western concessions, and the village carried the name Osgoode Station until 1962. It had been incorporated as a Police Village in 1910. The trains are gone, but the rail bed survives as the Osgoode Link Pathway — and the rail-era main street is why Osgoode, not the older stagecoach crossroads at Metcalfe, ended up with the arena, the grocery store and the densest services in the old township.

Does Osgoode have municipal water and sewer?

Plan on private services. Wells and septic systems are the norm across Osgoode and the whole of old Osgoode Township — village homes included — so every purchase carries the same homework: flow and potability on the well, age and capacity on the septic, and inspections budgeted before you firm up. Confirm the servicing of any specific property in writing before you buy.

Which schools serve Osgoode?

Osgoode Public School (OCDSB, JK to Grade 6, English and Early French Immersion) is in the village. For Grades 7 and 8, English-program students continue to Metcalfe Public School and immersion students to Castor Valley Elementary in Greely. Every public secondary student in the township rides to Osgoode Township High School in Metcalfe. Catholic elementary students are served by St. Catherine School in Metcalfe or St. Mary School in Gloucester depending on the address, and Catholic secondary students attend St. Mark Catholic High School in Manotick. Catchments follow your exact address and program — confirm any specific property in the board's school locator before you buy.

How far is Osgoode from downtown Ottawa?

About 35 minutes by car in free-flowing traffic, straight up Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) through Metcalfe and Greely. Greely is roughly 15 minutes north, Metcalfe about 10 minutes, and Manotick is a short drive west. These are road-time estimates — test your own commute at your own hour before you buy.

How big are the lots in Osgoode?

Village homes sit on compact lots of roughly half an acre in the core. Outside the village the pattern is rural Ottawa: detached homes on lots from about a third of an acre to two acres and more, hobby farms and working farms beyond, all inside city limits. That range — village lot to farm acreage under one postal code — is the Osgoode signature.

What is the Osgoode Link Pathway?

A 21-kilometre paved trail on the abandoned Ottawa–Prescott rail bed, running from Buckles Street in Osgoode village north through Greely's west side to Leitrim Road by the airport. It officially opened in 2010. It is the village's trailhead amenity — cycling, walking and riding straight from Main Street toward the city.

Is there public transit in Osgoode?

There is no regular OC Transpo route serving the village, and buyers should plan around that honestly: Osgoode is a car-dependent village. School busing runs separately, through the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority for eligible students.

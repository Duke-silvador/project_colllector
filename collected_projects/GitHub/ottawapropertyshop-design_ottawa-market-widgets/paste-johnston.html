The Catchment, From the Board’s Own Line

A catchment — the board calls it an attendance boundary — is the area a school draws its students from, and for Johnston three things about the line matter to a buyer. First, the board decides, not the map: the school at the end of the street is not evidence, and proximity is a hint, not an answer — the locator result on the exact address is. Second, every track has its own line: Johnston’s English and Middle French Immersion entries are published separately, so the track your child will actually enter is the entry to check. Third, lines move: boards review boundaries as neighbourhoods grow and enrolment shifts, which is why this series names the board source and its update date on every guide, and re-checks its catchments every September.

One feature of this line surprises buyers, and the locator proves it: Johnston’s boundary is drawn on lot lines, and east of March Road it reaches into the Leacock Drive pocket — homes on Leacock Drive itself, the street Stephen Leacock Public School stands on, are assigned to Johnston for the English program (the board’s locator returns Johnston for homes on that street for every grade, JK to 6). A school’s street address is where the building sits, not where its own line runs. This guide’s boundary was cross-checked the way every guide in this series is: the board’s index entry was read against the school’s own site, and against the transportation source — OSTA, which runs home-to-school transportation for the OCDSB. One distinction is worth stating plainly because buyers mix it up: OSTA eligibility is a distance test (for Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6, an address 1.6 km or more from the school) applied inside the boundary — it is not the boundary itself.

One Catchment, Two Schools: The Castlefrank and Katimavik Feeder Story

Families comparing this side of Kanata with the zone south of it always ask the same question: why does Castlefrank Elementary stop at Grade 3? It looks like a mistake. It is deliberate — one shared catchment split across two buildings by age, and the history is documented in the board’s own papers. Katimavik Elementary School, on Chimo Drive, opened in 1981 as a full Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8 school, and the growing neighbourhood overwhelmed it: by about 2010 it held more than 800 students, roughly 200 percent over capacity, with a dozen portables in the yard. Castlefrank Elementary School, on McCurdy Drive, was built in 1988 as the community grew, and by 2004 it too had become a full Kindergarten to Grade 8 school.

In April 2012, the board’s Kanata South Accommodation Review split the one catchment between the two buildings: from September 2013, Castlefrank became the primary school (Junior Kindergarten to Grade 3) and Katimavik the junior and intermediate school (Grades 4 to 8), phased in so no child had to bounce between schools. The little kids get a small school of about 300 students; the older kids get the big building. Same streets, same neighbourhood, start to finish — and both schools’ own websites describe the exact path: Castlefrank to Katimavik, then on to Earl of March Secondary School for high school.

That is the feeder picture for the whole Kanata English zone, and it is why this series builds it as one connected set of guides. North of the highway, Johnston and Stephen Leacock each run Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6 in one building, then Earl of March. South of it, the Castlefrank–Katimavik pairing covers the same years across two buildings, then the same Earl of March. Buy on either side and the destination converges — what changes is the shape of the elementary years, and a family that knows the story can choose the shape on purpose instead of discovering the Grade 4 move after the fact.

Questions Families Ask First

Does buying a home inside the W Erskine Johnston catchment guarantee my child a place at the school?
No. School assignment is always the school board’s decision, boundaries can change, and programs fill. A home inside the line is a strong starting point for a search — never a guarantee. Confirm the schools assigned to the exact address with the board’s school locator, and directly with any other school you are considering, before you firm up an offer.

How do I check which school a Kanata address belongs to?
Use the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board’s official School Locator, which the board lists as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. Enter the exact address — not just the street or the neighbourhood — because a boundary can run along lot lines and put one side of a street in a different catchment from the other.

Are the English and French Immersion boundaries for Johnston the same line?
No. The OCDSB publishes Johnston’s boundaries by program track: the English Program with Core French line (Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6, updated January 28, 2025) is the line this guide’s listings follow, and the Middle French Immersion track (Grades 4 to 6) carries its own boundary entry, updated July 30, 2026. The board is also phasing Middle French Immersion out board-wide, with a final intake in September 2028, while a French Immersion program is being phased into Johnston starting September 2026, adding a grade each year. If your child’s program is a specific track, check that track’s own entry before you rely on the line.

Where do Johnston students go after Grade 6?
After Grade 6, students in this catchment move to Earl of March Intermediate School for Grades 7 and 8, then Earl of March Secondary School for Grades 9 to 12 — both in the same building at 4 The Parkway. The board’s School Locator returns Earl of March I.S. for both intermediate grades at addresses across the catchment (checked across the catchment for the 2026–2027 school year), so the path runs Johnston, then one building from Grade 7 to graduation.

If my child needs a bus, does living in the catchment mean they get one?
Not automatically. Transportation in Ottawa is run by OSTA, and eligibility is a distance test on top of the boundary: for Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6, a student’s address must be inside the designated attendance boundary and 1.6 km or more from the school. Closer than that is the walk zone. OSTA’s “Do I Qualify?” check answers for one address at a time, and the board’s transportation page sets the rules out.

What does Johnston’s Fraser rating of 9.7 actually measure?
It is the Fraser Institute’s report-card rating for the school — 9.7 out of 10, ranked #42 in Ontario in the 2025 elementary report card. It is one input among many: it says nothing about whether a specific address falls inside the line, and it is not a promise about any individual child’s experience. This series prints the figure with its source and year so you can weigh it properly.

Are the listings on this page really inside the catchment?
Yes — that is the promise this page is built on. The searches run on the school’s own official board boundary, digitized from the board’s map, not on a postal code or a neighbourhood outline. Every listing address the searches display was checked against that line, and the numbers in each snapshot count exactly the homes its map displays — so when you click a home here, it sits inside the Johnston line. Confirm any single address on the board’s locator before you firm up an offer, the same check this guide runs.

The Village — North Gower’s Story (8009)

North Gower grew up as a farm-service village: the place the surrounding townships came to for supplies, services and school. It was the heart of North Gower Township — Marlborough Township farmed to its south — and its main crossroads still reads that way: Church Street climbing through the village, Roger Stevens Drive running the township line toward Richmond, and the streets between them lined with the older homes of a village that has never stopped being a working one.

The village keeps its institutions. North Gower Public School, on Church Street, has served the two old townships since 1969. The Alfred Taylor Recreation Centre on Community Way is the recreation and community hub — home to the village’s co-operative nursery school, its community events and its Christmas market. And in season, the North Gower Farmers’ Market on Roger Stevens Drive brings the surrounding farms’ produce into the middle of the village. Amalgamation folded the townships into Rideau Township and then, in 2001, into the City of Ottawa — but North Gower’s daily life is still organised around the village, the farms and the road to Richmond.

The Wider District — Concessions, Acreages and Farms (8009)

Step off the village streets and the district turns to country. Old Forest Drive, toward the river side of the district, carries the estate register — large treed lots and substantial custom homes set back from the road. Fourth Line Road and the concessions run the farm register: working farms, hobby farms and country homes on multi-acre parcels, where frontage, acreage and outbuildings set the value as much as the house does.

The homework is the rural file, and it is honest work: well quality and quantity, septic age and capacity, drainage, and what a parcel can actually be built, expanded or severed into. Village lots carry the lighter version of the same file. Either way, value in North Gower is land first — acreage, frontage, trees and position relative to the village and the 416 — and two properties with the same square footage can sit far apart on those things alone.

Living in North Gower — Schools, the Rec Centre and the Drive

North Gower’s own school stands in the village, and the pathway from it runs up the river — a genuinely small-community school run for a village this size. The standing warning applies — catchments are set by your exact home address and program, so confirm any specific property in the board’s school locator before you buy.

  • North Gower Public School (OCDSB, Junior Kindergarten–Grade 5) — 2403 Church Street, in the village. Regular English program with Core French; built in 1969 to serve the Townships of North Gower and Marlborough, and the school this village’s children start in.

  • Kars on the Rideau Public School (OCDSB, Junior Kindergarten–Grade 8) — in Kars, up the river. North Gower students move here in Grade 6 — the middle-grades step of the village’s own pathway.

  • South Carleton High School (OCDSB, Grades 9–12) — in Richmond. The public secondary for the region, drawing over 1,300 students from Manotick, Kars, North Gower and the surrounding countryside.

  • St. Mark Catholic High School (OCSB, Grades 7–12) — 1040 Dozois Road, in Manotick East. The Catholic secondary for the whole Manotick group, in English and French.

  • Catholic elementary (OCSB) — no Catholic elementary school stands in the village; Catholic elementary placements run to schools in the surrounding communities and follow your exact address, so confirm yours in the board’s school locator.

  • French-language boards (CEPEO / CECCE) — no French-language school sits in North Gower itself; French-board catchments follow their own sector maps, so confirm your exact address in the French boards’ locators.

For daily life, the village runs on the Alfred Taylor Recreation Centre — recreation, the co-operative nursery school, community events and the Christmas market — and, in season, on the farmers’ market on Roger Stevens Drive. Manotick, ten minutes north, supplies the rest: groceries, cafés, Dickinson Square and the river. For the drive, Roger Stevens Drive runs east to Highway 416 in minutes, and downtown is roughly half an hour outside rush hour. Transit is thin to the point of honesty: no regular transit runs to the village, so North Gower is a driving community — the land and the quiet are what the drive buys.

Questions Buyers Ask About North Gower

What is North Gower?
North Gower is OREB district 8009 — a historic farming village in Ottawa’s rural south, straddling Roger Stevens Drive where Church Street and Fourth Line Road meet it. It was the heart of the old North Gower Township, with Marlborough Township to its south, before amalgamation folded the townships into Rideau Township and then, in 2001, into the City of Ottawa. The district drawn for this guide is the village and its fringe: village lots, estate acreages and working farms inside one compact area, about 11 kilometres southwest of Manotick as the crow flies.

What kinds of homes and lots does North Gower offer?
Two registers. In the village: older homes and newer builds on village lots along Church Street, Roger Stevens Drive and the side streets — walkable to the school, the recreation centre and the farmers’ market. Around it: estate acreages and hobby farms on the concession roads, including the large treed properties off Old Forest Drive toward the Rideau River side of the district, and multi-acre parcels on Fourth Line Road. It is a freehold market — houses, acreages and farms, no condos.

What services do North Gower homes use?
Country servicing. Homes here run on private wells and septic systems — municipal water and sewer do not reach the village — so the homework is the well record, the septic file, and what both mean for the lot you are buying. Village lots are smaller and simpler; acreages add outbuildings, frontage and drainage to the list. None of it is unusual for Ottawa’s rural south, and all of it belongs in the file before you offer, not after.

Which schools serve North Gower?
North Gower Public School stands in the village at 2403 Church Street (OCDSB, Junior Kindergarten–Grade 5, English with Core French) — built in 1969 to serve the old Townships of North Gower and Marlborough. From Grade 6, village students move up the river to Kars on the Rideau Public School, and the public path finishes at South Carleton High School in Richmond (Grades 9–12). Catholic students use St. Mark Catholic High School in Manotick (Grades 7–12); Catholic elementary and French-board placements follow your exact address — confirm in the boards’ school locators before you buy.

How far is North Gower from Manotick, Highway 416 and downtown?
Manotick is about ten minutes north — the full-service village for groceries, cafés and the river. Highway 416 is minutes east on Roger Stevens Drive, and from the interchange downtown Ottawa is roughly half an hour outside rush hour. This is honestly a driving village: no regular transit runs here, and that is the trade residents make on purpose — land, quiet and dark skies, with the city’s south end in easy reach.

Why do North Gower listings span such a wide price range?
Because the district holds the village and the farm country in one small polygon. At one end are village homes on modest lots; at the other are estate acreages and farm properties where the land itself — acreage, frontage, trees and position — carries the value. The live range on this page runs from the village entry point to the estate ceiling, and two homes with the same square footage can sit far apart on land alone. Read the range as two markets, and price the one you are actually buying.

What actually comes up for rent in Longfields

Longfields is 82% owner-occupied, so its rental shelf is turnover, not a purpose-built rental stock — owners who are posted away, move up, or hold and lease. What surfaces is the pocket's own freehold: three-bedroom townhomes and semis on Berrigan Drive, Claridge Drive and the side streets off Longfields Drive, and detached houses up to the Tierney Drive end of the pocket. This week's live shelf shows the shape exactly: homes asking from $1,900, the townhome and semi band clustered around $2,550 to $2,800, and detached houses up to $3,600. The pocket's condo apartments and stacked condos (Hornchurch Lane, Tayside Private, the Claridge Drive buildings) rent too — they live in the separate condo-rental watch, not in this freehold guide. If you need a house, a yard and a garage in Barrhaven without buying, this freehold shelf is the one to watch, and it turns over one home at a time.

Station life — renting beside Longfields Station

The station is the renter's argument for this pocket. Longfields Station, on the Southwest Transitway just south of the rail line, opened April 17, 2011 — and it is the only station that belongs to a single Barrhaven neighbourhood. From a rental on Berrigan, Mountshannon or Longfields Drive itself, the morning commute is a walk, not a drive: the Frequent Route 75 runs the Transitway spine from Barrhaven Centre through Longfields to Tunney's Pasture, with Local 173, peak Connexion 275 and 279, Shopper 305 and event routes 406 and 456 also stopping here. A pathway runs under the tracks at the station, tying the north streets to the platforms. Look up as you board: the station's green roof carries Bellwether (2011), life-sized bronze sheep and a border collie by Erin Robertson and Anna Williams — a nod to the farmland these streets replaced, and one of the sheep stands in the grass beside the platform in the photo below. Plenty of Longfields renters keep one car, or none: between the station, the buses on Longfields Drive and everything on the pocket's edges, car-light living actually works here.

Who rents in Longfields — and why

Four renter profiles choose these streets on purpose. School-driven families, who want a specific school bench — the Berrigan-to-Longfields-Davidson Heights campus, two Catholic secondaries on Longfields Drive itself, French schools in both French boards — without buying into the pocket to get it. Relocating households and professionals on postings, who want a finished, quiet family street and a transit commute from day one. Station-first commuters, who trade a second car for a walk to the Transitway. And renters saving to buy in the pocket, who would rather rent on the street they intend to own on than start over in a new subdivision. What they share: they are ready before a listing appears, because a good Longfields freehold rental does not sit.

Schools — check the catchment before you sign the lease

A lease makes an address, and the address sets the school — so in Longfields, check the catchment before you sign, not after. Eight schools serve the pocket across all four boards, and five stand inside it. Public English: Berrigan Elementary School (199 Berrigan Drive, JK–6) on the same campus as Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School (149 Berrigan Drive, 7–12, about 2,250 students). Catholic: St. Luke School (60 Mountshannon Drive), Monsignor Paul Baxter School (333 Beatrice Drive) and St. Mother Teresa High School (440 Longfields Drive, 7–12). French Catholic: École élémentaire catholique Pierre-Elliott-Trudeau (601 Longfields Drive) and École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard (1110 Longfields Drive, 7–12). French public: École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean (11 Claridge Drive, JK–8, IB Primary Years). The three schools nearest the station — Pierre-Elliott-Trudeau, St. Mother Teresa and Longfields-Davidson Heights — make the north end of the pocket a walk-to-school, walk-to-transit zone. Zones are set by address and do shift: confirm yours with the board's school locator before you sign, or tell me the school first and I will confirm the catchment for any specific address.

Parks, pathways and everyday green space

Renting a house here buys you the pocket's parks without the mortgage. Ken Ross Park (600 Longfields Drive) is the anchor — sports fields, forested paths and one of the pocket's off-leash dog parks. Water Dragon Park (424 Chapman Mills Drive) adds a splash pad, sports fields, a covered gazebo and a winter tobogganing hill; Mancini Park (110 Mancini Street) has pickleball courts; the Berrigan Skate Park sits beside the school campus; W.C. Levesque Park (2436 Longfields Drive) and North Harrow Park (32 North Harrow Drive, also off-leash) round out a roster of roughly twenty neighbourhood parks — the deepest bench of any Barrhaven sub-area. The pathway network threads through all of it and under the tracks at the station entrance below, so most of the pocket is reachable on foot or by bike without touching an arterial road.

Getting around from Longfields

Transit is this pocket's home-field advantage: Longfields Station (Frequent 75, Local 173, Connexion 275 and 279, Shopper 305, Event 406 and 456) plus bus stops on Longfields Drive itself, including at Mountshannon Drive and by St. Mother Teresa High School. The 75 runs the Transitway spine to Tunney's Pasture, where the O-Train takes over for downtown. By car, Greenbank Road and Woodroffe Avenue frame the pocket, Fallowfield Road and Strandherd Drive run along its edges, and Highway 416 access puts downtown at roughly 25–30 minutes off-peak.

Child care

The City View Centre for Child & Family Services (1099 Longfields Drive) sits inside the pocket — nursery school, EarlyON playgroups and licensed child care under one roof. The OCSB runs school-age child care at Monsignor Paul Baxter School (333 Beatrice Drive), and Montessori by Brightpath – Fallowfield (3500 Fallowfield Road, 70 licensed spaces) is on the pocket's north edge.

Groceries and daily needs

Daily shopping sits on the pocket's edges and along its spine. Farm Boy (1581 Greenbank Road) anchors the west edge, with Sobeys and Canadian Tire at the Greenbank–Fallowfield corner; the Marketplace and Strandherd Drive corridor at the south edge carries the big-box set. Inside the pocket, the Al-Raheem South Asian and Middle Eastern Marketplace (34 Highbury Park Drive) serves daily needs, and the Longfields Drive strip has Papa John's (605 Longfields Drive), Juluca's Bistro Cafe and Tim Hortons, plus Longfields Pharmacy, Longfields Dental and the Dynacare lab. Longfields Community Church (3376 Woodroffe Avenue) anchors the east edge — in its own building after 25 years of meeting in school gyms.

What Ottawa landlords ask for — come prepared

Ontario landlords typically ask for a credit check, proof of income or an employment letter, references from previous landlords, and first and last month's rent as deposit — the only legal deposit in Ontario; "key money" is not permitted. Ontario's Standard Form of Lease is mandatory for most tenancies. Have your documents ready before you tour: when a Longfields freehold rental lists, the prepared applicant wins — and I can get your application in front of the listing agent the same day.

Ontario renting essentials

Most leases start with a one-year term, then continue month-to-month. Rent control applies to units first occupied before November 15, 2018. "No pets" clauses are void under Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act, which makes freehold houses the pet-friendly choice. Utilities are often tenant-paid; snow removal and lawn care are negotiable.

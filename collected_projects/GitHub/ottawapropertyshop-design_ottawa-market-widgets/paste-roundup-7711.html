The Barrhaven picture: Barrhaven-wide, 192 freehold homes are listed today, and Half Moon Bay's 90 are nearly half of them — 47 percent, the largest single share of the suburb's shelf, held by its newest community. Its $684,400 median sits just $3,500 above the Barrhaven-wide median of $680,900 — the two middles have nearly converged, and what gap remains is a mix story as much as a price story: nearly everything here is newer stock, much of it detached family homes, against a suburb-wide figure that blends in every pocket and product type, condos included in the wider market's mix of streets.

What I make of it: the story this time is movement, not stability — a thinner shelf, a middle that stepped up and a first rung that rose with it, while the top of the range never blinked. With 90 homes to compare, a buyer can still wait for the right street, the right builder and the right floor plan inside one community instead of chasing single listings across the city — just know the entry ticket costs $20,900 more than it did two weeks ago. Sellers get the mirror image of that advice: in a pocket where a buyer can see nine other homes this weekend, pricing to today's market is not a slogan, it is the whole strategy — the homes that sit are the ones priced for a market that has already moved on. And remember what these numbers are: asking prices on the public shelf. What happens to a listing after it leaves the shelf — sold, expired or withdrawn — the aggregates cannot tell us.

Barrhaven's newest community, still growing into its plan

Half Moon Bay is Barrhaven South's master-planned community: farmland within living memory — Mattamy launched it in 2007 — and nearly everything here is 21st-century stock. It rolls out in three sections: the original Half Moon Bay, Half Moon Bay West and Quinn's Pointe, wrapped around crescents, circles, pocket parks and stormwater ponds south of the Jock River. Even the street names look up — Watercolours Way, Moonglade Crescent, Aphelion Crescent. Builders are still at work: Mattamy, Minto, Caivan, Tamarack and Claridge phases continue to build out, which is why resale homes here are always being compared against brand-new product down the street, and why the shelf keeps refilling as phases release.

Getting around from Half Moon Bay

On transit, this is a bus community with a real frequent route: route 75 runs through the interior streets — extended south of Kilbirnie Drive along River Mist Road, Cappamore Drive and Greenbank Road, past the station in the banner above — and connects to Line 1 of the O-Train at Tunney's Pasture. Route 275 is the peak-period companion: Half Moon Bay to Tunney's Pasture in the weekday morning and afternoon peaks. For drivers who go partway, the nearest park and rides are Nepean Woods (278 free spaces), Strandherd (405 free spaces) and Fallowfield (1,648 free spaces). By car, this is the closest of Barrhaven's eleven pockets to Highway 416, out Barnsdale Road. And the corridor is being rebuilt around the community: the Greenbank Road realignment and Southwest Transitway extension will bring a new arterial with a bus rapid transit corridor in its median, stations at Cambrian, Dundonald and Kilbirnie, and a future park and ride at Barnsdale Road.

Schools around Half Moon Bay

Schools are this community's signature strength: Half Moon Bay has a school inside the community in every board — no other Barrhaven pocket can say that. What it does not have is one simple answer per board, because both English boards split the community by street:

  • Public English (OCDSB): two in-community elementaries share the pocket — Half Moon Bay Public School (kindergarten to grade 6, English and Early French Immersion) and Wazoson Public School, which opened in January 2024; its name means "bird's nest" in Algonquin/Anishinaabe, and a child-care centre shares its site. Wazoson opened with English and Early French Immersion from kindergarten through grade 5. Older students go to Cedarview Middle School (grades 7 to 8) and then John McCrae Secondary School (grades 9 to 12). Ahead: a funded public secondary school is planned inside Half Moon Bay itself, on Cambrian Road — reported for a September 2029 opening.

  • Catholic English (OCSB): three in-community elementaries share the pocket by street — St. Benedict, St. Cecilia and St. Juan Diego, which opened in 2024. All three feed St. Joseph High School (grades 7 to 12) on Greenbank Road, at the community's north edge.

  • French Catholic (CECCE): École élémentaire catholique Sainte-Kateri (kindergarten to grade 6) stands inside the community on River Mist Road; older students go to École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard (grades 7 to 12).

  • French public (CEPEO): École élémentaire publique Des Visionnaires (kindergarten to grade 6) stands inside the community in Quinn's Pointe and opened for the 2025–26 school year; the board's Barrhaven secondary is École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois (grades 7 to 12), which runs the International Baccalaureate program.

Catchments turn on the exact street here — sometimes on which side of Cambrian Road the street sits — so check yours on each board's school-locator tool before you buy. My Half Moon Bay guide points you to the same locators.

Parks, play and daily life

The anchor is the Minto Recreation Complex — Barrhaven, on Cambrian Road: a six-lane lap pool with diving boards, leisure pools, two NHL-sized rinks, an outdoor artificial-turf field, an indoor walking track and a gymnasium. Half Moon Bay Park carries a splash pad and the community's landmark — the "Falling Star," a 13-foot cast-aluminium star balanced on one point atop the toboggan hill, its surface pierced with hundreds of small holes that shimmer at night. Guinness Park adds a second splash pad, Half Moon Bay District Park wraps its walks around a stormwater pond, and a deep bench of pocket parks — River Run Park, River Mist Park, Watercolours Park and more — serves every section. The Jock River itself is the community's northern green edge, a protected corridor along the south bank.

Churches, plazas and the daily run

Retail has arrived inside the community: Food Basics is open on Cambrian Road — Barrhaven South's first grocery store, with a full-service pharmacy — at the community core where Cambrian meets the realigned Greenbank Road, and a second commercial node is taking shape in Half Moon Bay West, at Borrisokane Road and Flagstaff Drive. Faith life has an in-community home too: the MAC Barrhaven Islamic Centre stands on Greenbank Road, hosting daily and Jumu'ah prayers. The big weekly runs still go north across the Jock River, minutes away: Chapman Mills Marketplace, Barrhaven Town Centre and Strandherd Crossing cover the rest — Loblaws, Walmart Supercentre, the Cineplex, Metro and Your Independent Grocer among them.

The Short Answer

Earl of March Secondary School is an Ottawa-Carleton District School Board (OCDSB) secondary school in Kanata. The board publishes its attendance boundary as one combined entry — the Earl Of March SS English and Immersion Attendance Boundary on its official secondary boundary index, updated July 9, 2025 — and answers for one exact address at a time through its School Locator, listed as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. In the Fraser Institute’s 2025 report card (built on 2023/24 data), Earl of March rates 8.6 out of 10, ranked #39 of 747 Ontario secondary schools. Its two Kanata elementary feeders sit near the top of the elementary report card: W. Erskine Johnston Public School at 9.7 out of 10 (#42 in Ontario) and Stephen Leacock Public School at 9.4 out of 10 (#67 in Ontario). A family with a child in either elementary and a teenager at Earl of March is shopping one connected zone — so this series builds it as one, starting here.

The Catchment, From the Board’s Own Line

A catchment — the board calls it an attendance boundary — is the area a school draws its students from, and for Earl of March three things about the line matter to a buyer. First, the board decides, not the map: the school at the end of the street is not evidence, and proximity is a hint, not an answer — the locator result on the exact address is. Second, every track has its own line: Earl of March’s English and Immersion entry is published as one combined entry, but other programs carry their own boundaries, so the track your child will actually enter is the entry to check. Third, lines move: boards review boundaries as neighbourhoods grow and enrolment shifts, which is why this series names the board source and its update date on every guide, and re-checks its catchments every September.

This guide’s boundary was cross-checked the way every guide in this series is: the board’s index entry was read against the school’s own site, and against the transportation source — OSTA, which runs home-to-school transportation for the OCDSB. The sources agree, and one distinction is worth stating plainly because buyers mix it up: OSTA eligibility is a distance test (for grades 9–12, an address 3.2 km or more from the school) applied inside the boundary — it is not the boundary itself. The street-by-street line lives in the board’s official boundary map and locator, linked from this page; this guide does not print a street list it has not verified.

Questions Families Ask First

Does buying a home inside the Earl of March catchment guarantee my child a place at the school?
No. School assignment is always the school board’s decision, boundaries can change, and programs fill. A home inside the line is a strong starting point for a search — never a guarantee. Confirm the schools assigned to the exact address with the board’s school locator, and directly with any other school you are considering, before you firm up an offer.

How do I check which school a Kanata address belongs to?
Use the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board’s official School Locator, which the board lists as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. Enter the exact address — not just the street or the neighbourhood — because a boundary can run along a street and put one side of it in a different catchment from the other.

Are the English and French Immersion boundaries for Earl of March the same line?
Not necessarily. The OCDSB publishes its boundary entries by program track, and the tracks do not have to match. This guide covers Earl of March’s English and Immersion entry — one combined entry on the board’s secondary boundary index, updated July 9, 2025. If your child’s program is a different track, check that track’s own entry before you rely on the line.

Which elementary schools feed into Earl of March?
The two Kanata elementary feeders in this series are W. Erskine Johnston Public School (Fraser Institute rating 9.7 out of 10, #42 in Ontario in the 2025 elementary report card) and Stephen Leacock Public School (9.4 out of 10, #67 in Ontario). Each has its own guide in this series, linked above, and each verifies its own program tracks from the board’s elementary boundary index.

If my child needs a bus, does living in the catchment mean they get one?
Not automatically. Transportation in Ottawa is run by OSTA, and eligibility is a distance test on top of the boundary: for grades 9–12, a student’s address must be inside the designated attendance boundary and 3.2 km or more from the school. Closer than that is the walk zone. OSTA’s “Do I Qualify?” check answers for one address at a time, and the board’s transportation page sets the rules out.

What does Earl of March’s Fraser rating of 8.6 actually measure?
It is the Fraser Institute’s report-card rating for the school — 8.6 out of 10, ranked #39 of 747 Ontario secondary schools in the 2025 report card, which is built on 2023/24 data. It is one input among many: it says nothing about whether a specific address falls inside the line, and it is not a promise about any individual child’s experience. This series prints the figure with its source and year so you can weigh it properly.

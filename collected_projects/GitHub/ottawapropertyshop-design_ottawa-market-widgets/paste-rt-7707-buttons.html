What actually comes up for rent in Hearts Desire

Hearts Desire is almost entirely owner-occupied, so its rental shelf is an event, not a stock — an owner posted away for a year, a household between homes, an estate held and leased. What surfaces is the enclave's own freehold, and only that: detached two-storeys, bungalows and sidesplits, mostly custom or one-off builds from the 1970s to the early 2000s, on lots that dwarf modern Barrhaven — some approaching half an acre, a few with Jock River frontage. There are no townhomes, semis or condos in this pocket at all — the low-density character is protected — so a house rental here means a detached estate home or nothing. Two local realities come with the address: some homes run on a private well and septic system rather than full municipal services, and a lot this size means lawn and snow arrangements belong in the lease conversation, not after it. When a Hearts Desire home does list for rent, expect it to price with the estate stock it is — at the top of Barrhaven's house-rental range — and to draw applicants who have been waiting for exactly this pocket.

Renting the enclave — the Jock River at the end of the street

The renter's argument for this pocket is what it refuses to be: no through traffic, no production rows, no commercial strips — just mature maples, oversized lots and a river. The trade is deliberate: you give up a Transitway station of your own and the newest floor plans, and you get space, privacy and country quiet inside a suburb that grew up around you. Renters who choose these streets are choosing the lot and the quiet first; the house comes with both.

Parks, the Jock River and recreation

Renting a house here buys you the enclave's green without the mortgage. The anchor is Heart's Desire Park (3566 Woodroffe Avenue) — roughly 32 acres of mature maple forest with walking trails, an off-leash dog park, and the Jock River along its edge. The river itself is the enclave's south boundary: a 72 km tributary of the Rideau, with a paddle launch (Jock River Landing) just across Prince of Wales Drive. Smaller parks fill the pockets — Barcham Park (80 Bren-Maur Road) and Davidson Park (5 Ryerson Avenue, sports field and lit tennis courts) — and the Walter Baker Sports Centre on Malvern Drive, with its pools, arenas and library branch, is minutes north.

Getting around from Hearts Desire

Hearts Desire is a driver's enclave, honestly stated. Downtown is roughly 20 minutes off-peak: Greenbank Road or Prince of Wales Drive north to the core, or Strandherd Drive west to Highway 416 for Kanata and the 417. The airport runs about 20 minutes via Prince of Wales Drive. There is no Transitway station inside the enclave — the nearest are Strandherd Station and Barrhaven Centre Station, a short drive or local-bus ride away, with OC Transpo routes running along Greenbank Road, Strandherd Drive and Prince of Wales Drive. Most households here simply plan around two cars.

What Ottawa landlords ask for — come prepared

Ontario landlords typically ask for a credit check, proof of income or an employment letter, references from previous landlords, and first and last month's rent as deposit — the only legal deposit in Ontario; "key money" is not permitted. Ontario's Standard Form of Lease is mandatory for most tenancies. On an estate lot, settle the grounds in writing before you sign: lawn care, snow clearing, and — where the home runs on them — well and septic responsibilities. Have your documents ready before you tour: when a Hearts Desire freehold rental lists, the prepared applicant wins — and I can get your application in front of the listing agent the same day.

Ontario renting essentials

Most leases start with a one-year term, then continue month-to-month. Rent control applies to units first occupied before November 15, 2018. "No pets" clauses are void under Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act, which makes freehold houses the pet-friendly choice. Utilities are often tenant-paid; snow removal and lawn care are negotiable.

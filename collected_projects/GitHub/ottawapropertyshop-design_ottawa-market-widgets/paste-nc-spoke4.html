The $1M Line: Two Rebates, One Ceiling

A new home in Ottawa carries 13% HST: a 5% federal part (the GST) and an 8% provincial part. Two rebate routes meet at the $1,000,000 line. The first is the first-time buyer stack: the federal First-Time Home Buyers' GST rebate returns 100% of the 5% — up to $50,000 — and Ontario mirrors it with a first-time buyer rebate of the full 8% — up to $80,000. Together they return the entire 13% on a home to $1,000,000: up to $130,000. Above $1,000,000 the stack phases out in a straight line, reaching zero at $1,500,000.

The second route is Ontario's enhanced HST rebate, and it is wider: it covers all eligible buyers, not just first-timers, on agreements signed from April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027. It also pays the full 13% on a home to $1,000,000 — and then, unlike the first-time stack, it holds its $130,000 cap flat all the way to $1,500,000 before phasing down to a $24,000 floor at $1,850,000. The mechanics guide in this series works that program line by line. How the two routes interact: the federal side is covered once, never twice — inside the Ontario window, the provincial top-up fills whatever the federal rebates leave behind; it does not duplicate them. And the warning that runs through this whole series, because it is the most repeated error in this market: there is no federal 13% rebate. The 13% is always two parts — a federal 5% and a provincial 8%.

The Product That Fits Under the Cap

New homes under $1M Ottawa builders are actually selling come off four product shelves. Everything else in this guide hangs off these four:

  • Townhomes and semi-detached homes — the volume shelf. In the big suburban communities this is where the under-$1M market lives: rows of towns and semis released phase by phase, priced to stay under the cap because the builders know exactly what the rebate does to demand at this line.

  • Detached homes in the ring towns — the same house on cheaper land. A single-detached home that cannot exist under $1,000,000 inside the Greenbelt still can in the towns around the city, where the lot costs a fraction of a suburban lot.

  • New condos — the deepest shelf. Condo apartments and condo towns are the one product line where most of the catalogue sits under the cap, in every part of the city — with their own deposit and Tarion rules, covered by the condo guide in this series.

  • Quick-possession and inventory homes — the shortcut shelf. Standing homes a builder has already built price to move, close in months instead of years, and take the same rebates the same way.

Two Clocks Are Running

The under-$1M advantage runs on two different clocks, and which one governs you depends on who you are. The Ontario clock is loud: the enhanced rebate covers agreements signed April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027, and the 2026 Ontario Budget stated it will not be extended. If you are not a first-time buyer, that window is your whole story — sign inside it and the full 13% comes back on a home to $1,000,000; sign outside it and you fall back to the standard rebates, which at Ottawa prices are a fraction of the money.

The first-time buyer clock is quiet: agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, construction begun before 2031, the home substantially complete before 2036. No annual window, no deadline quarter. A first-time buyer under the cap reaches the full $130,000 on either clock — which means a first-time buyer can choose the right community and the right lot instead of the fastest signature. How to Shop the Cap Without Getting Burned

  • Ask whether the price is before or after rebates — every time. Some builders quote "net of HST" pricing, with the Ontario rebate already deducted; others quote before everything. A net $999,000 and a gross $999,000 are not the same house money-wise, and comparing one builder's net against another's gross is comparing fiction.

  • Watch the upgrades, not just the base price. The rebate math runs on the final price. A $985,000 base home with $40,000 of design-studio upgrades is a $1,025,000 purchase — and if you are a first-time buyer, the slide into the phase-out has already started.

  • Register properly on the first visit. The rebate is not the only thing a careless first visit can cost you: walk into a sales office alone and the builder registers you to themselves, and your own agent is locked out. The flagship guide in this series exists because of that one rule.

  • Plan the deposit, not just the price. Under-cap product still carries builder deposit schedules that run from a few percent staged over the build to $80,000–$100,000 on some singles. The home you can sign for is the one whose deposit schedule your cash flow survives.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "under $1M" mean for the rebates — the price before or after tax?

The price of the new home itself — the agreement price the HST is charged on. At $1,000,000 or less, the first-time buyer rebates pay 100% of their parts ($50,000 federal plus $80,000 Ontario), and the Ontario enhanced rebate pays the full 13% as well. Cross the line and the first-time buyer stack starts phasing out, dollar by dollar.

How much comes back on an $850,000 new home?

All of the tax: $110,500. That is 13% of $850,000, returned in full — by the first-time buyer stack if you qualify as a first-time buyer, or by the Ontario enhanced rebate if your agreement is signed inside its April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027 window.

I am not a first-time buyer. Do I still get the full $130,000 under $1M?

Yes — inside the Ontario window. The enhanced rebate has no first-time condition: on an agreement signed April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027, any eligible buyer of a new home to live in (or to hold as a residential rental) gets the full 13% back, to $130,000 at $1,000,000. The federal 5% inside it is covered once, through the Ontario top-up — never twice.

What happens to the rebate at $1,250,000?

The two routes split. The first-time buyer stack is halfway through its phase-out: $65,000 back. The Ontario enhanced rebate is still paying its full $130,000 cap, which holds flat to $1,500,000 inside the window before phasing down to a $24,000 floor at $1,850,000.

Which Ottawa communities still have new homes under $1M?

Townhomes and semis in Barrhaven, Kanata, Stittsville, Orléans, Riverside South and Findlay Creek; detached homes in the ring towns — Embrun, Russell, Kemptville, Carleton Place, Rockland and Almonte; and new condos across the city. Quick-possession inventory can put any of those shelves in reach on a shorter clock.

Do condos under $1M qualify for the full rebate?

Yes — new construction is new construction, and most new condo product sits under the cap on price. What changes with a condo is everything around the rebate: a heavier deposit structure, an interim occupancy period before final closing, and Tarion deposit protection with its own limits. The condo guide in this series covers that file in full.

Are builder prices under $1M quoted before or after the rebate?

Both — and the sheet has to say which. "Net of HST" pricing already has the Ontario rebate deducted; a before-rebate price does not. Comparing one builder's net price against another's gross price is the most common apples-to-oranges error in this market, and it can be worth tens of thousands of dollars on the same floor plan.

On purpose. Clicking through and visiting alone is how buyers end up registered to the builder instead of represented by their own agent — and once that registration happens, it cannot be undone. The detailed builder information lives in the free Builder Deals guide instead: models, deals, rebates, discounts, floor plans, prices, lot sizes and builder masterplans, where available.

When does the under-$1M advantage end?

On two different dates. The Ontario enhanced rebate ends for agreements signed after March 31, 2027, and the 2026 Ontario Budget stated it will not be extended. The first-time buyer stack runs much longer — agreements before 2031 — so after March 2027, being a first-time buyer becomes the only route to the full $130,000.

How do I actually claim the money?

Usually you do not pay it first. Most buyers assign the rebate to the builder at closing — you sign CRA Form GST190, the builder credits the amount on your closing statement and claims it from CRA. If you claim it yourself, you pay the full HST at closing and file the same form after. Read the assignment clause before you sign: most builder agreements make you liable to repay the rebate if CRA later denies the claim.

The Senior Move Timeline: What to Do When

Every successful downsizing move I have managed in Ottawa followed roughly the same timeline. The moves that go sideways are almost always the rushed ones — a health event forces a move in three weeks, and everything becomes triage. If you have the gift of time, use it.

Three to six months out: decisions and decluttering. Decide what kind of next home fits your life — smaller house, condo, 55+ adult lifestyle community, or a retirement residence with care available if you ever need it. This is the stage to talk to a REALTOR® who does senior moves (more on that below), get a realistic price opinion on your current home, update your will and powers of attorney with an estate lawyer before the paperwork of a sale begins, and start the decluttering system I describe in the next section. If you are weighing whether to stay put with help instead of moving, this is also the moment to price home-care agencies and accessibility upgrades — sometimes the math surprises people. Book movers early if your move will fall in May, June, or September — the three busiest moving months in Ottawa.

One to two months out: offers and arrangements. With your next home chosen, the sell-first-or-buy-first decision gets made (see below). Arrange movers, start packing non-essentials, decide what will be sold through an estate auctioneer versus donated or hauled away, notify your insurance broker, bank, pharmacy, and government accounts of the coming change of address, and book any minor repairs or staging for the home you are selling.

Closing week: the final sweep. Pack a "first night" box with medications, chargers, toiletries, bedding, and key documents so moving day does not start with a frantic box hunt. Confirm elevator or parking reservations for condo moves. Do a final walkthrough of the old home with your REALTOR® before closing.

Local tip: Ottawa moving companies fill up fast around Canada Day and Labour Day long weekends — if you can close mid-month instead, you will usually pay less and get your pick of crews. I flag this for every downsizing Ottawa client on a tight budget.

Right-Sizing a Lifetime of Belongings

Decluttering is the emotional heart of a senior move. A family home holds decades — children's bedrooms, workshops, holiday storage — and most of it cannot come along. The system I recommend to my clients is simple: one room at a time, four categories (keep, gift to family, donate, discard), and a strict rule that no one has to decide everything in a weekend.

Start with the easy wins: expired pantry goods, duplicate kitchen tools, old paperwork you no longer need. Save the emotional decisions — photographs, heirlooms, your late spouse's belongings — for later, when the system feels routine. Give adult children a firm but fair deadline to claim what they want; anything unclaimed after that date gets donated or sold. Who Does What: The Senior-Move Professionals Worth Knowing

Here is something most downsizing Ottawa guides skip: you do not have to do this alone, and the help available goes far beyond a moving truck. Ottawa has a real ecosystem of professionals who do nothing but help seniors through transitions. Knowing who does what — before you need them — is half the battle. Senior move managers and organizers. These are the quarterbacks of a senior move. A senior move manager coordinates the whole transition: decluttering room by room, arranging donations, packing, supervising the movers, unpacking at the new home, and even setting up the kitchen and bedrooms so the first night feels like home. Some belong to a national association of senior move managers with training standards and designations. It is an extra cost, but for a solo senior, a long-distance move, or a family coordinating from another city, it can be worth every dollar. Choosing the Right Movers for a Senior Move

Not all movers are equal, and seniors are targeted by the worst of them — the lowball quote that doubles on moving day, the "we need cash before we unload" crew. Here is my mover checklist for a senior move in Ottawa:

  • Get three written, in-home (not phone-only) estimates from movers who are insured and carry WSIB coverage.
  • Ask whether senior moves are a genuine specialty — the best crews know retirement-residence move-in rules, tight condo elevators, antiques, and pianos.
  • Check reviews under the company's legal name, not just the trading name.
  • Confirm what happens if they are late or damage something — get it in writing, not just a handshake.
  • Never pay more than a small deposit up front; the balance is due on delivery.

Sell First or Buy First?

This is the question that decides whether your downsize feels calm or chaotic. Selling first means you know exactly what you have to spend — but you may need a short-term rental or a bridge arrangement between closings. Buying first means one move instead of two — but you carry two homes if your sale stalls.

There is no universal right answer; it depends on your equity, your tolerance for interim moves, and the market for your current home. One cost many downsizers miss in this math: you will pay Ontario land transfer tax in full on the buy leg — there is no senior discount — so budget it into your buy-first math from day one.

Choosing Your Next Home in Ottawa

Ottawa seniors choosing a downsize usually land in one of four buckets: a smaller low-maintenance house or bungalow, a condominium with no exterior upkeep, a 55+ adult lifestyle community, or a retirement residence with meals and care available. Each has real trade-offs — a bungalow keeps your independence but not your snow shovelling, a condo kills the yard work but adds monthly fees, and a retirement residence buys peace of mind at a monthly cost that surprises many first-time researchers.

Frequently Asked Questions About Senior Moves in Ottawa

When should a senior start planning a downsizing move in Ottawa?

Start three to six months before your target move date. That gives you time to declutter properly, choose your next home without pressure, and book movers before the busy May, June, and September slots fill up. Rushed moves — under a month — almost always cost more and feel worse.

What is the best month to move in Ottawa as a senior?

Mid-month dates in April, October, and November are usually cheapest and easiest to book, because movers are quietest then. Avoid Canada Day and Labour Day long weekends if you can — demand and prices peak around them.

How do I choose a trustworthy moving company in Ottawa?

Get three written in-home estimates, confirm the mover is insured with WSIB coverage, check reviews under the company's legal name, and never pay more than a small deposit before moving day. Ask specifically whether senior moves are a genuine specialty — and register for my Senior Resources Gift for the vetted directory of Ottawa movers with real senior-move experience.

What is a senior move manager, and is it worth the cost?

A senior move manager coordinates the entire transition — decluttering, donations, packing, supervising movers, unpacking, and setting up the new home. For a solo senior or a family coordinating from another city, it is often worth every dollar. Should I sell my home before buying a smaller one?

Selling first gives you certainty about your budget but may mean a temporary move; buying first means one move but carries two homes if the sale stalls. Your equity, timeline, and the market for your current home decide it — and remember that Ontario land transfer tax is due in full on the buy leg either way, with no senior discount.

What should I do with furniture and belongings that will not fit in my new home?

Sort into keep, gift to family, donate, and sell. For the sell pile, Ottawa has on-site estate-sale companies and online estate auctioneers who will photograph, catalogue, and auction your household contents from your home — some with no upfront costs.

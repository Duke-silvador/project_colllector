Two pockets, two rivers

Barrhaven East is everything in Barrhaven between Woodroffe Avenue and the Rideau River, and it divides around Chapman Mills Drive. The western pocket, Chapman Mills, grew from the late 1990s around Chapman Mills Drive and Leamington Way — Minto rows, semis and detached singles. The eastern pocket, Davidson Heights, runs toward the water around Stoneway Drive and the Clarington Avenue crescents — detached and bungalow stock on streets that grow larger as they run east. The Jock River crosses the southern streets on its way to the Rideau, and the river itself is the eastern boundary: the streets that front it — Winding Way and Tradewinds Drive — are the neighbourhood’s top tier, with deep lots and treed banks. Two rivers at its edges and the widest spread of freehold homes in Barrhaven: that is the Barrhaven East signature.

Schools in Barrhaven East

The school grid is this community’s signature strength: Barrhaven East has a public elementary school in all four boards inside its own boundaries — a bench no other Barrhaven sub-area matches. In the public board, Chapman Mills Public School on Leamington Way serves Chapman Mills, and Adrienne Clarkson Elementary School on Stoneway Drive serves Davidson Heights; both feed Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School for grades 7–12. Catholic elementary schools sit inside both pockets. The French Catholic board has École élémentaire catholique Jean-Robert-Gauthier on Penfield Drive — it opened in 1999 as the first French-language school in Barrhaven — and the French public board has École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean on Clarington Avenue, with École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois just outside the boundary on the Chapman Mills corridor. One caution that matters: the splits here run by pocket, so your exact school still follows your exact address — always confirm on the board locator before you buy.

Parks and the rivers

Berry Glen Park is the Davidson Heights neighbourhood park — playground, splash pad, basketball court and an open green, signed in both official languages and busy from the first warm weekend. The Chapman Mills side answers with pocket parks along its crescents, and both pockets plug into the path network that runs to the water. Where the Jock meets the Rideau on Prince of Wales Drive, Jock River Landing is the neighbourhood’s launch point: canoe and kayak launch, dock and fishing spots where the two rivers join. The estates front the water; the landing is where everyone else gets on it.

Shopping and daily life

There is no large-format retail inside the pockets themselves — Barrhaven East is settled residential streets, and residents treat that as a feature. Daily life runs at the edges: neighbourhood-scale plazas and services along Chapman Mills Drive carry groceries, pharmacies and takeout at the scale the streets were built for, and the full Barrhaven retail spine — Barrhaven Town Centre and the Marketplace district — sits a few minutes west. For getting around, frequent buses run along Chapman Mills Drive toward Barrhaven Town Centre and Fallowfield Station, Beatrice Station serves the corridor, and the Nepean Woods Park & Ride at Woodroffe and Strandherd is minutes away. Prince of Wales Drive runs straight downtown along the river — one of Ottawa’s most scenic commutes — and to the south, the Vimy Memorial Bridge carries Strandherd Drive across the Rideau to Riverside South.

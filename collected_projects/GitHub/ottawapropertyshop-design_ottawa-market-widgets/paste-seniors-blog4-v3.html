The Land Transfer Tax Trap for Downsizing Seniors in Ontario

Downsizing is supposed to simplify your life: sell the big family home, buy something smaller, pocket the difference. But on the buy leg of that move, Ontario charges a tax that catches downsizing seniors off guard — land transfer tax. You probably paid it once, decades ago, and never thought about it again. Here is the trap in one sentence: you paid it when you bought your current home, and you will pay it fresh, in full, on the smaller home you move into. There is no senior discount and no downsizer rebate. On an $800,000 Ottawa bungalow the tax is $12,475, due in cash at closing, and it cannot be rolled into your mortgage.

If you are buying and selling real estate in Ottawa in the same move, this is the tax to understand first. This article explains how the tax works, shows the exact math, and explains why the province's only rebate excludes seniors who have owned before. It is general information, not tax or legal advice — talk to your real estate lawyer and your accountant about your own situation.

What Ontario Land Transfer Tax Is and When You Pay It

Land transfer tax is a provincial tax on the transfer of land ownership, paid by the buyer and collected by the buyer's lawyer when the deed is registered on closing day. The seller pays $0 — it is the buyer's tax, full stop.

That is where the trap hides for downsizers, who are both seller and buyer. The sell leg costs no land transfer tax; the buy leg costs the full amount on the price of the smaller home. Moving "down" does not move the tax to zero — it only shrinks it to the tax on the cheaper price. A four-figure bill on the buy leg is the normal outcome.

Ontario Land Transfer Tax Rates: The Marginal Brackets

Ontario’s land transfer tax rates are unchanged from the 2017 rules — no recent Ontario budget altered the provincial brackets. The rates are marginal: each rate applies only to the slice of the price inside its band.

  • 0.5% on the first $55,000
  • 1.0% on $55,000.01 to $250,000
  • 1.5% on $250,000.01 to $400,000
  • 2.0% on $400,000.01 to $2,000,000
  • 2.5% above $2,000,000 (land with one or two single-family residences)

Any reputable land transfer tax Ontario calculator uses these exact brackets — run the numbers yourself and you will land on the same totals shown below. Before you make an offer, plug your target price into a land transfer tax Ontario calculator so the closing-day cash figure is never a surprise.

The Math: How Much Land Transfer Tax Ottawa Buyers Pay on $800,000

Ottawa has no municipal land transfer tax, so the provincial calculation is the entire bill. Here is the slice-by-slice answer to how much land transfer tax Ottawa buyers pay on an $800,000 home:

  • $55,000 × 0.5% = $275
  • $195,000 × 1.0% = $1,950
  • $150,000 × 1.5% = $2,250
  • $400,000 × 2.0% = $8,000
  • Total: $12,475

That is about 1.56% of the purchase price — one line on a closing statement that also carries legal fees, title insurance, and moving costs. On a fixed retirement income, $12,475 of after-tax cash deserves a line in your downsizing budget from day one, not a surprise at the lawyer's office.

Why Downsizers Get No Rebate

You bought your family home years ago and paid the tax then. Now you buy again — smaller — and pay it again. Two purchases, two tax bills. Ontario offers no credit for tax already paid and no age-based relief. The one exception: if you inherited your current home rather than buying it, you never paid the tax on it, since only purchases trigger it. The First Time Home Buyer Land Transfer Tax Rebate Ontario Offers — and Why It Excludes You

Ontario's only land transfer tax refund is the first-time homebuyer refund: up to $4,000, covering the tax on the first $368,000 of the price. Downsizing seniors are excluded by definition — you have owned before, and that alone disqualifies you.

The province's eligibility wording is strict and worth quoting exactly as ontario.ca states it: "To claim a refund, you must be at least 18 years of age, you cannot have owned a home or an interest in a home anywhere in the world, and your spouse cannot have owned a home or interest in a home, anywhere in the world while he or she was your spouse… The method of acquiring the home (e.g., purchase, gift or through an inheritance) is not relevant."

Three details matter for downsizing couples. The spouse rule: if your spouse owned a home while they were your spouse, both of you are disqualified. Anywhere in the world: a home owned decades ago in another country counts — purchase, gift, inheritance, or partial interest. You cannot re-qualify: the province states this explicitly, and warns its definition "may be different from other federal programs for first-time homebuyers (e.g., the Canada Revenue Agency Home Buyers' Plan)." The remaining conditions — principal-residence occupancy within 9 months, Canadian citizen or permanent resident, application within 18 months of registration — are straightforward. The ownership-history test is the wall. Plan on paying the full amount, and confirm it with your lawyer.

Ottawa vs Toronto: No Municipal Tax in Ottawa

Toronto is the only Ontario municipality with its own municipal land transfer tax, charged on top of the provincial tax. Ottawa has none — an Ottawa buyer's bill is the provincial calculation only, which is why the $800,000 example above is the complete story here. Toronto's municipal tax changes do not affect Ottawa purchases.

The Real Trap: Cash Due at Closing

The number is half the trap; the timing is the other half. Land transfer tax is collected by your lawyer on closing day and cannot be rolled into your mortgage — it must be cash, over and above your down payment. Buy an $800,000 bungalow with $400,000 down and you need the $400,000 plus $12,475 in tax plus legal fees and title insurance, all in certified funds, all at once. If your equity is still tied up in the home you are selling, that cash comes from savings or short-term borrowing. The land transfer tax downsizing seniors Ontario buyers face is pure out-of-pocket money — build it into the budget before you start browsing Ottawa houses for sale, not after you fall in love with one.

Price-Point Awareness: The $400,000 Bracket Line

Every dollar above $400,000 is taxed at 2% instead of 1.5%, which makes $400,000 the most useful threshold for a downsizer to know:

  • $395,000 purchase: $4,400 in land transfer tax
  • $405,000 purchase: $4,575 in land transfer tax

The $10,000 pricier home costs $175 more in tax, and the gap widens as prices climb — the 2% band runs to $2,000,000. Not a reason to buy a home you like less, but a legitimate data point when comparing two similar options. A REALTOR® who works with downsizers runs these numbers with you before you write the offer.

Timing the Move: Sell First, Buy First, or Bridge

The tax is due on the buy leg whatever order you move in — but the order changes your cash position when it comes due. Sell first: budget certainty, proceeds in hand, but possible interim housing. Buy first: one direct move, but two properties at once and possibly bridge financing around prime plus 2–3%. Same-day closings: neither interim housing nor bridge financing, but no room for delays. What to Ask Your Lawyer Before You Downsize

Your real estate lawyer calculates, collects, and remits the tax — bring these questions to your first meeting:

  • What is the exact land transfer tax on my target purchase price?
  • Am I eligible for any rebate? (Expect no — have them confirm why.)
  • How does the tax appear on my closing statement, and in what form are funds due?
  • If I buy before I sell, how does bridge financing interact with closing funds?
  • Any title or ownership-structure issues, such as joint tenancy, affecting registration?

Frequently Asked Questions

Do seniors get a land transfer tax discount in Ontario?

No. Ontario has no senior-specific exemption, reduction, or rebate. Downsizers pay the full marginal rates, and the first time home buyer land transfer tax rebate Ontario offers explicitly excludes anyone who has owned a home before. Confirm your situation with your real estate lawyer and accountant.

Do I pay land transfer tax when I sell my home?

No. The tax is always paid by the buyer, never the seller — the sell leg of your downsize is $0. The bill arrives on the buy leg, which is why sellers who focus only on their proceeds get surprised at closing.

Can I add land transfer tax to my mortgage?

No. It cannot be rolled into your mortgage; your lawyer collects it in cash on closing day, over and above your down payment. On $800,000 that is $12,475 in certified funds on top of everything else.

My spouse owned a home before we married. Can I claim the first-time buyer refund?

No. If your spouse owned a home or an interest in a home while they were your spouse, you are both disqualified — ownership anywhere in the world, by purchase, gift, inheritance, or partial interest, all count, and you cannot re-qualify.

Is there a municipal land transfer tax in Ottawa?

No. Toronto is the only Ontario municipality with one. Ottawa buyers pay only the provincial tax on the marginal brackets above.

This week in Cedargrove

Homes for sale: 21 active listings. That is up from the 18 in my Cedargrove guide's October 7 snapshot — the shelf grew by three in an established pocket where new resale supply normally arrives one household move at a time.

Median asking price: $679,000. Against the guide's median of $661,950, that is a rise of $17,050 in three weeks — about two and a half per cent.

Average asking price: $770,576. The average is up $26,782 from the guide's $743,794 — about three and a half per cent — and it sits well above the median. As the guide noted back on October 7, that gap is the top of the set pulling the average up; this month the pull is stronger.

The range: $464,000 to $1,365,000. Here is the shape of the change: the entry point essentially held — the low end sits within $5,000 of the guide's $469,000 floor — while the ceiling stretched $125,000 past the guide's $1,240,000 top. The movement is concentrated in the middle and at the top, not at the entry.

The Barrhaven picture: Barrhaven-wide, 249 freehold homes are listed today, and Cedargrove's 21 make it one of the smaller established pockets in the count. Its $679,000 median sits $50,000 above the Barrhaven-wide median of $629,000 — and that gap is a mix story, not a verdict on value: Cedargrove's set is detached-heavy, built mostly in the 1980s and 1990s on mature lots, while the Barrhaven-wide figures blend in every pocket and product type, condo-heavy areas and brand-new subdivisions included.

What I make of it: three more homes, a median up $17,050 and a ceiling up $125,000 says the listings that joined this set since October 7 sit in the middle and at the top — the renovated detached end of the pocket is where the new asking prices are landing. The floor holding near the guide's level says the entry tier — the townhouses and the low-rise condos at Silhouette Private — did not reprice with them. What the aggregates cannot tell us is whether the listings that left in those three weeks sold, expired or were withdrawn; three net additions also means some homes did leave the shelf. If you are buying in Cedargrove, the guide's standing advice still applies: these are 30-to-40-year-old houses, so budget for the inspection to matter — roof, foundation and mechanicals — and note that four-bedroom detached homes draw the most competition here. If you are selling, a fuller shelf than October 7 means your home is compared against more of its neighbours than it would have been three weeks ago.

Established streets, one new edge

Cedargrove and Fraserdale were built mostly through the 1980s and 1990s, and the pocket still reads that way: detached homes — many of them Tartan-built — on mature streets around Cedarview Road and Jockvale Road, with townhouses mixed in and the low-rise condos and stacked towns at Silhouette Private forming the entry tier. Renovated examples are common, and the mature tree canopy is a big part of the draw — this is the Barrhaven that was finished before the building boom pushed south. The one new edge is at the pocket's southern boundary, where a proposed Minto development of 266 dwellings along Jockvale Road is set to add new homes and, in time, a linear greenspace along the corridor.

Getting around from Cedargrove

Transit is one of this pocket's quiet strengths. OC Transpo's route 173 (Bayshore to Barrhaven Centre) runs the Jockvale, Tartan and Strandherd corridors, with stops at Jockvale and Weybridge, Tartan and Jockvale, and Strandherd and Jockvale, and route 99 runs along Strandherd Drive. The Strandherd Transitway station — pictured above and below — anchors the pocket's north edge for connexion and rapid routes toward Tunney's Pasture, including the weekday peak-period route 264, with school routes 673 and 699 serving the local schools. Off-peak, the drive downtown runs about 25 to 30 minutes.

Schools around Cedargrove

Families here draw on a deep bench across the boards: Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School for grades 7 to 12, Cedarview Middle School, Berrigan Elementary and Chapman Mills Public School on the public side, St. Luke Catholic Elementary, and École élémentaire catholique Pierre-Elliott-Trudeau for French Catholic families. Catchments turn on the exact street, so check yours on each board's school-locator tool before you buy — my Cedargrove guide walks through the full bench, board by board.

Parks, play and daily life

The honest version, as my guide puts it: no named park sits inside the pocket itself. What Cedargrove offers instead is the mature green of its own streets today, and the future Jockvale Road linear greenspace at its southern edge tomorrow. The big recreation draw is a short drive south: the Minto Recreation Complex—Barrhaven on Cambrian Road, with its 25-metre pool, two NHL-size rinks, gymnasium and indoor walking track. Daily life runs along the Strandherd corridor — the auto-services cluster at the pocket's north end, the cinema on Riocan Avenue — while the full grocery and big-box run stays a short drive away at Barrhaven Town Centre and Marketplace, since no large-format retail sits inside the pocket.

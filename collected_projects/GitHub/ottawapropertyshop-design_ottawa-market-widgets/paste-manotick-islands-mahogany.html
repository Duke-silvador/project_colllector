Long Island & Nicholls Island — the Island Neighbourhoods (8001)

West of the village core, the Rideau splits around two islands that form their own neighbourhood — Long Island and Nicholls Island (8001). Long Island is the island Dickinson named the village for: bridge-accessed — Bridge Street crosses the dam at the mill and becomes Long Island Road — tree-canopied, and lined with waterfront homes whose backyards are the river itself: docks, launches and sunset water. Its own elementary school, St. Leonard Catholic School, stands on Long Island Road, and David Bartlett Park gives the island its green centre, with river access and the dog park. Nicholls Island, smaller and quieter, sits just north-west of the Long Island locks and carries the same waterfront logic in a more tucked-away register.

Island homes trade on water frontage and privacy. The standing homework is wells, septic systems and shoreline allowances — the strip of shore road allowance that can sit between a lot line and the water on older island surveys — plus the flood and access questions any riverfront file deserves. None of it is a reason to stay away; all of it is a reason to buy with the file open. The right island property rarely waits long for its buyer.

Mahogany — Minto’s New-Home Community (8003)

South of the village core, Mahogany (8003) is Minto Communities’ master-planned community — the new-home face of Manotick. Mahogany is built around Mahogany Pond, its parks and a protected woodlot: singles, bungalows and townhomes released in phases. Its harbour streets — Mahogany Harbour Lane, Coast Circle, Chriscraft Way, Yacht Terrace — carry a boating theme into the village’s south side, and the Ascari, Apron and Beachburg streets fill in the subdivision behind them, down to Century Road.

The Manotick Walk pathway ties the community into the village, which is the whole proposition: new-home construction and a builder warranty with an 1860 mill village as your downtown — Main Street’s shops, the river and Dickinson Square are a short walk away. Families weighing Mahogany against a village or island resale are really choosing between a warranty and a heritage street; this group prices both, side by side.

Living Between the River and the Village — Schools, Parks and the Drive

Schools are the organising spine for most families here, and this side of Manotick holds a rare card: an elementary school standing on the island itself. The standing warning applies — catchments are set by your exact home address and program, so confirm any specific property in the board’s school locator before you buy.

  • St. Leonard Catholic School (OCSB, Junior Kindergarten–Grade 6) — 5344 Long Island Road. The Catholic elementary on Long Island itself — over 500 students, in English and French.

  • Manotick Public School (OCDSB, Junior Kindergarten–Grade 5) — 1075 Bridge Street. The village’s public elementary, teaching in English and French, in the middle of the village both areas lean on.

  • St. Mark Catholic High School (OCSB, Grades 7–12) — 1040 Dozois Road, in Manotick East. The Catholic secondary for the whole Manotick group, in English and French.

  • Kars on the Rideau Public School (OCDSB) — in Kars, south on the river. The area’s public middle school — French programming from Kindergarten and English/French through the middle grades.

  • South Carleton High School (OCDSB, Grades 9–12) — in Richmond. The public secondary for the region, drawing over 1,300 students from Manotick, Kars, North Gower and the surrounding countryside.

  • French-language boards (CEPEO / CECCE) — no French-language school sits in Manotick itself; French-board catchments follow their own sector maps, so confirm your exact address in the French boards’ locators.

For daily life: David Bartlett Park gives the islands their green centre — river access, open field and the dog park — while Mahogany answers with its pond, its parks and the Manotick Walk into the village. For the drive, Rideau Valley Drive runs north to Highway 416 in about ten minutes, and downtown is roughly half an hour outside rush hour. Transit is thin, and honest planning matters: OC Transpo’s Route 186 runs a limited number of trips linking the village toward Barrhaven and the wider network, so both areas are driving neighbourhoods — and the islands add one more truth: everything comes and goes over the bridges.

Questions Buyers Ask About the Islands and Mahogany

 

Are Long Island and Nicholls Island really islands?

 

Yes — the Rideau River splits around them beside the village. Long Island is reached by road over the dam at Watson’s Mill, where Bridge Street becomes Long Island Road; Nicholls Island, the smaller and quieter of the two, sits just north-west of the Long Island locks. There is no ferry and no seasonal access question — the bridges are ordinary city streets — but every trip on or off the islands crosses the river, which is exactly the point of living there.

 

What services do island homes use?

 

Island homes typically run on private wells and septic systems, and the file deserves the standard rural homework done properly: well water quality and quantity, septic age and capacity, and the shoreline road allowance question — on older island surveys a strip of shore allowance can sit between the lot line and the water, which matters for docks and boathouses. None of it is a reason to stay away; all of it is a reason to buy with the paperwork open.

 

Who builds in Mahogany, and what kinds of homes?

 

Mahogany is Minto Communities’ master-planned community, built in phases around Mahogany Pond, its parks and a protected woodlot. The housing runs to singles, bungalows and townhomes — including the harbour streets by the pond — and new homes carry Ontario’s new-home warranty protection. Resale homes in the earlier phases trade alongside the builder’s current releases, so a Mahogany search is always part builder price list, part resale market.

 

Can I walk from Mahogany to Manotick Village?

 

Yes — the Manotick Walk pathway ties the community into the village, and Main Street’s shops, cafés and the river at Dickinson Square are a short walk from Mahogany’s streets. That walkability is the community’s whole proposition: a new home with an 1860 mill village as its downtown.

 

Which schools serve the islands and Mahogany?

 

St. Leonard Catholic School stands on Long Island Road itself (JK–Grade 6), and Manotick Public School serves the village at 1075 Bridge Street (JK–Grade 5). Catholic secondary is St. Mark in Manotick East (Grades 7–12); the public path runs through Kars on the Rideau for the middle grades and South Carleton High School in Richmond for Grades 9–12. Catchments follow your exact address and program — confirm in the board’s school locator before you buy.

 

Why do so few island homes come up for sale?

 

Geography. The islands hold a fixed set of streets — Bravar Drive, McLean Crescent, Driscoll Drive, Long Island Road and Nicolls Island Road — and no new supply is being built. Island homes change hands when a family’s life changes, not when a builder releases a phase, so the island market moves in single listings rather than waves. Buyers who want the water register an alert and stay ready.

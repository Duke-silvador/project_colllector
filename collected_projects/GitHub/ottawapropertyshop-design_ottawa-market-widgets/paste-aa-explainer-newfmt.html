What Revenue Share Actually Is (and Is Not)

Every brokerage takes a cut of your commission. At eXp Realty in Canada, that cut is 20% of each deal until you have paid $16,000 in your anniversary year — then you keep 100%. That 20% is called the company dollar, and it is the fuel for everything below.

eXp Realty operates in 29 countries, and the revenue-share mechanics work the same way in each — the figures below use the Canadian schedule, the same numbers an Ontario agent works with.

Here is what makes eXp different from every traditional brokerage: instead of keeping the whole company dollar, eXp gives up to half of it back to agents — specifically, to the agents who introduced productive agents to the company. When you sponsor an agent into eXp (you are the person who brought them in) and that agent closes deals, eXp pays you a percentage of the company dollar generated by their transactions.

The three facts that matter

1. It costs the producing agent nothing. The agent who closed the deal keeps their exact same 80/20 split (or 100% post-cap) whether someone sponsored them or not. Revenue share is paid from eXp's share, never from the agent's commission.

2. It only exists if the agents you attract actually sell homes. No production, no revenue share. It is transaction-backed, not a sign-up bonus.

3. It compounds through seven tiers. You earn on agents you sponsored directly, and smaller amounts on agents they sponsor, down seven levels.

What it is not: it is not a fee, not a tax on anyone's commission, and not money that appears from nowhere — it is eXp voluntarily sharing its own revenue with the agents who grow the company. Since 2015, eXp has paid more than $1 billion in cumulative revenue share to its U.S. agent community, including over $160 million in 2025 alone. That is real money — but as the honest section below shows, it does not flow to everyone equally.

Worked Dollar Examples: One Deal, Five Agents, Ten Agents

Percentages are abstract. Here is what they mean in dollars, using simple round numbers. All examples are illustrations, not promises — real payouts follow eXp's monthly pool mechanics.

Example 1 — A single deal (Tier 1)

You sponsor an agent. They sell a $500,000 home and earn a $15,000 commission. Your Tier 1 payout: 3.5% × $15,000 = $525 — paid to you by eXp, from eXp's share. The agent keeps their full commission. If that agent closes 20 such deals in a year ($300,000 GCI), your Tier 1 earnings from them would be 3.5% × $300,000 = $10,500 — but the published maximum caps it at $2,800/year for that agent.

Example 2 — Five productive frontline agents (illustrative)

You sponsor 5 agents; each does $60,000 in annual GCI. Tier 1 alone: 5 × 3.5% × $60,000 = $10,500/year. Add Tier 2 spillover as those agents attract a few agents of their own, and a realistic modeled range is roughly $12,000–$20,000/year in revenue share — on top of your own sales commissions.

Example 3 — Ten productive frontline agents (illustrative)

You sponsor 10 agents at $60,000 GCI each. Tier 1: 10 × 3.5% × $60,000 = $21,000/year. With Tier 2 and Tier 3 depth developing beneath them, modeled totals land roughly in the $25,000–$40,000/year range. At this point revenue share starts to look like a second income — and it keeps paying every year those agents keep producing, whether you personally sell another house or not.

The compounding insight: Tier 1 rewards you for the agents you know. Tiers 2–7 reward you for building a culture where your agents attract agents. That only happens with mentorship — which is exactly what a sponsor choice is really about. More on that below.

FLQAs: The Key That Unlocks Deeper Tiers

FLQA stands for Front Line Qualifying Agent: an agent you personally sponsored who has generated at least $5,000 in gross commission income in the past 6 months. In plain English — a producing agent you brought in, not just a name on a list.

Your FLQA count is the key that unlocks Tiers 4 through 7:

  • 5+ FLQAs unlock Tier 4 (up to $1,200/agent/year)

  • 10+ FLQAs unlock Tier 5 (up to $800/agent/year)

  • 15+ FLQAs unlock Tier 6 (up to $2,000/agent/year)

  • 30+ FLQAs unlock Tier 7 (up to $4,000/agent/year)

This is the part most hype videos skip: deep tiers are not automatic. They are earned by attracting productive agents — which means your sponsor's ability to help you attract and mentor agents is the whole game. A sponsor who hands you a link and disappears has given you Tiers 1–3 and nothing more.

eXpansion Share vs. eXponential Share

Two terms you will see on the official chart, explained simply:

  • eXpansion share — a small payout on tiers you have not unlocked yet. Think of it as eXp's way of paying you something while you are still building toward the FLQA thresholds. The percentages are small by design.

  • eXponential share — the full published percentage (the 3.5%, 4%, 2.5% figures in the table above) paid once a tier is unlocked. This is where the real money lives.

In practice: everyone starts with eXponential share on Tiers 1–3 (auto-unlocked since REVenue Share 2.0 in 2024) and eXpansion share on the deeper tiers, graduating to full eXponential payouts as FLQA counts grow. Check the current official chart for the exact eXpansion micro-percentages — they are small, and they are not the point. The point is unlocking.

What Happens When Your Sponsored Agent Caps

Canada's annual cap is $16,000 — reached at $80,000 in gross commission income. When an agent you sponsored hits their cap, two things happen:

  • They keep 100% of every commission for the rest of their anniversary year (minus the $29 broker-review fee per transaction).

  • Your tier payouts from that agent pause — because revenue share is calculated on the company dollar, and a capped agent is not generating company dollar until their anniversary reset.

This is a feature, not a bug: capped agents are your most productive people, and the pause is temporary — payouts resume at their anniversary reset. It also explains the per-agent annual maximums in the table: they exist precisely because payouts pause at the cap. A deep organization of many producing agents smooths this out — while one agent is capped, others are still generating.

Revenue share flows only to agents who attract productive agents. The real question is not "what does the median agent earn" — it is "will I be set up to be in the attracting minority?" That is a sponsor question, not a chart question.

Revenue Share FAQs

What is eXp Realty revenue share?

It is eXp sharing up to 50% of its 20% company-dollar cut with agents who attracted productive agents to the brokerage. When an agent you sponsored closes a deal, eXp pays you a percentage of its own share — the producing agent's split is completely unaffected.

How much can I earn per tier?

Published maximums per capping agent per year: Tier 1 $2,800 (3.5% of GCI), Tier 2 $3,200 (4% of AGCI), Tier 3 $2,000 (2.5%), Tier 4 $1,200 (1.5%), Tier 5 $800 (1%), Tier 6 $2,000 (2.5%), Tier 7 $4,000 (5.0%). Re-verify against the current official chart before relying on these figures.

What is an FLQA?

A Front Line Qualifying Agent — an agent you personally sponsored who generated at least $5,000 in gross commission income in the past 6 months. FLQA counts unlock Tiers 4–7 (5, 10, 15, and 30 FLQAs respectively). Tiers 1–3 are auto-unlocked since REVenue Share 2.0 (2024).

Does revenue share cost the producing agent anything?

Nothing. Zero. The agent who closed the deal keeps their exact same split whether they were sponsored by someone or joined on their own. Revenue share comes out of eXp's 20% company dollar, never out of agent commissions.

What happens to my revenue share when my sponsored agent hits their cap?

Payouts from that agent pause until their anniversary-year reset, because a capped agent is not generating company dollar. In Canada the cap is $16,000 (reached at $80,000 GCI). A deep organization of many agents smooths this out.

What is the difference between eXpansion share and eXponential share?

eXpansion share is a small payout on tiers you have not unlocked yet; eXponential share is the full published percentage once a tier is unlocked. Everyone starts with eXponential on Tiers 1–3 (auto-unlocked) and graduates upward as FLQA counts grow.

Is eXp revenue share willable?

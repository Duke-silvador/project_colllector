This week in Hearts Desire

Homes for sale: 2 active listings. The shelf halved — my Hearts Desire guide recorded four active listings on October 13, and one of the four was a vacant estate lot. What is left is two detached estate homes. In an enclave where the guide's research counted just 17 sales across a full year, a two-listing shelf is not a dip; it is close to this pocket's normal state, and it is the thinnest shelf this series has recorded.

Median asking price: $1,162,400. With two listings, the median is simply the midpoint of the pair. It sits $137,450 above the guide's median of $1,024,950 — a little over thirteen per cent — and that is arithmetic, not momentum: the vacant lot and the lower-priced house left the shelf, so the middle moved up. It is not evidence that any home's price rose.

Average asking price: $1,162,400. Identical to the median, because with two listings the average and the midpoint are the same number. The average is up $131,230 from the guide's $1,031,170 — a little under thirteen per cent — for the same mix reason as the median.

The range: $924,900 to $1,399,900. The floor rose $250,020 from $674,880 — the guide's entry point was the vacant estate lot itself, and no lot is listed today. The top of the range held to the dollar at $1,399,900.

The Barrhaven picture: Barrhaven-wide, 247 freehold homes are listed today, and Hearts Desire's two are the smallest pocket count in that total. Its $1,162,400 median sits $537,400 above the Barrhaven-wide median of $625,000 — the enclave premium, and again a mix story: custom detached homes on oversized lots beside a river, against a suburb-wide figure that blends in every pocket and product type.

What I make of it: two listings is not a market index — a single arrival or departure rewrites every number on this page, and the aggregates cannot tell us whether individual listings sold, expired or were withdrawn along the way. If you are buying in Hearts Desire, the work is in the inspection: this is 1970s-to-2000s custom stock, so roof, foundation, furnace and windows matter, and on the homes that run on a private well and septic system, the potability test and septic inspection matter just as much. If you are selling here, yours would be one of two choices in a built-out enclave with no new subdivision coming behind it — and the buyers for these streets have usually been waiting for months.

Estate lots, mature trees and a river

Hearts Desire was built from the 1970s to the early 2000s, and it still reads that way: custom detached two-storeys, bungalows and sidesplits — many of them one-off builds rather than production models — on oversized lots, some approaching half an acre, under mature maples. The streets are Barcham Crescent, Bren-Maur Road, Ryerson Avenue, Cortleigh Drive, Marjan Crescent, Newland Drive and Howard Court, laid out with no through traffic by design. There are no townhomes, no semis and no condos in the enclave stock; the low-density character is protected by zoning. Because some streets predate full municipal servicing, a share of homes run on a private well and septic system — a normal, inspectable part of buying here.

And no other Barrhaven pocket has a south edge like this one. The Jock River — a 72-kilometre tributary of the Rideau — runs along the bottom of the enclave, and Heart's Desire Park puts roughly 32 acres of mature maple forest, walking trails and an off-leash dog park between the streets and the water. The Jock River Landing paddle launch sits just across Prince of Wales Drive. In most of Barrhaven the river is a drive away; here it is the end of the street.

Getting around from Hearts Desire

By car, Prince of Wales Drive and Greenbank Road run north to the core in about 20 minutes off-peak, Strandherd Drive heads west to Highway 416 for Kanata and the 417, and the airport runs about 20 minutes via Prince of Wales Drive. This is a driver's enclave, honestly stated — most households here plan around two cars.

On transit, there is no station inside the enclave, and that is part of its quiet — but the service runs closer than most people think. Route 70 travels Strandherd Drive and Woodroffe Avenue, the enclave's own north and east edges, on its way between Fallowfield Station and Limebank Station on Line 2 of the O-Train, and routes 99 and 110 also run along Strandherd Drive. For drivers who go partway, Strandherd Station sits minutes north: its park and ride holds 336 spaces, and OC Transpo built it for the residents of the surrounding pockets — Heart's Desire among them, by name. The station is served by routes 70, 75, 80, 275, 279 and 305, plus school routes 671, 672, 677 and 690.

Schools around Hearts Desire

No school building sits inside the enclave — the lots are too big and the streets too few. The bench that serves it stands around its edges, across all four boards, and more than anywhere else in Barrhaven, the exact street decides:

  • Public English (OCDSB): catchments split the enclave street by street across the wider Barrhaven roster. The suburb's public secondaries are John McCrae Secondary School (grades 9 to 12) and Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School (grades 7 to 12) — which one an address feeds, and which elementary school comes first, is a locator question, not a neighbourhood one.

  • Catholic English (OCSB): St. Joseph High School (grades 7 to 12) stands on Greenbank Road at the enclave's eastern edge — the school in the banner above. Since September 2024, the board's line runs along Greenbank Road: addresses east of it feed St. Joseph, while addresses west of Greenbank Road and north of the Jock River were redirected to St. Mother Teresa High School (grades 7 to 12). The board's feeder elementary schools for St. Joseph are St. Emily, St. Benedict, St. Cecilia and St. Juan Diego; for St. Mother Teresa, St. Patrick, St. Elizabeth Ann Seton, St. Luke Nepean, St. Andrew and Monsignor Paul Baxter.

  • French Catholic (CECCE): École élémentaire catholique Jean-Robert-Gauthier (kindergarten to grade 6), then École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard (grades 7 to 12) — both serve the board's Barrhaven sector, which covers this enclave.

  • French public (CEPEO): École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean (kindergarten to grade 8) — Barrhaven's only French public elementary school — then École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois (grades 7 to 12), which runs the International Baccalaureate program.

  • Private: Ottawa Christian School (junior kindergarten to grade 8) on Tartan Drive, west of the enclave — and the Sunday meeting place of Sequoia Community Church.

Catchments turn on the exact street — in this enclave, sometimes on which side of Greenbank Road the street sits — so check yours on each board's school-locator tool before you buy. My Hearts Desire guide points you to the same locators.

Parks, play and daily life

The enclave's own green is Heart's Desire Park on Woodroffe Avenue: roughly 32 acres of mature maple forest with walking trails, an off-leash dog park and the Jock River along its edge. Inside the streets, Barcham Park on Bren-Maur Road — often called the prettiest park in Barrhaven — adds a playground, picnic sites and benches under the trees, and Davidson Park on Ryerson Avenue carries a sports field, a playground and two lit tennis courts. The big indoor draw is minutes north: the Walter Baker Sports Centre on Malvern Drive, with two ice rinks, a 25-metre lap pool with a dive tank and teaching pools, a rock wall, gyms and squash courts — plus the Ruth E. Dickinson branch of the Ottawa Public Library under the same roof.

Churches, plazas and the daily run

Faith life sits at the enclave's edges, like everything else here: St. Andrew Parish celebrates Mass at St. Joseph High School on Greenbank Road, Barrhaven United Church and Barrhaven Fellowship Christian Reformed Church stand on Jockvale Road west of the enclave, and Sequoia Community Church meets on Sundays at Ottawa Christian School on Tartan Drive. No church — and no store — sits inside the enclave itself; it is strictly residential by design.

The daily run is a short drive, not a drawback: at the enclave's northeast corner, Chapman Mills Marketplace on Strandherd Drive at Woodroffe Avenue carries 66 stores, including Loblaws, Walmart Supercentre, Home Depot, Shoppers Drug Mart, Winners and the Cineplex. The Metro and FreshCo on Strandherd Drive are both under two kilometres from most enclave streets, and the Barrhaven Town Centre corridor is minutes farther west for everything else.

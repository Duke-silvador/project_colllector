Manotick South Homes for Sale in Ottawa

Manotick South is the rural half of the Manotick story — estate homes on big treed lots along the Kars shore of the Rideau River, farm-country roads on the west bank, and a land market that runs from single building lots to a large multi-parcel development assembly, all of it minutes south of Manotick village in Ottawa's rural south end (AMP district 8004, Manotick South to Roger Stevens).

The district has two halves. On the east bank, a lobe of shore country wraps around the village of Kars — Fairway Drive, Edgewater Drive, Woodeden Drive, Bunker Road, Marina Drive and River Road, running south to Rideau Valley Provincial Park — plus the Antochi Lane strip of estate lots just south of Bridge Street. On the west bank, the district is working farm country between Prince of Wales Drive and the river: Century Road at the top, Roger Stevens Drive at the bottom, with First Line Road and Carsonby Road running through it. This guide covers both halves: the live freehold market, what has actually sold, the land picture, the schools split, and the homework that comes with wells, septic systems and shoreline lots.

The Kars Shore and the Antochi Strip

The east bank is why most buyers come. South of Bridge Street, Antochi Lane carries a short strip of estate lots — and a 10.67-hectare assembly of future development land that is the district's long-term story. Below it, the shore roads run down the Kars side of the Rideau: the Fairway Drive crescents, Edgewater Drive, Woodeden Drive, Bunker Road, Marina Drive and River Road, with Island View Drive and James Bell Drive tucked into the trees. The lobe wraps around the village of Kars, which has its own guide in the table below, and ends at Rideau Valley Provincial Park, where there is a canoe and kayak launch and a picnic area on the river.

Shore living here is cottage-country texture inside the city: big lots, mature trees, docks and boathouses, and water on the West Branch of the Rideau. It also comes with the standard waterfront homework — shore road allowances on the older lots, private wells and septic systems, and a hard look at what the flood mapping says about the bank. The buyers who do that homework end up with the best lots on this stretch of the river, because the homework scares off the buyers who do not.

The West Bank — Century Road to Roger Stevens

West of the river, Manotick South is farm country that still works. Century Road runs along the district's north edge — its east end carries village-edge homes a few minutes from Bridge Street — and from there the concession roads run south: First Line Road, Carsonby Road East and Sutherland Drive, down to Roger Stevens Drive at the bottom. The roads mix village-edge houses, estate homes on acreage, and working farmland — farms have worked these concession roads for generations — and the Highway 416 interchange at Prince of Wales Drive puts downtown Ottawa about half an hour away.

Bridge Street at the south end of Manotick village — the gateway into Manotick South. Photo: Tomasz R. Witek.

What the west bank sells is space and quiet at a lower price per acre than the shore: room for a shop, a barn, horses or a home business, on roads where the traffic is tractors in seeding season. Services are private — well and septic again — and winter means your own snow clearing on a long driveway. For buyers leaving Barrhaven or Half Moon Bay who want land more than they want a subdivision, this is the closest true farm country to the village.

Land in Manotick South

Land is a market of its own in 8004. The parcels that come to market range from single building lots on roads like Sutherland Drive to the Antochi Lane assembly south of Bridge Street — a multi-parcel block of future development land. Between those poles, parcels turn over quietly: severances off farm lots, estate lots on the shore roads, acreage on the concessions. Every one needs its own homework: zoning and permitted uses, severance and frontage, a drilled well's flow before you count on it, septic feasibility, and hydro at the road. Living in Manotick South

Daily life runs on the village, five to ten minutes north: two grocery stores, pharmacies, banks, the shops and restaurants of Manotick Main Street, Watson's Mill and Dickinson Square, and A.Y. Jackson Park on the south bank at Bridge Street — the district's front step, with the mill and the dam across the water. Centennial Park, the arena and the tennis and pickleball courts are all in the village. There is no bus service down the concession roads — OC Transpo's Route 186 gives the village a limited service, and Manotick South is car country by definition.

A.Y. Jackson Park at 1102 Bridge Street, on the south bank at the edge of Manotick South. Photo: Tomasz R. Witek.

Schools split by bank — the detail is in the questions below:

  • Kars on the Rideau Public School, in Kars — the elementary school for Kars-shore families on the east bank.

  • Manotick Public School — the elementary school for west-bank families, in the village.

  • Half Moon Bay Public School — French Immersion for west-bank families.

  • St. Leonard School, in the village — Catholic elementary.

  • St. Mark Catholic High School, in the village, and South Carleton High School, in Richmond — the two high schools.

On the river, the district's recreation is the water itself: boating and paddling from the provincial park launch, fishing along the shore, and the Rideau's long reach south toward Kemptville. In winter it is snowmobile trails across the fields and the village arena up the road. This is the part of Manotick where the lot does the entertaining.

Churches in Manotick South

Church life splits by bank, exactly the way the schools do: shore families worship in Kars, in the village the district's east-bank lobe wraps around, and west-bank families run five to ten minutes north to the churches of Manotick. These are old congregations in old buildings — in both villages the church is among the oldest institutions standing, and parish life is still part of the weekly rhythm down here.

Kars keeps two churches of its own on Rideau Valley Drive South. St. John the Baptist Anglican Church (6699 Rideau Valley Drive South) was built in 1850 on land that is now Elmview Cemetery — and then it travelled: cross-braced in the winter of 1904–05, it was moved on skids over Stevens Creek to the site it occupies today, its 19th-century pine pews and floorboards still in place. Trinity United Church (6656 Rideau Valley Drive South) is the younger landmark, a rock-faced stone church in the Gothic Revival style built in 1894–95 to replace an earlier wooden church on the same grounds — its facing stone arrived, fittingly, by boat up the Rideau.

St. John the Baptist Anglican Church in Kars — built in 1850 and moved on skids over Stevens Creek to its present site in the winter of 1904–05. Photo: Tomasz R. Witek.

The west bank's churches are the village set in Manotick. St. Brigid Roman Catholic Church (2015 River Road) is the senior building of the whole area — its small stone church was blessed in 1858, and it is the oldest standing church in old Osgoode Township. Manotick United Church (5567 Manotick Main Street) opened its stone church in 1904, the oldest church structure in the village. St. James the Apostle Anglican Church (1138 Bridge Street) first opened for worship in 1877 and rose again on the same site in 1985, in the same Norman form, keeping its original stained glass. Knox Presbyterian Church (5533 Dickinson Street) opened in 1926, and St. Leonard Roman Catholic Church (5332 Long Island Road), the village's modern parish church, opened in 1981. Whichever bank a Manotick South family settles on, Sunday morning is a short drive — and the church at the end of it has usually been standing for well over a century.

Manotick South questions, answered

 

Where exactly is Manotick South?

 

Manotick South is AMP district 8004 — everything south of the Manotick village core down to Roger Stevens Drive. It has two halves: an east-bank lobe along the Kars shore of the Rideau River (Fairway Drive, Edgewater Drive, Marina Drive, River Road and their crescents, wrapping around the village of Kars), plus the Antochi Lane strip just south of Bridge Street; and a west-bank body of farm-country roads between Prince of Wales Drive and Highway 416 in the west and the Rideau River in the east, down Century Road, First Line Road and Carsonby Road to Roger Stevens Drive.

 

Why do some Manotick South listings not appear on the live map?

 

The listings on this website come through the CREA DDF feed. When a home is listed, its brokerage chooses whether to distribute it to agent websites; a listing that is not distributed still appears on REALTOR.ca and in the board's Matrix system, but never reaches this site. A handful of the district's current listings are in that position, so the live map is most of the picture, not all of it — ask me and I will pull the complete list from the board system.

 

What should I check before buying on the Kars shore?

 

Three things, before the offer firms up. First, the shore road allowance: on older waterfront lots the 66-foot allowance along the water may still belong to the municipality, which affects what you actually own and what you can build. Second, services: almost everything down here runs on a private well and septic system, so water quality, well flow and a septic inspection belong in the conditions. Third, flood and slope mapping along the riverbank — your lawyer and insurer will both ask. None of it is a reason to walk away; it is the standard homework that makes a shore purchase a good one.

 

Is Manotick South mostly houses, or is land a factor?

 

Land is a real factor here — more than in any other Manotick district. Building lots and acreage parcels come up on roads like Sutherland Drive, and the Antochi Lane strip south of Bridge Street carries a multi-parcel assembly of future development land. Every parcel needs its own homework — zoning, severance history, well and septic feasibility, and access — which is why the Manotick Land flagship guide in the table above tracks the whole area's land market in one place.

 

Which schools serve Manotick South?

 

It splits by bank. Families on the Kars shore are in the catchment for Kars on the Rideau Public School in Kars; west-bank families use the Manotick schools, with French Immersion at Half Moon Bay Public School. Catholic elementary families use St. Leonard School in the village, and the high schools are St. Mark Catholic High School in the village and South Carleton High School in Richmond. Always confirm the school for the exact address with the board before you buy — the disclaimer below explains why.

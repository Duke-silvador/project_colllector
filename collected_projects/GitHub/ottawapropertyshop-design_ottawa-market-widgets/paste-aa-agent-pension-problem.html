The Problem Nobody Mentions at the Awards Dinner

A career in real estate builds lifestyle, not retirement capital. Your database feels like an asset, but it is mostly relationships — and relationships do not transfer like a shop.

In most jobs, decades build savings and a pension that pays whether you show up or not. In real estate, decades build a reputation and a contact list. Neither pays you next year if you stop.

The book of business is the hardest truth. Clients chose you — your judgment, your name. A buyer is really buying a list of people free to ignore the new agent. Some databases do sell, usually to a partner who worked the clients alongside you, but the price reflects that risk. Meanwhile licence and board fees do not retire when you do. So most agents' actual plan is to work until health or family decides for them. That is not a plan — it is an absence of one, discovered late.

Option One and Two: Sell the Book, or Hand It Over

Selling works best early, to someone clients know. Succession preserves more value — trust transfers gradually.

Selling the book. A clean sale needs something verifiable: a current, permission-based database, a record of repeat business, and clients who have heard the buyer's name before the announcement. Expect payment over time, tied to business that closes after the handover.

Succession. You introduce a successor while still active. They co-work listings and meet clients at real appointments, so nobody is handed to a stranger. The economics — typically a referral arrangement on past clients for an agreed period — must be written down through your brokerages.

Can I really sell my book of business when I retire?

Sometimes, partially — usually for less than agents hope. What sells is a warm, well-kept database with proven repeat business, ideally to an agent your clients recognise. A cold list, however long, does not sell well. If a sale is in your plan, start three to five years before you want to stop.

Option Three: The Part-Time Wind-Down

Working less suits many agents — past clients and referrals only. It only works if fixed costs shrink with your workload.

The wind-down is the option most veterans choose. You keep the work you enjoy — past clients, their children, referrals earned over decades — and drop the rest. Income falls but does not vanish, and you choose the pace.

Is revenue share a pension?

No — walk away from anyone who calls it one. A pension is a defined, funded obligation. Revenue share is a share of company revenue, paid under program rules while agents in your organisation produce.

Living in Osgoode Township — North and South of the Line

The north (1605) has the township’s one real rural-community story outside the villages: Edwards, at the headwaters of Bear Brook on Mitch Owens Road. It was named in 1901 for William Cameron Edwards, lumberman and the area’s member of Parliament, and it lived as a flag stop on the New York & Ottawa Railway from 1898 to 1957 — station, siding and telegraph, with a post office kept in the same family from about 1900. The trains are long gone; what remains is a dispersed community of about 350 people, a city park, small businesses, and the Lynnwood Gardens land-lease community out by the Highway 417 interchange at Boundary Road. Around it, 1605 is estate lots and hobby farms all the way west to Manotick Station Road.

The south (1606) never had an Edwards. Dalmeny — a dispersed crossroads near the source of the South Castor River, perhaps 225 people in the surrounding blocks — Reids Mills and Spring Hill were concession crossroads: a church, a school, a store once, thinned as farms consolidated. There are no services of their own in the south today, and residents will tell you that is the arrangement, not a problem: Osgoode village is the west’s answer for everything, Vernon and Metcalfe the east’s. The fabric of both districts is borrowed from the villages around them — which is precisely why this hub exists instead of two thin standalone pages.

A Short History of Osgoode Township

Osgoode Township was established in 1798 and named for William Osgoode, the first Chief Justice of Upper Canada. Settlement began around 1827 — and who arrived first is a genuinely contested piece of local history: the McDonell family’s claim and the Archibald York family’s claim both survive in the record, and honest local history reports both rather than picking a winner. The township’s first crossroads, Baker’s Corners, is today’s Metcalfe. The township was incorporated in 1850, and on January 1, 2001 it amalgamated into the City of Ottawa.

The railways sorted the township’s places into winners and bystanders. Osgoode boomed as a station village on the Ottawa–Prescott line; stagecoach Metcalfe was bypassed and kept the fair instead; Edwards lived and died as a flag stop on the New York Central’s line (1898–1957); and on the Castor River the mill villages ground, sawed and shipped for the farms around them. Around the crossroads grew the shared furniture both districts still use: one-room schools, cheese factories, the Metcalfe Fair (running since 1856), and eventually one high school in Metcalfe that every teenager in the township still rides the bus to. Districts 1605 and 1606 are what was always between those places — the farm fabric itself, now wearing two MLS labels.

Homes & Housing Stock in Osgoode Township

In the north, the stock runs from Greely-grade estate homes on the fringe — newer builds on big serviced-by-well lots in the subdivisions wrapping Greely’s south side — through the Adam Baker and Iveson pockets, to bungalows and two-storeys on one-to-two-acre lots along the concession roads, with working farms and hobby farms of ten acres and more behind them. In the south, the same rural stock stretches thinner: older farmhouses, big-lot country homes, and estate builds along the Stagecoach Road corridor. There are no condos and no townhouse rows in either district — everything is freehold, and almost everything runs on a private well and septic system.

What that stock means in practice: township homes reward buyers who read land, not just floor plans. Acreage, frontage, soil, drainage and road frontage move value here as much as kitchens do — and a farm property carries homework a subdivision home never asks for. The next section is not fine print here; in the township it is the purchase.

Wells & Septic — the Township Homework

Neither district is on municipal water or sewer, and neither is anywhere else in the township’s villages. Every purchase here should include:

  • A potability and bacteria water test on the well, plus a flow/recovery check — estate-lot wells and farm wells behave very differently.

  • A septic inspection with the tank pumped and the bed assessed; ask for the system’s age, size and any permits on file. A replacement bed is one of the largest cheques a rural buyer can inherit.

  • Heating and services history — oil, propane and electric heat are all common out here, and each carries a different monthly reality.

  • For farm and acreage properties, a hard look at zoning, road frontage, drainage and any tile or easement records before conditions come off — the land is half the purchase.

None of this is a reason to avoid the township — half the Ottawa valley lives happily on well and septic. It is a reason to buy with your eyes open, and it is standard practice in every offer I write out here.

Schools Serving Osgoode Township

There is no school inside districts 1605 or 1606 — children are bused to the schools of the villages and the Greely side, and which school depends on which side of the township the address sits. The map as it stands:

  • Greely Elementary School (OCDSB) — covers the Greely-adjacent fringe of 1605.

  • Castor Valley Elementary School (OCDSB) in Greely — the Early French Immersion centre for Grades 7 and 8 for the wider Osgoode area.

  • Metcalfe Public School (OCDSB, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8) in Metcalfe — the default elementary for the east side of both districts, Edwards included, and eastern 1606.

  • Osgoode Public School (OCDSB, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6) in Osgoode village — serves the village and the surrounding rural lots, including western 1606.

  • Osgoode Township High School (OCDSB, Grades 9 to 12) in Metcalfe — the township’s only public high school; its own published list of communities served includes Metcalfe, Greely, Osgoode, Vernon, Edwards and Kenmore.

  • St. Catherine School (OCSB, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6) in Metcalfe — the Catholic elementary for the east side; the Catholic map splits with St. Mary School in Gloucester for the west side, by exact address.

  • St. Mark Catholic High School (OCSB, Grades 7 to 12) in Manotick — the Catholic secondary destination for the township.

  • French-language boards (CECCE and CEPEO): catchments here are address-specific — run your exact address through each board’s school locator rather than relying on any summary, including this one.

School buses are run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority (OSTA) for the English boards and the Consortium de transport scolaire d’Ottawa for the French boards. In a township where every school is a bus ride, the pickup time is part of the house — ask about it.

Community Life in Osgoode Township

The township’s calendar is shared, and both districts plug into all of it. The Metcalfe Fair — running since 1856, run by the Metcalfe Agricultural Society — is the anchor event of the township’s year. The Osgoode Link Pathway runs 21 kilometres of paved trail on the old Prescott-subdivision rail bed, from Osgoode village north through Greely’s west side to Leitrim by the airport, stitching 1603 to 1605 to the city. Add the township museum in Vernon, Pioneer Day, the arenas and halls of Osgoode and Metcalfe, and the churches of the villages — a rural district’s social life here is a short drive in a known direction, not an absence of one.

Getting Around & Who Runs the Township

Four roads organize everything. Ottawa Road 6 (Roger Stevens Drive – Snake Island Road – Victoria Street) is the east–west line the districts are named by. Mitch Owens Road (Ottawa Road 8) runs the north edge — Manotick to Greely to Edwards to Vars — and carries the township’s commuter traffic to the Highway 417 interchange at Boundary Road, which also serves Edwards and the east side. Stagecoach Road (Ottawa Road 25) links Manotick Station and Greely south through the 1606 corridor to Osgoode village. Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31, the old Highway 31) is the north–south spine up the middle to the city. There is no regular OC Transpo service — the township drives. Municipal services are the City of Ottawa’s: Ward 20 (Osgoode) is the township’s seat at council, and waste, snow and road maintenance run on the city’s rural schedules.

Osgoode Township vs Its Neighbours

Buyers cross-shop the township’s districts against its villages, so here is the honest comparison. Greely, at the north edge, is the big one — estate subdivisions, the highest prices, the closest thing to suburbia. Metcalfe has the fair, the high school and the daily services. Osgoode village is the service centre — grocery, pharmacy, the Link Pathway. Vernon is the museum village and the township’s value entry, and Kenmore the quiet Castor River hamlet on the eastern edge. Districts 1605 and 1606 are what the villages sit in: the land itself — the pick for buyers who want acreage, quiet and a specific road, and do not need a village address to get it.

Questions Buyers Ask About Osgoode Township

What is Osgoode Township today?
A former township inside the City of Ottawa. Osgoode Township was established in 1798, incorporated in 1850, and amalgamated into Ottawa on January 1, 2001 — today it is Ward 20 (Osgoode) at city council. The township name never went away: residents still use it, and the real-estate districts in this corner of the city — the 1600s — still follow its map.

What are districts 1605 and 1606?
They are the MLS compass districts for the township’s rural remainder. District 1605 is everything north of Regional Road 6 outside the Greely, Metcalfe and Osgoode village districts; district 1606 is everything south of the line outside the Osgoode and Vernon village districts. Neither has a village core or a name of its own — which is exactly why they share this hub guide, with two separate live searches so every count stays exact.

What is Regional Road 6?
Ottawa Road 6 — the east–west line that splits the two districts: Roger Stevens Drive in the west, Snake Island Road through the middle, and Victoria Street through Metcalfe. It is not Mitch Owens Road — that is Ottawa Road 8, the next east–west line north, running Manotick to Greely to Edwards and the Highway 417 interchange.

Does Osgoode Township have municipal water and sewer?
No. Private wells and septic systems are the norm across both districts and in the township’s villages alike. Budget for a water test, a septic inspection, and the ongoing care both systems need, and verify the specifics lot by lot with the City and your inspector.

Which schools serve districts 1605 and 1606?
It splits by side. The Greely fringe of 1605 feeds Greely-area schools; the east side of both districts — Edwards included — defaults to Metcalfe Public School; the western side of 1606 looks to Osgoode Public School. Every public secondary student in the township rides to Osgoode Township High School in Metcalfe — its published list of communities served includes Edwards by name. Catholic families split between St. Catherine School in Metcalfe and St. Mary School in Gloucester by address, then St. Mark Catholic High School in Manotick. Always confirm the schools assigned to an exact address in the board’s locator before you buy.

How far is Osgoode Township from downtown Ottawa?
Osgoode village sits 32 kilometres from downtown as the crow flies — the locator map on this page shows the straight-line distance to every surrounding community. By car, plan on roughly 35 to 45 minutes from most of the township in normal conditions — an estimate, and winter has opinions. Edwards, at the northeast corner, runs through the Highway 417 interchange at Boundary Road; Dalmeny and the south are closer to the village roads.

Why do 1605 and 1606 share one guide?
Because they are one place wearing two MLS labels. Neither district has a village, a store or a name anyone searches by — they are the farm country between the township’s villages, and their story, schools and services are the township’s shared story. What is kept separate is the data: each district keeps its own exact listing search, its own snapshot and its own sold figures on this page.

What kinds of homes are in 1605 and 1606?
Estate lots wrapping Greely’s south fringe, subdivision pockets like Adam Baker and Iveson, hobby farms and working farms on the concession roads, and estate builds along the Stagecoach Road corridor in the south. Everything is freehold — there are no condos in either district — and almost everything runs on a private well and septic system.

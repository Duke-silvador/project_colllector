The Short Answer

West Ottawa’s new-home market is two communities sharing one builder roster. In Kanata, the building concentrates in three bands: the north, around March Road and the technology park; the west, at Huntmar Drive and Terry Fox; and the south, in Bridlewood and the Kanata South fabric. In Stittsville, it wraps around a genuine village core — growth to the south and west along the Fernbank and Shea corridors, and newer pockets near Main Street. The same names appear on both sides of the line — Minto, Mattamy, Cardel, Richcraft, Claridge, Caivan, Tamarack and more — often with different product on different lot sizes. So the community choice comes first and the builder comparison second: pick where you want to live, then compare the lot, the model, the deposit schedule, the studio and the closing date across every builder selling there. And settle your representation before the first sales-centre visit — that rule governs the west end exactly as it governs everywhere else in this series.

Two Communities, One Builder Market

Kanata was designed before it was built: laid out in the 1960s as a planned garden city, with curving streets, hedgerow suburbs like Beaverbrook, and a technology park planted along March Road as its economic engine. It worked — the research park grew into Canada’s largest tech park, and the suburbs grew around it in rings: Glen Cairn, Bridlewood, Katimavik, Hazeldean, Kanata Lakes, Morgan’s Grant. Stittsville took the opposite road. It began in 1854 as a village in Goulbourn Township, grew around a railway stop and a main street of hotels, stores and trades, and stayed a village in feel long after the suburbs reached it. The Trans Canada Trail now runs the old rail line through the centre of it, crossing Main Street at Abbott.

The two meet along a seam most buyers feel rather than see: Kanata South and Stittsville’s north edge read as one fabric, communities like Blackstone sit on the line between them, and the same household routinely cross-shops both — a townhome release in Kanata South against a single in Stittsville, priced off the same builder lists. Highway 417 runs across the top of both, which is why the commute comparison so often comes out a draw and the decision falls to the place itself: planned city or old village, tech-park gravity or main-street gravity. Neither answer is wrong. They are different lives, built by the same builders.

Kanata: The Planned City Around the Tech Park

Kanata’s new construction follows the plan’s logic — it extends the rings. In the north, along the March Road corridor and the South March highlands, Minto builds at Brookline and Mattamy at Northwoods, while Richardson Ridge — shared by Cardel and Uniform Developments — and Uniform’s Copperwood Estate carry the upper end: towns, singles and estate-calibre lots within sight of the tech park’s campuses. This is the band where the employment is, and the product runs deepest, from stacked towns to detached homes on generous lots.

In the west, Minto’s Arcadia stands at Huntmar Drive and Terry Fox beside the Canadian Tire Centre — avenue, urban and metro towns and singles, with the stacked towns of Parkside at Arcadia at the accessible end, and Kanata Centrum’s retail across the road. In the south, the building fills the established fabric: Cardel’s Blackstone on the Stittsville seam, Mattamy’s Trailwest, and Monahan Landing — built by Mattamy and Glenview and now largely complete — inside Bridlewood’s mature streets. Claridge, Richcraft, Urbandale and HN Homes also build in Kanata, phase by phase. The pattern to hold onto: Kanata’s established neighbourhoods are finished places with finished amenities, and its new phases plug into them — you are rarely buying a promise of future shops and schools, because the plan built them first.

Stittsville: The Village That Kept Its Main Street

Stittsville’s centre is a working main street, not a stage set: the red-brick hotel building at Main and Abbott — Green’s Hotel, raised in the railway era — still anchors the corner, the trail crosses where the trains used to stop, and the old village fabric of Carp Road and Hazeldean sits a short walk from subdivisions that did not exist a generation ago. Buyers who choose Stittsville are usually choosing that layering on purpose: a village core with a suburb’s housing stock around it.

The growth wraps the core. Westwood, in the centre, is a three-builder community — Claridge, Richcraft and Tamarack. At Fernbank and Shea, Cardel’s EdenWylde is in its later phases, its current release a set of back-to-back terrace towns. Mattamy’s Fairwinds is the established name on the north side, and Mattamy’s Mainhaven and Minto’s Abbott’s Run are the newer edges. South of Fernbank, the next neighbourhoods are already in the approval pipeline — Caivan’s Magnolia among them — and smaller pockets like Tartan’s Idylea and Valecraft’s Shea Village fill the seams. Read Stittsville as a village with growth rings, the same shape as Kanata, drawn by history instead of by a planner.

The Builders, Side by Side

The west-end roster divides by habit. The volume builders — Minto and Mattamy above all — run master-planned communities on both sides of the line, with the deepest model ranges and the most phased releases. Cardel, Richcraft, Claridge, Caivan and Tamarack work the middle: substantial communities, strong design studios, product from stacked towns to singles. Uniform Developments is the boutique end in Kanata — fewer homes, estate positioning — and Tartan and Valecraft hold smaller Stittsville pockets. Every one of them also builds elsewhere in Ottawa, which is what makes the west end such a clean comparison market: the same builder’s Kanata community and Stittsville community can be visited in one afternoon and read against each other.

Read them against each other properly, though, because the same builder is not the same product in two places.   • Start with the lot, not the model. Six Checks Before You Sign

  • Compare the same builder across both communities. Before you choose a community, read one builder’s Kanata offer against its Stittsville offer — lot, standard specs, premiums, dates. The differences are the market telling you what each side values.

  • Get the lot premium and its reason in writing. Park-facing, corner, pie-shaped, oversized — every premium has a stated reason. If the reason is not on the sheet, the premium is a number, not a fact.

  • Ask which phase you are buying into. A first-phase release and a final-phase release are different purchases: construction around you, finished amenities, and the builder’s posture all differ by phase. Know which one your agreement is in.

  • Check the closing window against your own dates. Your sale, your lease, your school year. A west-end closing that lands a season off your life costs more than any upgrade on the studio wall.

  • Ask what is condo and what is freehold. Stacked towns, back-to-back towns and private-road communities can carry common-element or road fees on top of the mortgage. Establish the tenure of the exact product, in the exact phase, before signing.

  • Put every incentive in the agreement. Price adjustments, included upgrades, buy-downs, credits — if it was offered across the desk, it gets written into the document, or it does not exist.

Questions Buyers Ask Me

Which builders build new homes in Kanata?
The deepest benches are Minto — Brookline in the north and Arcadia in the west — and Mattamy, at Northwoods and Trailwest. Cardel builds at Richardson Ridge and Blackstone, Uniform Developments at Richardson Ridge and Copperwood Estate, and Claridge, Richcraft, Urbandale and HN Homes are active phase by phase. The roster shifts as phases open and finish, which is why the comparison framework matters more than any single list.

Which builders build new homes in Stittsville?
Cardel at EdenWylde; Claridge, Richcraft and Tamarack together at Westwood; Mattamy at Fairwinds and Mainhaven; Minto at Abbott’s Run; Caivan at Magnolia, the large neighbourhood working through approvals to the south; and Tartan and Valecraft in smaller pockets. It is one of the longest builder lists in the city for a community of its size — a legacy of the village growing in rings.

Should I buy a new home in Kanata or in Stittsville?
Start with the life, not the floor plan. Kanata offers the planned city: the tech park’s employment, mature garden suburbs, and new phases that plug into finished amenities. Stittsville offers the village: a real main street, the trail through the centre, and newer rings around an old core. The commutes are close cousins, the builders overlap heavily, and the same budget often works on both sides — so cross-shop both, then let the place decide.

What is the difference between Kanata North, Kanata West and Kanata South for new homes?
The north is the growth band along March Road — Brookline, Northwoods, Richardson Ridge, Copperwood Estate — closest to the technology park and carrying the widest product range, from stacked towns to estate lots. The west is Arcadia’s ground at Huntmar and Terry Fox, beside the Canadian Tire Centre and Kanata Centrum. The south is infill inside the established fabric — Blackstone on the Stittsville seam, Monahan Landing in Bridlewood — where the surrounding streets, schools and shops are already finished.

Are west-end communities still releasing new phases?
Yes — communities here sell in phases, and the phase map is always moving: one community is in a first release while the street beside it is in its final one. That is exactly why a printed list ages badly and the registered detail matters: what is open, what is waitlisted and what is about to release is information builders give to registered buyers first.

Do I need my own agent to buy a builder home in Kanata or Stittsville?
You need the question settled before the first visit, whichever way you answer it. Builders register visitors, and a first visit made unregistered and unaccompanied can leave you unrepresented for that community from that day forward — the one-way door at the heart of this series. My strong advice is to be represented from the first visit: the builder pays the fee, and the representation covers the deposit schedule, the studio and the closing, not just the signing.

How much deposit will a west-end builder ask for?
It varies by builder, by product and by how far out the closing sits — freehold homes usually carry staged deposits across the build, while condo-form towns follow their own structure with deposit protection. There is no west-end standard number, which is the point of comparing the schedules side by side before comparing anything else: the same price with different deposit dates is a different offer.

Are there condo townhomes among the west-end new homes?
Yes, at several price points: stacked towns at Parkside at Arcadia, back-to-back terrace towns at EdenWylde, and other condo-form products across both communities. Condo form is not a defect — it is a tenure, with common elements to understand: private roads, shared elements and their fees belong in the comparison exactly like the lot premium does. Read the disclosure for the specific product and phase.

Can I buy a finished new home in Kanata or Stittsville without waiting for construction?
Yes. Builders hold finished and nearly finished homes in both communities — spec builds, model homes at a phase’s end, returned deals — and sell them on the builder’s closing date rather than a construction schedule. The finishes are the builder’s choices and the date is firmer, but the home is new, the warranty stands, and you walk through the actual home before you buy it.

What should I compare before signing with a west-end builder?
Six things: the same builder’s offer across both communities; the lot premium and its written reason; the phase you are buying into; the closing window against your own dates; the tenure — freehold or condo form, and its fees; and every incentive, written into the agreement. Run those six and the west end stops being two dozen sales centres and becomes one readable market.

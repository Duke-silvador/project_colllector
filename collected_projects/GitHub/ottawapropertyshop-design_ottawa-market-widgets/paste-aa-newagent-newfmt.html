eXp Realty commission split

Plain language: A "commission split" is how each commission check gets divided between you and the brokerage. GCI (gross commission income) is the full commission your deal earns before anyone takes a cut.

At eXp Realty in Canada, the split is 80/20: you keep 80% of every commission, eXp keeps 20% — until you have paid eXp $16,000 CAD in a single anniversary year. That is the cap: the maximum you will ever pay eXp in split in one year. The cap is reached at $80,000 GCI ($80,000 × 20% = $16,000).

After the cap, you keep 100% of your commissions for the rest of your anniversary year, minus a small per-transaction fee (details below). The cap resets on your anniversary date — the date you joined.

Canada worked example: On an Ottawa sale at $750,000 with a 2.5% commission, the deal earns $18,750 GCI. At the standard 80/20 split, you keep $15,000 and eXp keeps $3,750 toward your $16,000 cap. Roughly four to five deals like that and you are capped — every deal after that is 100% yours.

The new-agent exception: new agents enrolled in eXp's mentorship program pay the standard 80/20 split plus an extra 20% mentorship fee on their first three transactions — you net 60% on deals 1–3 — which funds the mentor working beside you. This is a real cost most attraction pages omit. It is also the price of never doing your first three deals alone.

eXp Realty revenue share

Plain language: "Revenue share" is money eXp pays you when agents you introduced to the brokerage close deals. It comes out of eXp's 20% company dollar — never out of the other agent's commission. Your 80% is never touched.

Plain language on the flow: eXp commits roughly half of its company dollar to the revenue-share pool. So of the $16,000 cap each agent pays, up to about $8,000 flows into the monthly pool and is distributed across 7 tiers to the sponsors and uplines of productive agents; eXp keeps roughly the other $8,000 to run the brokerage.

The 7 tiers, in plain terms: Tier 1 is the agents you personally introduced. Tier 2 is the agents they introduced, and so on down to tier 7. You earn a percentage of each agent's adjusted GCI at every level — the deeper tiers pay smaller percentages, but across many agents the totals compound.

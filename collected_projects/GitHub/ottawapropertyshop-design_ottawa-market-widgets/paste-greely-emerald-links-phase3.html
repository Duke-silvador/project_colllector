The pocket — Plan 4M-1660, the big-lot phase

Emerald Links began in 1988 as the Patterson family’s golf-course community: estate homes wrapping the Emerald Links Golf & Country Club, a 27-hole course with its clubhouse at 6357 Emerald Links Drive. Phase 1 (Plans 4M-655 and 4M-656) wrapped the course’s west and north sides; Phase 2 (Plan 4M-1267, 2005) added the Pebblewoods Drive corridor. Phase 3 is the third and last piece — Plan 4M-1660, the lands south of Pebblewoods Drive, draft-approved in 2012 and registered after the City directed registration in 2020.

The Phase 3 pocket — every street inside the gold boundary belongs to Plan 4M-1660, and the listings at the top of this guide are drawn from inside that same boundary.

Three streets carry the whole pocket. Green Links Way is the main east–west street, entering from Manotick Station Road and running east to the Emerald Estates boundary. Green Jacket Crescent is the southern loop around the pocket’s park block — a 3-hectare park sits inside the plan — and Course View Street is the short north–south link up to Pebblewoods Drive and Phase 2. The lots are the story, though: the registered plan sets them at 0.8 to 1.18 hectares — roughly two acres each, against the roughly one-acre lots of Phases 1 and 2. Phase 3 is the big-lot phase, and its homes have the room to show it: the crescent backs onto greenbelt and a multi-use pathway runs along the pocket’s southern boundary.

Why every home here is custom. The Phase 3 lots were not sold to one builder. They were sold one at a time, to individual buyers — local knowledge of the pocket puts those 2021–2022 lot sales at roughly $500,000 a lot — so at full build-out Phase 3 is all custom homes: each owner brought their own builder and their own design. Patterson Homes, the developer family’s building arm, has put up some of the pocket’s $2-million-plus homes as specs, but there is no model home, no design studio and no production floor plan here. What has risen on Green Jacket Crescent so far is exactly what that produces: individual estate homes — stone and stucco, 5,000-plus square feet — each one different from its neighbour. The full three-phase story, with the community’s combined map, is in the Emerald Links guide.

Servicing in Phase 3 — wells, septic and big-lot homework

Phase 3 homes run on private drilled wells and private septic systems — the servicing pattern across the pocket’s own listings — with natural gas and hydro at the lot line. There is no municipal water or sewer bill here; the services are yours, on your lot. That trade is the standard one for Greely estate lots, and it comes with the standard homework on any purchase: have the well water tested, have the septic system inspected, and budget for both as the private infrastructure they are. On a two-acre lot, add the acreage itself to the list — a long driveway, a riding mower or a plough arrangement, and the heating bill that comes with a 5,000-square-foot custom home.

Living in Emerald Links Phase 3

Daily life here starts with the course: 27 holes of the Emerald Links Golf & Country Club wrap the community’s north and east, and Phase 3’s streets sit a few hundred metres from the clubhouse. The pocket itself is quiet by construction — 43 lots on two acres each is a low-density fabric, with the plan’s own 3-hectare park in the middle of the crescent and the pathway along its south edge. The village core is about 4.7 km east — The Shoppes of Greely plaza on Bank Street for groceries and everyday services, the Greely Community Centre and the library branch on Meadow Drive. Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) is the spine north: downtown Ottawa is roughly half an hour in good traffic. The honest transit picture matches the rest of Greely: no regular OC Transpo route runs through the village — the Thursday Shopper Route 304 is the one exception — so Phase 3 is a driver’s address, and school buses, run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority, are the transit that matters to families here.

Schools — read this before you assume a path

Phase 3 sits at Greely’s west edge, where the school catchment lines meet — and they do not meet cleanly over this pocket. Measured against the boards’ published boundary maps, the Phase 3 boundary crosses the designated lines for the village’s public elementary and immersion catchments, so there is no single designated public path for the pocket: the assigned schools depend on the exact lot, and a Green Jacket Crescent address and a Green Links Way address can draw different answers. The Greely guides set out the village’s school paths in full — start with the Emerald Links guide — but for any specific Phase 3 property, the only honest check is the board’s school locator on that exact address, before you buy. School buses serve the area either way.

Emerald Links Phase 3 questions, answered

What is Emerald Links Phase 3?
The third and final phase of the Emerald Links golf-course community in Greely — Plan 4M-1660, south of Pebblewoods Drive. It is 43 estate lots on three streets: Green Links Way, Green Jacket Crescent and Course View Street. This guide is its own page because the pocket is a different product from Phases 1 and 2: bigger lots, newer homes, and every build a custom one.

How big are the Phase 3 lots?
The registered plan sets them at 0.8 to 1.18 hectares — roughly two acres each, marketed across the pocket’s listings as two-acre lots. That is about double the roughly one-acre lots of Phases 1 and 2, which is the core of the pocket’s luxury character.

Who builds the homes in Phase 3?
Whoever the lot owner hires. The lots were sold individually in 2021–2022 rather than to a single builder, so there is no production builder and no model home here — each home is a one-off custom build. Patterson Homes, the developer family’s building arm, has built some homes here as specs, alongside owners’ own custom builds.

Is Emerald Links Phase 3 the same as Emerald Estates?

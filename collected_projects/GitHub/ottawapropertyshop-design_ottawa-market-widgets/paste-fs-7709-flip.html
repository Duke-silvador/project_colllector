This guide to the Strandherd (7709) neighbourhood starts with the real numbers — the schools, parks and price bands that matter — because streets here trade on corridor access: a Transitway station of their own, the Marketplace shops at the east end, Highway 416 at the west end, and the Jock River along the south.

Strandherd is Barrhaven's late-1990s and 2000s family pocket (AMP area 7709 — Barrhaven — Strandherd) — centred on Strandherd Drive and Chapman Mills Drive and built around curving crescents and courts south of the arterial. It takes in Strandherd Meadows and the Chapman Mills streets, built mainly by Richcraft Homes and Phoenix Homes, and it holds five schools inside its own boundaries. Its signature feature is the Strandherd Transitway station and Park & Ride at its northwest corner. And it is the closest of Barrhaven's northern pockets to Highway 416, whose interchange sits at its own west end.

Schools & Catchments

The school bench is the headline here — five schools sit inside the pocket itself: Chapman Mills Public School (260 Leamington Way, OCDSB, JK–6, English and Early French Immersion, opened 2012), St. Elizabeth Ann Seton School (41 Weybridge Drive, OCSB elementary), St. Emily Catholic School (500 Chapman Mills Drive, OCSB elementary), École élémentaire catholique Jean-Robert-Gauthier (651 Chapman Mills Drive, CECCE French Catholic elementary) and the French-public École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois (1310 Chapman Mills Drive, CEPEO 7–12) — a French secondary inside the pocket is rare on Ottawa's west side. Serving the pocket from just outside: Cedarview Middle School (2760 Cedarview Road, OCDSB 7–8) and Mary Honeywell Elementary School (54 Kennevale Drive, OCDSB, JK–6) at the west side. English-public and Catholic secondaries split by street — Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School (149 Berrigan Drive), John McCrae Secondary School (103 Malvern Drive), St. Mother Teresa High School (440 Longfields Drive) and St. Joseph High School (3333 Greenbank Road) — plus the private Ottawa Christian School (255 Tartan Drive, JK–8) on the Tartan corridor. Commute & Transportation

Strandherd station — the pocket's own Southwest Transitway station with its Park & Ride (3680 Strandherd Drive, at the northwest corner by Greenbank Road; the station opened in 2007) — is served by routes 70, 75 and 80, with peak-period routes 275, 279 and 305. Route 70 runs Fallowfield to Limebank through the Marketplace and the Tartan corridor; route 99 is the local that runs through the pocket itself, along Chapman Mills Drive and Strandherd Drive between Barrhaven Centre and Limebank station on the Line 2 O-Train; route 110 runs Strandherd Drive, and route 173 serves the pocket's west edge along Weybridge Drive, Fable Street and Kennevale Drive. Road access matches the transit: the Highway 416 interchange sits at the pocket's own west end, the Vimy Memorial Bridge carries Strandherd Drive east over the Rideau River to Prince of Wales Drive and Riverside South, and downtown Ottawa is a typical 25–30 minute drive off-peak. The long-term plan converts the Transitway to LRT with a Strandherd station.

Parks, Trails & Recreation

The Jock River is the pocket's southern green edge — about 72 km of river, first recorded as River Jacques in 1816 and carrying the Jock River name by 1830 — with walking paths along the river streets. At the northeast edge, just across the arterial, the Chapman Mills Conservation Area (253 Winding Way, Rideau Valley Conservation Authority) protects 23 acres on the Rideau River: a 1.5 km boardwalk trail, a fishing platform, an accessible dock for canoes and kayaks, a covered picnic shelter and free parking, on land gifted by the South Nepean Development Corporation. Chapman Mills Park (139 Windhurst Drive) is the playground park at the same edge. At the southwest edge, The Conservancy lands add a builder-stated 140 acres of integrated greenspace with over 3 km of Jock River frontage. The Minto Recreation Complex — Barrhaven (3500 Cambrian Road: 25 m pool, two NHL-size arenas, gymnasium and fitness) is a short drive south.

And the pocket's east gate is a landmark: the Vimy Memorial Bridge carries Strandherd Drive over the Rideau River — opened on July 12, 2014 as the Strandherd-Armstrong Bridge and renamed for Vimy Ridge on October 1, 2014, it won the Gustav Lindenthal Medal in 2015 and carries three vehicle lanes plus a dedicated bus lane and a bike lane in each direction.

At the west end, the Strandherd and Greenbank node covers the grocery-and-services run, and Costco Barrhaven (4315 Strandherd Drive, at Citigate toward Highway 416) is minutes west. Places of worship along the Woodroffe Avenue and Greenbank Road corridors serve the pocket, and Barrhaven-wide community associations cover the Strandherd Neighbourhood — these streets plug into the wider Barrhaven community fabric rather than a single pocket association.

Safety & Street Character

Quiet crescents and courts — Garrity Crescent, Serena Way, Tacom Circle, Akita Walk — branch off the collectors (Paul Metivier Drive, Harbour View Street, Chapman Mills Drive), so the arterial carries the through-traffic and the interior streets stay residential. The road itself is part of the story: until the mid-1990s, Strandherd Drive ran only between Moodie Drive and Woodroffe Avenue; Highway 416 and south Barrhaven's growth extended and realigned it into today's corridor, and its Maravista Drive to Jockvale Road segment has since been widened to four lanes. Future Development

Barrhaven's future downtown lands close to home: the City is planning the Barrhaven Civic Centre at 180 Riocan Avenue — a district library branch, a cultural centre with performance space, a dedicated seniors' space and an urban park and plaza — on land purchased together with the site of a future LRT station, the complex fronting Chapman Mills Drive, the same corridor that runs through this pocket. At the southwest edge, The Conservancy by Caivan keeps building out along the Jock River. And the long-term Barrhaven LRT plan converts the Transitway to rail, Strandherd station included.

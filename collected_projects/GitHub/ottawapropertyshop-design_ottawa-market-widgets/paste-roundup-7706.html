Schools in Longfields

Schools are half the reason families choose Longfields. The neighbourhood gives its name to Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School (grades 7–12, about 2,250 students), which shares its Berrigan Drive campus with Berrigan Elementary School (JK–6). Catholic families have St. Mother Teresa High School (grades 7–12) on Longfields Drive, plus St. Luke School and Monsignor Paul Baxter School at elementary level. French-language options are in the neighbourhood too: École élémentaire catholique Pierre-Elliott-Trudeau, École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard (grades 7–12) and École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean (JK–8). Few Ottawa suburbs put this many schools, in this many boards, inside one neighbourhood — one caution that matters: catchments split by street, so your exact school follows your exact address — always confirm on the board locator before you buy.

Transit, parks and pathways

Longfields is the only Barrhaven sub-area with its own named Transitway station: Longfields Station opened in spring 2011 on the Southwest Transitway, the pathway network runs right underneath it, and the Route 75 bus along the Transitway runs around the clock. Look for the Bellwether bronze sheep grazing by the platform — public art that has become the station’s quiet mascot. The park bench is deep: Ken Ross Park (with an off-leash dog area), Water Dragon Park with its splash pad and tobogganing hill, Mancini Park’s pickleball courts, the Berrigan skate park beside the school campus, and a long roster of pocket parks woven through the crescents. Established trees, pathway links and schools you can walk to — this is 1990s–2000s Barrhaven at its most complete.

Shopping and daily life

Daily life runs along the Greenbank Road corridor at the neighbourhood’s west edge — Farm Boy and Sobeys anchor the grocery runs — with the Strandherd Drive retail strip and Barrhaven’s Marketplace a few minutes south and east for everything else: big-box stores, banks, dentists, clinics and pharmacies all sit within a short drive, and Longfields’ own Longfields Drive strip covers the everyday stops in between. It is a neighbourhood designed so that most weeks, the car barely leaves Barrhaven.

Something interesting about Longfields

For 25 years, Longfields Community Church met in school gyms, borrowed churches and an old farmhouse on its Woodroffe Avenue property — and it has now opened its own purpose-built church building on that same land. A congregation that waited a quarter-century for a home of its own is a fair symbol of this neighbourhood: patient, family-rooted, and built to stay.

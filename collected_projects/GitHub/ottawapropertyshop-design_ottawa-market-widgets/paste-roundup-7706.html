The Barrhaven picture: Barrhaven-wide, 190 freehold homes are listed today, and Longfields' 40 are about one in five of them. Its $642,400 median sits $37,500 below the Barrhaven-wide median of $679,900 — and what gap remains is a mix story as much as a price story: Longfields is established 1990s–2000s family stock, against a suburb-wide figure that blends in every pocket and product type across Barrhaven.

What I make of it: the story this time is a shelf compressing — choice narrowing at both ends while the middle rises. A buyer still gets 40 homes to compare inside one neighbourhood, but the first rung of this market costs $175,000 more than it did two weeks ago, so the homework matters more than the waiting. Sellers get the mirror image of that advice: in a pocket where homes are leaving the shelf and the middle is climbing, pricing to today's market is not a slogan, it is the whole strategy — the homes that sit are the ones priced for a market that has already moved on. And remember what these numbers are: asking prices on the public shelf. What happens to a listing after it leaves the shelf — sold, expired or withdrawn — the aggregates cannot tell us.

Barrhaven's established family belt

Longfields is the big residential belt Barrhaven built through the 1990s and 2000s — some 19,800 residents between Greenbank Road to the west, Woodroffe Avenue to the east, Fallowfield Road and the railway to the north and Strandherd Drive to the south. The stock is the established Barrhaven mix: detached family homes, semis and townhomes on crescents with mature trees, pathway links woven through, and two anchors no other Barrhaven pocket can match — a Transitway station that carries the neighbourhood's own name, and a secondary-school campus that gives the area the second half of its name, Davidson Heights. Nothing here is brand new, and that is the point: this is the suburb at its most settled, where the comparables are deep and the streets have twenty years of history behind them.

Getting around from Longfields

Longfields Station opened in spring 2011 on the Southwest Transitway, and it sits inside the neighbourhood it is named for — Barrhaven's only sub-area that can say that. Route 75 runs along the Transitway around the clock, the pathway network passes right underneath the station, and the Bellwether bronze sheep grazing by the platform have become the station's quiet mascots. For drivers, Greenbank Road and Woodroffe Avenue frame the pocket north–south, Fallowfield Road runs along the top, and Strandherd Drive carries the retail strip along the bottom — most of the week's driving never leaves Barrhaven at all.

Schools around Longfields

Schools are half the reason families choose Longfields — the neighbourhood carries a bench across all four boards, anchored by the campus that shares its name:

  • Public English (OCDSB): Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School (grades 7 to 12, about 2,250 students, opened in 2009) shares its Berrigan Drive campus with Berrigan Elementary School (kindergarten to grade 6) — one campus carries a Longfields child from kindergarten to graduation.

  • Catholic English (OCSB): St. Mother Teresa High School (grades 7 to 12) stands on Longfields Drive, with St. Luke School and Monsignor Paul Baxter School at elementary level inside the pocket.

  • French Catholic (CECCE): École élémentaire catholique Pierre-Elliott-Trudeau serves the elementary years, and École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard (grades 7 to 12) carries the secondary years.

  • French public (CEPEO): École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean (kindergarten to grade 8) rounds out the four-board bench inside the neighbourhood.

Catchments turn on the exact street here, so check yours on each board's school-locator tool before you buy. My Longfields guide points you to the same locators.

Parks, play and daily life

The park bench is deep: Ken Ross Park with its off-leash dog area, Water Dragon Park with its splash pad and tobogganing hill, Mancini Park's pickleball courts, the Berrigan skate park beside the school campus, and a long roster of pocket parks woven through the crescents. Daily life runs along the Greenbank Road corridor at the neighbourhood's west edge — Farm Boy and Sobeys anchor the grocery runs — with the Strandherd Drive retail strip and Barrhaven's Marketplace a few minutes south and east for everything else: big-box stores, banks, clinics and pharmacies all sit within a short drive, and the Longfields Drive strip covers the everyday stops in between.

Something interesting about Longfields

For 25 years, Longfields Community Church met in school gyms, borrowed churches and an old farmhouse on its Woodroffe Avenue property — and it has now opened its own purpose-built church building on that same land. A congregation that waited a quarter-century for a home of its own is a fair symbol of this neighbourhood: patient, family-rooted, and built to stay.

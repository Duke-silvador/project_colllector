Rideau Forest — the Estate Enclave (8005)

East of the river, south of Mitch Owens Road, Rideau Forest is the reason Manotick East carries the group’s prestige end. The community was carved from thickly wooded land — curving streets through mature oak, maple and pine, on Rideau Forest Drive, Queenscourt Crescent, Kilchurn Terrace, Scobie Crescent, Vidame Road and Knights Drive — and privacy is its founding idea: homes set so deep in the trees that the street reads as forest first, houses second.

The model is unlike anything else in the group. The developer — Rideau Forest Development Ltd., the Ken Gordon Holdings family company of Manotick — sells the LAND only, in phases. Each buyer builds a custom home with their own builder and architect, but the vendor keeps design control: working drawings go to the developer for written approval before a building permit, homes are built to the approved plans and material requirements, and lot clearing is limited to the approved building envelope. Lots typically run about one and a half to two-plus acres. Servicing is estate-lot style — private well and septic, with underground hydro, cable, high-speed internet and natural gas at the lot. The result is a composed landscape of individual stone, stucco and brick estates that no production builder could have planned — and a resale market where every listing is genuinely one of one.

Village amenities, minutes away — the plaza and Main Street serve the east bank as much as the village itself.

The Wider East Bank — River Road to Manotick Station (8005)

Beyond Rideau Forest, the district holds the river’s east shore and the concession corridors behind it. River Road carries the shore properties — older homes, cottages grown into houses, and waterfront estates looking back at the village and Long Island. Inland, the Nick Adams Road and Herberts Corners Road pockets and the Manotick Station Road corridor run east and south in a rural-residential register: acreages, hobby-farm lots and newer custom homes on country roads, down toward Flag Station at the district’s south edge.

The homework changes with the pocket. Shore properties carry the riverfront file — wells, septic, flood questions and the shoreline road allowance check on older surveys. Inland acreages carry the rural file — septic capacity, well quality, road frontage and what the lot can actually be built or severed into. Either way, the value in Manotick East is land first: frontage, trees, privacy and position relative to the village. Two homes with the same square footage can sit a million dollars apart on those four things alone.

Living on the East Bank — Schools, Parks and the Drive

Manotick East holds the group’s Catholic secondary school on its own ground, which anchors family life on this side of the river. The standing warning applies — catchments are set by your exact home address and program, so confirm any specific property in the board’s school locator before you buy.

  • St. Mark Catholic High School (OCSB, Grades 7–12) — 1040 Dozois Road, in Manotick East itself. The Catholic secondary for the whole Manotick group, in English and French.

  • Manotick Public School (OCDSB, Junior Kindergarten–Grade 5) — 1075 Bridge Street. The village’s public elementary, teaching in English and French, just across the river.

  • St. Leonard Catholic School (OCSB, Junior Kindergarten–Grade 6) — 5344 Long Island Road. The Catholic elementary on Long Island, over 500 students, in English and French.

  • Kars on the Rideau Public School (OCDSB) — in Kars, south on the river. The area’s public middle school — French programming from Kindergarten and English/French through the middle grades.

  • South Carleton High School (OCDSB, Grades 9–12) — in Richmond. The public secondary for the region, drawing over 1,300 students from Manotick, Kars, North Gower and the surrounding countryside.

  • French-language boards (CEPEO / CECCE) — no French-language school sits in Manotick itself; French-board catchments follow their own sector maps, so confirm your exact address in the French boards’ locators.

Centennial Park — the arena, outdoor rink and park grounds serving the east side of the village.

For daily life, the east side leans on Centennial Park — the Manotick Arena, the outdoor rink and the park grounds off Doctor Leach Drive — and on Rideau Forest’s own Phase 1 park on Scobie Crescent, with its play structures, field and courts. The village across the bridge supplies the rest: Main Street shops and cafés, Dickinson Square and the river. For the drive, River Road runs north toward Highway 416 in about ten minutes, and downtown is roughly half an hour outside rush hour. Transit is thin, and honest planning matters: OC Transpo’s Route 186 runs a limited number of trips linking the village toward Barrhaven and the wider network, so Manotick East is a driving neighbourhood — the space and the trees are what the drive buys.

Questions Buyers Ask About Manotick East

What is Manotick East?
Manotick East is OREB district 8005 — the east bank of the Rideau River at Manotick, running from Bridge Street toward Manotick Station. It holds Rideau Forest in full, the River Road shore, and the Nick Adams Road, Herberts Corners Road and Manotick Station Road corridors. The village core is minutes away across the bridge; the land itself is estate country — large treed lots, custom homes and room to breathe, inside the City of Ottawa.

What is Rideau Forest, and how does buying there work?
Rideau Forest is Manotick East’s prestige estate enclave: a low-density community carved from thickly wooded land east of the river, where Rideau Forest Development Ltd. — the Ken Gordon Holdings family company — sells the land in phases and every buyer builds a custom home with their own builder and architect. The vendor keeps design control: working drawings need the developer’s written approval before a building permit, and clearing is limited to the approved building envelope. That is why a community of individually designed homes still reads as one composed, wooded landscape.

What services do Manotick East homes use?
Estate-lot servicing. Rideau Forest lots run on private wells and septic systems, with underground hydro, cable, high-speed internet and natural gas at the lot — not municipal water and sewer. River Road shore properties carry the riverfront file as well: well and septic homework, flood questions, and the shoreline road allowance check on older surveys. None of it is a reason to stay away; all of it is a reason to buy with the paperwork open.

Which schools serve Manotick East?
St. Mark Catholic High School stands in Manotick East itself, on Dozois Road (Grades 7–12). Younger children use Manotick Public School in the village (JK–Grade 5) or St. Leonard Catholic School on Long Island (JK–Grade 6); the public path continues through Kars on the Rideau for the middle grades and South Carleton High School in Richmond for Grades 9–12. Catchments follow your exact address and program — confirm in the board’s school locator before you buy.

How far is Manotick East from the village and from downtown?
The village is the next stop, not a trip: Bridge Street and Main Street’s shops, cafés and Dickinson Square are a few minutes across the river from most east-bank streets. Highway 416 is about ten minutes north, and downtown Ottawa is roughly half an hour outside rush hour. OC Transpo’s Route 186 links the village toward Barrhaven on a limited schedule, so Manotick East is honestly a driving neighbourhood — the trade is space and trees for the wheel.

Why do Manotick East listings span such a wide price range?
Because the district holds two markets on one map. At one end are older River Road homes and smaller shore properties; at the other are Rideau Forest estates — two-acre wooded lots carrying large custom homes that trade at the top of the Ottawa market. The live range on this page runs from the district’s entry point to its estate ceiling, and the median sits with the custom-home end. Price here is lot, trees, frontage and build quality — never just square footage.

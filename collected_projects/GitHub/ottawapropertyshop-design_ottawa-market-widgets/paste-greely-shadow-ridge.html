Shadow Ridge Estates — the community

Shadow Ridge Estates is Greely’s established estate subdivision, sitting between Old Prescott Road and Bank Street, south of Parkway Road — close enough to the village core to use it daily, far enough into the township grid to feel like the country. Its streets — Donwel Drive, Shadow Ridge Drive, Quinnfield Way, Mary Anne Drive, Waterdown Street and Shauna Crescent, with the newer-phase streets of Stonecrop Avenue, Calwood Avenue and Evening Shadow Avenue — carry detached estate homes on fully serviced lots, a different proposition from Greely’s usual well-and-septic acreage.

The exact streets of Shadow Ridge — every street inside the gold boundary belongs to the subdivision, and the listings at the top of this guide are drawn from inside that same boundary.

Shadow Ridge’s boundaries, approximately: Parkway Road runs along the north, the Old Prescott Road side of the township grid edges the west, and the community’s southern streets — the Calwood Avenue and Waterdown Street side — close the south. To the south-east, Shadow Ridge’s edge meets Lakewood Trails along Shoreway Drive and Lake Trail Road. Those boundaries are approximate: they follow the community’s own street footprint — the streets traced on the map above — rather than a surveyed line.

The brick entrance sign tells the subdivision’s founding story in its own words: this is a community that features ground-source heat pumps and energy-efficient construction — an energy-built subdivision, ahead of its time for a rural Ottawa village. In the middle of it sits the community park — playground, gazebo and split-rail fence — the neighbourhood’s front room.

The homes themselves span roughly two decades of building waves. The original developer, Donwel, built the first phases — the first registered phase dates to 2006 and is built out — and a second registered phase followed in 2014. Building never really stopped: DCR Phoenix is actively building the newest streets around Stonecrop Avenue and Calwood Avenue today, so an established estate home from the first wave and a brand-new build can sit streets apart inside the same subdivision — and more homes are still to come as the held lots release.

The communal system — Shadow Ridge’s servicing story

Almost every home in Greely runs on a private well and septic system. Shadow Ridge is the exception — together with Lakeland Meadows, it is the village’s only city-serviced pocket, on communal drinking-water and wastewater systems run by the City of Ottawa. The City treats the pair as one system: its development-charge by-law carries schedules for the “Shadow Ridge and Lakeland Meadows Communal Drinking Water and Wastewater Systems” by name.

The wastewater side works differently from a city sewer, and it is worth understanding before you buy. Each Shadow Ridge home has its own septic tank, sitting in a municipal easement; what leaves the tank flows through small sewers to a shared treatment facility — a peat filter and constructed wetland the City runs off Waterdown Street, near Old Prescott Road. The City pumps the tanks itself, on its own schedule. This is an active, maintained system, not a historical footnote: the City’s May 2026 notice for its Shadow Ridge sanitary sewer rehabilitation names Mary Anne Drive, Calwood Avenue, Waterdown Street and Stonecrop Avenue among the streets in the work, with construction through the summer and fall of 2026.

The drinking water comes from two communal wells with City treatment. The honest caveat is capacity: the water system has run at or near its rated limits, the wells were deepened in 2023, and a developer-funded upgrade to the water plant is under way. Until it lands, the remaining Phase 2 lots sit in a servicing reserve — they cannot be built on yet. Read that as a serviced subdivision whose growth is paced by its plant, not as a servicing failure: the homes that exist are fully serviced, and the pacing is part of what keeps them that way.

For a buyer, the servicing cuts both ways, honestly: there is no private well to test and no private septic bed to inspect, replace or budget for — the two big rural homework items shrink to understanding a tank the City pumps and a treatment system the City runs — but the capacity story is part of the subdivision’s future, and it is worth understanding before you buy. It is also the reason Shadow Ridge reads differently from the rest of Greely: serviced lots, a denser estate fabric, and a village address minutes from the core.

Living in Shadow Ridge

Daily life runs through the park at the subdivision’s heart and the village core about 2.0 km north — The Shoppes of Greely plaza on Bank Street for groceries and everyday services, the Greely Community Centre and the library branch on Meadow Drive, and Eva’s Chip Wagon up Bank Street when the season opens. Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) is the spine: downtown Ottawa is roughly half an hour north in good traffic. The honest transit picture: there is no regular OC Transpo route through the village — the one exception is the Thursday Shopper Route 304 — so Shadow Ridge is a driver’s address, and school buses, run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority, are the transit that matters to families here.

Shadow Ridge questions, answered

Is Shadow Ridge on city water and sewers?
On communal systems, yes — the City of Ottawa’s communal drinking-water and wastewater systems serve Shadow Ridge and Lakeland Meadows together, the village’s only city-serviced pocket. Drinking water comes from two communal wells with City treatment. For wastewater, each home has a septic tank in a municipal easement — the City pumps the tanks, and what leaves them is treated at a shared peat filter and constructed wetland the City operates. There is no private well to test and no private septic bed to inspect or replace on a Shadow Ridge purchase.

What kinds of homes are in Shadow Ridge?
Detached estate homes on fully serviced lots. The subdivision was built around energy efficiency — its own entrance sign notes the ground-source heat pumps and energy-efficient construction the community features — and the streets mix the established phases with newer-phase homes on Stonecrop Avenue, Calwood Avenue and Evening Shadow Avenue.

Which schools serve Shadow Ridge?
The designated paths are set out in the schools section above: Greely Elementary School and Castor Valley Elementary School on the public side, Osgoode Township High School for Grades 9 to 12, and St. Mary School to St. Mark Catholic High School on the Catholic side. Confirm any exact address in the board’s school locator before you buy.

How is Shadow Ridge different from Lakeland Meadows?
They are sister subdivisions that share one servicing framework — the City’s development-charge by-law names the “Shadow Ridge and Lakeland Meadows Communal Drinking Water and Wastewater Systems” together. Shadow Ridge is the established community west of the village core; Lakeland Meadows, at 1626 Old Prescott Road, is the newer lot community about 0.5 km from Shadow Ridge.

How far is Shadow Ridge from downtown Ottawa?
About 25 kilometres — roughly half an hour by car in good traffic, straight up Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31). There is no regular OC Transpo route through the village; the Thursday Shopper Route 304 is the one exception.

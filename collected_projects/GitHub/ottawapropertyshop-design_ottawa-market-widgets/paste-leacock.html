The Short Answer

Stephen Leacock Public School is an Ottawa-Carleton District School Board (OCDSB) elementary school at 25 Leacock Drive in Kanata (K2K 1S2 · 613-592-2261), teaching Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6 in Early French Immersion only — the school’s own site lists Early French Immersion as its program, and no English program runs at the school — with a school day that runs 08:00–14:30. The board publishes its boundary as a single entry — the Stephen Leacock Public School JK to 6 Early French Immersion Attendance Boundary on its official elementary boundary index, updated January 28, 2025 — and answers for one exact address at a time through its School Locator, listed as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. The path through this catchment is one clear line: Stephen Leacock Public School (Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6, Early French Immersion) → Earl of March Intermediate School (Grades 7 to 8) → Earl of March Secondary School (Grades 9 to 12).

The Catchment, From the Board’s Own Line

A catchment — the board calls it an attendance boundary — is the area a school draws its students from, and for Stephen Leacock three things about the line matter to a buyer. First, the board decides, not the map: the school at the end of the street is not evidence, and proximity is a hint, not an answer — the locator result on the exact address is. Second, this line belongs to a program, not just a building: it is the Early French Immersion boundary, and the same address carries a different elementary school for the English program — one address, two answers, separated by track. Third, lines move: boards review boundaries as neighbourhoods grow and enrolment shifts, which is why this series names the board source and its update date on every guide, and re-checks its catchments every September.

This guide’s boundary was cross-checked the way every guide in this series is: the board’s index entry was read against the school’s own site, and against the transportation source — OSTA, which runs home-to-school transportation for the OCDSB. One distinction is worth stating plainly: OSTA eligibility is a distance test applied inside the boundary — it is not the boundary itself.

Two Catchments on the Same Streets: Leacock and Johnston

Buyers comparing north Kanata guides notice something that looks like an error: this guide and the W. Erskine Johnston guide cover much of the same ground. It is not an error — it is how a program school works. The board publishes Leacock’s Early French Immersion line and Johnston’s English line as two separate boundaries, and across north Kanata they overlap: nearly two-thirds of the Leacock catchment also sits inside Johnston’s English catchment. A family on one of those shared streets is not choosing between two schools on a map — the program chooses the school. Early French Immersion runs at Leacock; the English program runs at Johnston. The school’s own street makes the point in one address range: homes on Leacock Drive itself — the street the school stands on — are inside the Leacock line for Early French Immersion and inside the Johnston line for English, which is why the Johnston guide tells the same story from the other side. Is Stephen Leacock an English-program school?

If my child needs a bus, does living in the catchment mean they get one?
Not automatically. Inside the walk zone, students walk. OSTA’s “Do I Qualify?” check answers for one address at a time, and the board’s transportation page sets the rules out.

This week in Heritage Park

Homes for sale: 17 active listings. That is down from the 41 in my Heritage Park guide's October 2 snapshot — twenty-four fewer homes in four weeks, in a pocket where resale supply normally turns over one household move at a time.

Median asking price: $596,000. The middle of the Heritage Park set now sits under the $600,000 line. Against the guide's median of $608,000, that is a dip of $12,000 in four weeks — just under two per cent.

Average asking price: $603,905. The average is up $3,072 from the guide's $600,833 — about half of one per cent — and it now sits above the median. The guide's October read had the average just under the median; with the bottom of the range gone, the relationship has flipped, and the average is carried by the homes that stayed.

The range: $399,000 to $899,900. Here is the shape of the change: the entry point jumped — the low end is up $79,100 from the guide's $319,900 floor, because the homes at the bottom of the range are the ones that left — while the ceiling did not move at all, $899,900 then and $899,900 now.

The Barrhaven picture: Barrhaven-wide, 242 freehold homes are listed today, and Heritage Park's 17 make it one of the smaller pockets in the count. Its $596,000 median sits $28,900 below the Barrhaven-wide median of $624,900 — and that gap is a mix story, not a verdict on value: Heritage Park's set blends walk-up apartment condos and townhouses in with its detached homes, while the Barrhaven-wide figures blend in every pocket and product type, condo-heavy areas and the newest subdivisions at the suburb's south end included.

What I make of it: a shelf that more than halved, with a higher floor and a slightly softer middle says the thinning was concentrated at the bottom — the entry tier is where choice shrank fastest. What the aggregates cannot tell us is whether the listings that left sold, expired or were withdrawn; twenty-four net departures also means new listings did join along the way. If you are buying in Heritage Park, the entry tier is where competition tightened most, and these are 25-to-35-year-old houses, so budget for the inspection to matter — roof, foundation and mechanicals. If you are selling, a shelf less than half the size of early October means your home is compared against fewer of its neighbours than it would have been four weeks ago.

Family streets, linear parks and one new edge

Heritage Park was built mostly through the 1990s and early 2000s, and it still reads that way: the large Fraser Fields community by Holitzner Homes and Tartan Homes — streets like Harthill Way and Broxburn — plus Minto's Haven-model townhomes, Caivan-built detached homes and the low-rise walk-up apartment condos, Tartan's Java at Fraser Fields among them. The pocket's signature green feature is unlike anything else in Barrhaven: the Harthill Way Linear Park and the Fraser Fields Parkette, both created from the decommissioned former Strandherd Drive alignment. The one new edge is Minto's Riversbend at Harmony, actively selling townhomes and detached homes at the pocket's boundary — new construction joining a pocket that was finished a generation ago.

Getting around from Heritage Park

Road access is this pocket's edge: it is the closest of Barrhaven's three northern pockets to Highway 416 and to the Costco on Strandherd Drive, and the off-peak drive downtown runs about 25 to 30 minutes. One honest note from my guide: the VIA Rail corridor crosses Strandherd Road just south of the pocket — worth knowing if you are sensitive to train noise near the crossing.

On transit, local routes loop the pocket's own streets. Route 70 runs from Fallowfield Station to Limebank Station through the middle of Heritage Park — down Tartan Drive, Berrigan Drive and Queensbury Drive — tying the pocket to Line 2 of the O-Train. Route 73 works the Longfields Drive and Mountshannon Drive corridor between Fallowfield and Limebank, extended to Tunney's Pasture in peak periods. Route 99 links Barrhaven Centre Station to Limebank along Longfields Drive and Chapman Mills Drive, and route 173 runs from Barrhaven Centre to the Citigate business area along Strandherd Drive, past the Costco. School routes layer on top at bell times — the 673 to École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois and Longfields-Davidson Heights, the 671 to John McCrae and the 690 to St. Mother Teresa among them.

The fast ride is one stop away: Strandherd Station, on the Southwest Transitway at Strandherd Drive and Greenbank Road, pairs the frequent route 75 — around the clock to Tunney's Pasture and the Line 1 connection downtown — with a park and ride of more than 300 spaces. Peak-period Connexion routes 275 (from Half Moon Bay) and 279 (from Manotick) stop there too, running to Tunney's Pasture in the morning peak and back in the afternoon.

Schools around Heritage Park

The school cluster is the headline here — the strongest of the three northern pockets, and deeper than one school per board. The full bench serving Heritage Park addresses, board by board:

  • Public English (OCDSB): Mary Honeywell Elementary School (kindergarten to grade 6) is the pocket's own elementary school, dual-track with an English program and Early French Immersion. Its graduates move to Cedarview Middle School (grades 7 and 8, English and French immersion) and then to John McCrae Secondary School (grades 9 to 12). Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School (grades 7 to 12) stands just east of the pocket, and some Heritage Park streets feed it instead — one of the catchment lines worth checking on your exact street.

  • Catholic English (OCSB): St. Luke Catholic Elementary School (kindergarten to grade 6) on Mountshannon Drive, then St. Mother Teresa High School (grades 7 to 12) right on Longfields Drive — pictured below.

  • French Catholic (CECCE): École élémentaire catholique Pierre-Elliott-Trudeau (kindergarten to grade 6) on Longfields Drive, then École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard (grades 7 to 12), also on Longfields Drive — the French Catholic pair sits minutes from the pocket's streets.

  • French public (CEPEO): École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean (kindergarten to grade 8) just east in Davidson Heights, then École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois (grades 7 to 12), Barrhaven's French public secondary school, which runs the International Baccalaureate program.

  • Private: Ottawa Christian School (junior kindergarten to grade 8) on Tartan Drive, inside the pocket — pictured further down.

Catchments turn on the exact street, so check yours on each board's school-locator tool before you buy — my Heritage Park guide walks through the bench in more depth, board by board.

Parks, play and daily life

The green network is layered. At the top, Clarke Fields Park — pictured at the top of this roundup — is the pocket's sports and festival ground: soccer fields through the summer and Barrhaven's multi-day Canada Day festival with fireworks, the suburb's signature summer event. Through the middle run the Harthill Way Linear Park and the Fraser Fields Parkette, the green corridor left behind when Strandherd Drive was realigned — a walking spine through Fraser Fields rather than a single destination park. At the edges: Weybridge Park, beside Mary Honeywell Elementary School, carries a soccer field and a baseball diamond, and at the pocket's southwest, Clarity Park adds a splash pad and playground, with Barrhaven Mews Park next door. The Jock River trails sit within walking distance of the pocket's south end. The big indoor draw is a short drive south: the Minto Recreation Complex—Barrhaven on Cambrian Road, with its 25-metre pool, two NHL-size rinks, gymnasium and indoor walking track.

Churches, plazas and the daily run

Faith life sits right on the pocket's Strandherd edge: Haven Baptist Church and the Nepean Seventh-day Adventist Church stand side by side on Strandherd Drive at the Fraser Fields end of the pocket, and Longfields Community Church — a congregation that worshipped in school gyms for 25 years before opening its own building — serves the area from Woodroffe Avenue, just east. Between them, most of the congregations a Heritage Park family would look for are within a few minutes of the front door.

The daily run splits two ways. The big shop is about three minutes away at Barrhaven Town Centre and the RioCan Marketplace — groceries, big-box and the cinema — and the Costco sits on the pocket's own Strandherd corridor. The small shop stays closer: Cedarview Square, the neighbourhood plaza at Strandherd Drive and Cedarview Road, carries the Guardian Pharmacy, two childcare centres and the local services; the Strandherd Plaza at Strandherd Drive and Greenbank Road is anchored by its Shoppers Drug Mart; and the Tartan Drive strip inside the pocket — the Royal Oak pub, the V!VA Barrhaven retirement community and Ottawa Christian School — is where the neighbourhood's everyday life gathers.

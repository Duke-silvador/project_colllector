The Catchment, Street by Street

Colonel By’s boundary is one of the easiest in Ottawa to read, because the streets do the work. The north edge runs along the Ottawa River on the Sir George-Étienne Cartier Parkway. The east edge follows Shefford Road down through Beacon Hill South, with the boundary stepping around the townhouse courts off Lassiter Terrace and Loyola Avenue. The south edge is Montréal Road. The west edge is Blair Road, from the river down to Montréal Road. Highway 174 crosses the catchment diagonally, and Ogilvie Road — the school’s own street — runs through the middle of it.

Inside the line sit the streets families ask about by name: the Rothwell Heights lots along the river, the crescents of Beacon Hill North — Naskapi Drive, Kender Avenue, La Vérendrye Drive — and the family streets of Beacon Hill South, Eastvale Drive, Loyola Avenue and the Marquis Avenue courts among them. That mix is the catchment’s character in one paragraph: a handful of large river-side properties at the top of the market, a deep bench of detached family homes on quiet crescents, and condominium enclaves along Ogilvie Road, Montréal Road and Lassiter Terrace holding the entry price far below the detached market.

Colonel By Secondary School — The Facts

Colonel By is a non-semestered secondary school of roughly 1,100 students in Beacon Hill, and it has a reputation that travels well beyond Gloucester: strong academics, a full French Immersion program, and specialist programs that draw applications from across the board. Because the school day runs on a non-semestered timetable, students carry their courses across the full year — a detail families transferring from semestered schools notice right away.

A note on program tracks. Colonel By is also one of the Ottawa-Carleton board’s International Baccalaureate schools. The IB Diploma Programme is an application program track: students apply and are admitted through the board’s application process, and IB admission is separate from the attendance boundary on this page. The same applies to the school’s Specialist High Skills Major in Arts and Culture: Digital Media. Living inside the Colonel By catchment designates Colonel By as your teenager’s high school for the regular English and French Immersion programs — program-track places are decided by application, not by address.

The School Pathway Into Colonel By

Colonel By is the top of a Beacon Hill pathway. The elementary schools that feed this catchment — Robert Hopkins Public School and Le Phare Elementary School among them, with Carson Grove Elementary School in the same feeder set — send their graduates to Henry Munro Middle School, and Henry Munro sends its Grade 8 graduates on to Colonel By. The designated path for an address inside this catchment reads as one clear line:

Elementary school in the Beacon Hill feeder set → Henry Munro Middle School, Grades 6 to 8 → Colonel By Secondary School, Grades 9 to 12.

That single line is why the boundary matters at purchase time. A family buying in Beacon Hill is not buying four years of high school — it is buying the whole run, middle school included, without a move in between. Families whose children are still in elementary school should read the guide for Henry Munro’s feeders alongside this one, and confirm the elementary designation for their exact address, because the elementary lines inside Gloucester are drawn school by school.

The Fraser Rating — One Measure, In Context

The Fraser Institute rated Colonel By 8.9 out of 10 in its 2025 Report Card on Ontario secondary schools — 23rd of 747 schools, with a five-year average of 9.2. That places Colonel By among the highest-rated public secondary schools in Ottawa, and it is a large part of why Beacon Hill homes hold their value the way they do.

Use the rating the way it deserves to be used: as a measure of past academic results at the school, not a promise about any one child. What the number cannot show is the non-semestered timetable, the immersion program, the teachers your teenager will actually get, or the walk down Ogilvie Road every morning. Visit the school, walk the streets around it, and let the rating be one input — a strong one — among several.

Colonel By or Cairine Wilson? The East-End Choice

East of Shefford Road, Gloucester turns into Convent Glen, and the designated Ottawa-Carleton secondary school changes. Cairine Wilson Secondary School serves the Convent Glen and Orléans addresses east of this catchment, and its guide is coming in this series. The practical difference for a buyer is simple: two similar east-end neighbourhoods, two different designated high schools, and a boundary that runs down one road. If the high school is the reason for the move, the address check comes before the offer — every time.

One honest note in Colonel By’s favour: its IB Diploma Programme is open by application to students across the board, so a family that settles east of the line is not shut out of Colonel By’s best-known program — but application is a process with deadlines, while a designated seat inside this catchment is simply yours by address.

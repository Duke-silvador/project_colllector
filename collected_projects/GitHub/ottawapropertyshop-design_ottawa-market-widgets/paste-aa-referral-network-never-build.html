Why Most Referrals Happen by Accident

A favour is not a network. A network is a short list of agents you already know, in places your clients move to, with paperwork agreed before a name changes hands.

Think about the last client who told you they were moving: a job transfer to Alberta, a parent downsizing to be near family in British Columbia, newcomers arriving in Ottawa with a home still to sell back home. Each move holds two transactions — and most agents only ever see one.

The outgoing half leaks away. The agent picks a name from a directory, makes one hopeful call, and never hears what happened. No agreement in writing, no update at closing, no relationship for next time. That is a coin toss with your client's move riding on it.

A built network works the other way. You choose partners before a client needs them, and you know how fast they respond and which neighbourhoods they actually work. When the call comes, you are not searching. You are introducing.

How Referral Fees and Referral Structure Actually Work

A referral is a written agreement between brokerages, made before the introduction. The referring agent is paid an agreed share of the receiving agent's commission after closing.

  • Agreement first. Before any name is shared, both agents agree terms in writing, on a referral agreement signed through their brokerages. No agreement, no referral.

  • Introduction second. You introduce the client directly and say why you chose this agent. A warm introduction converts; a forwarded phone number usually does not.

  • The receiving agent does the work. They serve the client and earn the commission in their own market, under their own licence and split.

  • Payment at closing. The referral fee is a percentage of the receiving side's gross commission, paid brokerage to brokerage once the deal closes and the commission is received. No closing, no fee.

There is no single standard percentage — the rate is negotiated case by case and written into the agreement. Build the Network Before You Need It

Pick the markets your clients actually move to, vet a few agents in each, agree terms in advance, and record every introduction.

  • Map your real corridors. From your last three years of clients: the GTA, Alberta, British Columbia, the Ottawa Valley — and, for agents serving newcomer communities, the countries those families came from.

  • Vet for response time first. Call the agent yourself. If your call takes two days to come back, so will your client's.

  • Agree terms while nothing is at stake. Percentage, update rhythm, and what happens if the client does not close.

  • Track every introduction. Date, client, market, receiving agent, outcome — that record tells you whether the network is growing.

  • Feed the network both ways. Be the agent others think of when a client says Ottawa. Networks that only take, stop receiving.

Can I refer a client to an agent in another country if I am not licensed there?

Yes — that is exactly what a referral is. You introduce the client to a locally licensed agent under a written referral agreement between the brokerages, and that agent does the work. Your role is the trusted introduction and the follow-up.

What should I check before I refer a client to an agent I have never worked with?

Three things: the agent is licensed and active in the exact market; they respond to you the way you want them to respond to your client; and terms are agreed in writing before the introduction. If any one fails, keep looking — your client will remember your name on that move, not theirs.

The Quiet Payoff, Stated Honestly

Referrals will rarely be your biggest income line. What they do is turn moves you were going to lose anyway into served clients, returned goodwill, and a second transaction.

It is not passive or guaranteed income — clients change plans and deals fall through. What the network does is compound trust. The family you guided through both ends of an international move does not shop for an agent next time, and the agent who received three clean introductions from you sends the fourth Ottawa-bound client your way unasked. That reciprocity, built inside one brokerage reaching 29 countries, is the asset. Start before the phone rings.

What the 13% Actually Is: Two Programs Wearing One Coat

Ontario charges 13% HST on a new home: a 5% federal part and an 8% provincial part. For agreements signed in the current window, two programs together return both parts. The Ontario Enhanced New Housing Rebate (ENHR) returns 100% of the 8% provincial part — up to $80,000 — under Ontario Regulation 196/26. The Ontario New Homes Affordability Payment (ONHAP) returns the 5% federal part — up to $50,000 — as a federally cost-shared top-up under the same regulation, administered through CRA Notice 346. Eight plus five is thirteen: on a home at $1,000,000, the two together are worth up to $130,000.

That is also why the most common description of this program is wrong. There is no federal 13% rebate. Ottawa did not create one giant federal program — Ontario built the 8% rebate, and the 5% beside it is a cost-shared top-up that covers the federal part. Anyone advertising a second, separate federal cheque on top of the 13% is double-counting the same money.

The shape of the enhanced rebate by price:

• Up to $1,000,000 — the full 13% comes back: 100% of the provincial part (to $80,000) plus 100% of the federal part (to $50,000).

• $1,000,000 to $1,500,000 — the rebate holds flat at the $130,000 ceiling: $80,000 provincial plus $50,000 federal.

• $1,500,000 to $1,850,000 — the enhanced rebate phases down in a straight line, from $130,000 toward the old $24,000 Ontario rebate.

• $1,850,000 and above — no enhanced rebate; only the standard Ontario rebate of up to $24,000 remains.

And the conditions that decide whether any of it applies to you:

• The agreement date rules everything — your purchase agreement must be signed on or after April 1, 2026 and on or before March 31, 2027. Terminating an older agreement and re-signing it to land inside the window does not work — the anti-avoidance rules catch exactly that.

• Construction deadlines — for a builder purchase, construction must begin on or before December 31, 2028 and be substantially complete on or before December 31, 2031. Owner-built homes run on a shorter clock: construction begins inside the window and finishes before 2030.

• You (or a relation) must live in it — the home must be the primary place of residence of you or a qualifying relation. Pure investment purchases use the rental rebate route instead (below).

• Any buyer qualifies — first-time, move-up, downsizer. The enhanced rebate is not a first-time buyer program; the first-time buyer programs sit beside it and matter most outside the window.

The Rebates That Were Already There

Long before 2026, two standard new housing rebates existed, and they still do — they are simply too small to matter much at Ottawa prices. The federal new housing rebate returns 36% of the 5% federal part, to a maximum of $6,300, and it phases out between $350,000 and $450,000 of value — above $450,000 it is gone entirely. The Ontario new housing rebate returns 75% of the 8% provincial part, to a maximum of $24,000 (or $16,080 where no HST was paid on the land). Combined ceiling: $30,300 — and at a typical Ottawa new-build price, the realistic figure is the Ontario $24,000 alone. That is the baseline the enhanced rebate replaced for in-window agreements: $24,000 became up to $130,000, for one year of signings.

First-Time Buyers: The Other $130,000

First-time buyers have their own, longer-running stack. The federal First-Time Home Buyers' GST rebate — in effect March 12, 2026, for agreements on or after March 20, 2025 — returns 100% of the 5% federal part on a first new home up to $1,000,000, worth up to $50,000, then phases out in a straight line to $1,500,000 (CRA's own example: a $1,250,000 home receives $25,000). Ontario mirrors it with a first-time buyer rebate of the 8% provincial part — the full 8% to $1,000,000, worth up to $80,000, phasing down on the same line. Together: up to $130,000 for a qualifying first-time buyer, on agreements running to 2030 — long after the enhanced window closes.

How do the two stacks interact for a first-time buyer signing inside the enhanced window? By one simple rule: the federal side is covered once, never twice. The Ontario top-up (ONHAP) is reduced by whatever federal rebate you are entitled to — it fills the gap in the 5%, it does not duplicate it. The Ontario 8% side is covered by the enhanced rebate in the window. Either route lands a first-time buyer at the same place on a home to $1,000,000 — the full 13% — and total relief can never exceed the HST actually paid. Where the first-time buyer programs really earn their keep is outside the window: sign before April 1, 2026 or after March 31, 2027, and they are the only route to the full amount.

The Worked Example: A $900,000 New Home, Line by Line

Take a $900,000 new home in Ottawa, bought from a builder by an owner-occupier, agreement signed inside the window. HST at 13% is $117,000 — $45,000 federal, $72,000 provincial. The enhanced rebate returns the whole provincial part ($72,000), and the Ontario top-up returns the whole federal part ($45,000). Total rebate: $117,000 — every dollar of the HST. How the Money Moves: Assign It to the Builder, or Claim It Yourself

Route A — assign it at closing (what most buyers do). You sign the rebate forms — CRA Form GST190 with the Ontario schedule RC7190-ON — and hand them to the builder. The builder credits the full rebate amount against your closing balance and claims it from CRA after closing. You never pay the HST portion the rebate covers: on the $900,000 example, your price money is $900,000, not $1,017,000. CRA's own worked example in Notice 346 runs a $1,000,000 home through exactly this route for the full $130,000 credit. The risk to read before you sign: most builder agreements make you liable to repay the rebate if CRA later denies the claim, even where the price was advertised after-rebate. Your lawyer reads that clause — it is one of the most expensive paragraphs in the agreement.

Route B — claim it yourself after closing. You pay the full price plus HST at closing ($1,017,000 in the example), then file Form GST190 with RC7190-ON to CRA yourself and the rebate is paid to you. Same money, later, with a financing gap in between — most buyers only take this route when the builder will not accept an assignment. Owner-built homes always claim directly (Form GST191 is the owner-built equivalent). Either way, the eligibility is tested the same way: agreement date, primary residence, construction deadlines.

Net of HST: The Advertised Price Already Spent Your Rebate

Now connect this to the sticker prices. When a builder advertises a price "net of HST", that price already assumes Route A — it assumes you qualify, that you assign the rebate, and that CRA pays it. The $900,000 column in the worked example is the net-of-HST column. If you turn out not to qualify — wrong agreement date, the home is not your primary residence — the rebate does not exist, and the difference lands back on your closing statement. First-Time Buyer, Move-Up Buyer, Investor: Who Gets What

• Owner-occupiers, any history — sign inside the window and the enhanced stack applies in full, first home or fifth. Outside the window, only the standard rebates remain ($24,000 Ontario at Ottawa prices).

• First-time buyers — covered in the window, and uniquely protected outside it: the federal GST rebate plus Ontario's matching 8% rebate run on agreements to 2030, up to $130,000 under $1,000,000. Neither you nor your spouse can have lived in a home either of you owned in the current year or the four before it.

• Buying for a relation — a parent buying a new home as the primary residence of an adult child (or another qualifying relation) can claim the owner-occupier rebates; the residence test follows the person who will live there.

• Investors and landlords — a home bought purely to rent out does not qualify for the owner-occupier rebates. The rental route is the new residential rental property rebate, and for qualifying new rental supply an enhanced rental rebate returns up to 100% of the 8% provincial part — up to $80,000 per unit — plus the top-up. Long-term leases only: the tenancy must be a year or more; short-term rental use is excluded, and HST self-supply rules apply when a builder-landlord first rents a new unit. This route has its own forms and deadlines — it needs advice before the agreement is signed, not after.

The March 31, 2027 Deadline

Everything enhanced about the 13% HST rebate Ontario new construction buyers are counting on hangs on one date. Agreements signed on or before March 31, 2027 can carry up to $130,000 of relief; an agreement signed April 1, 2027 falls back to the standard rebates — at Ottawa prices, $24,000 — unless you are a first-time buyer, whose programs run to 2030. That is a difference of up to $106,000 decided by a signature date. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Ontario HST rebate on new homes, in plain terms?

For agreements signed April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027, Ontario returns the full 13% HST on a new home up to $1,000,000 — up to $130,000 — through two programs that work as one: the Ontario Enhanced New Housing Rebate covers the 8% provincial part (to $80,000), and the Ontario New Homes Affordability Payment covers the 5% federal part (to $50,000). The legal sources are CRA Notice 346 and Ontario Regulation 196/26.

Is the 13% a federal program?

No. The 8% is Ontario's enhanced rebate under Ontario Regulation 196/26. The 5% beside it is a cost-shared top-up that covers the federal part — it is not a second federal rebate, and the two cannot be claimed as 13% plus another 5%.

Do I have to be a first-time buyer to get the enhanced rebate?

No. Any buyer who will live in the home (or buy it for a relation who will) qualifies, first home or fifth. First-time buyers have separate programs of their own, which matter most for agreements outside the window.

How much do I get back on a $900,000 new home?

All of the HST: $117,000. The HST on $900,000 is $45,000 federal plus $72,000 provincial; the enhanced rebate returns the $72,000 and the top-up returns the $45,000. The worked example above shows both routes the money can travel.

How do I actually claim the rebate — do I pay the HST first?

Usually not. Most buyers assign the rebate to the builder at closing: you sign Form GST190 and the Ontario schedule RC7190-ON, the builder credits the amount on your closing statement, and the builder claims it from CRA. If you claim it yourself instead, you pay the full HST at closing and file the same forms with CRA after closing.

What forms does the rebate use?

For a home bought from a builder: CRA Form GST190 (GST/HST New Housing Rebate Application for Houses Purchased from a Builder) with Form RC7190-ON, the Ontario schedule. Owner-built homes use Form GST191. Your lawyer prepares them as part of the closing package.

Can I stack the first-time buyer GST rebate on top of the Ontario rebate?

The programs combine, but they never pay the same dollar twice. The Ontario top-up is reduced by whatever federal rebate you are entitled to, so the federal 5% is covered once — by the federal rebate, the top-up, or a blend. With the Ontario 8% covered by the enhanced rebate (in the window) or Ontario's first-time buyer rebate (to 2030), a first-time buyer still reaches the full 13% on a home to $1,000,000.

What happens if my new home costs more than $1 million?

The enhanced rebate holds at the $130,000 ceiling from $1,000,000 to $1,500,000, then phases down in a straight line to $24,000 at $1,850,000. Above $1,850,000 only the standard Ontario rebate of up to $24,000 remains. The first-time buyer rebates phase out earlier — they reach zero at $1,500,000.

I am buying the home to rent it out. Do I get a rebate?

Not the owner-occupier rebates — those require the home to be your primary residence or a relation's. Rental purchases use the new residential rental property rebate, and qualifying new rental supply can use an enhanced rental rebate of up to 100% of the 8% provincial part — up to $80,000 per unit — plus the top-up. Long-term leases of a year or more only; short-term rentals are excluded. Get advice before signing, because the route is chosen in the agreement.

What happens after March 31, 2027?

The enhanced rebate ends for new agreements. A repeat buyer signing April 1, 2027 is back to the standard rebates — at Ottawa prices, the Ontario $24,000. First-time buyers keep their route to up to $130,000 on agreements to 2030. If you are weighing a new build, the agreement date is now one of the biggest numbers in the deal.

Stonebridge is Barrhaven’s golf-course family pocket (AMP area 7708 — Barrhaven — Stonebridge) — centred on Jockvale Road and Greenbank Road and built around a figure-eight championship course that winds between the crescents. It takes in Minto, Mattamy, Monarch, Uniform, Tamarack, Cardel and Phoenix homes, and it holds Barrhaven’s deepest bench of fairway-backing executive homes. Its signature Stonebridge Golf Club is the 18-hole public course the whole community wraps around. And it is the closest of Barrhaven’s southern pockets to the Minto Recreation Complex – Barrhaven.

Stonebridge Golf Club — the community’s front yard

Most suburbs have a park at their heart; Stonebridge has a golf course. The Stonebridge Golf Club at 68 Hawktree Ridge is a public 18-hole course designed by Ted Baker and later redesigned by Doug Carrick, a certified Audubon Cooperative Sanctuary with a clubhouse, bistro and patio overlooking the 18th hole. The course snakes through the community in a figure-eight, so a large share of lots back directly onto fairways — no rear neighbours, unobstructed green views, and a quiet that production subdivisions cannot copy. Streets like Kilbirnie Drive and Hawktree Ridge carry the premium fairway product; Monarch spends real money every year planting trees to soften errant-ball landings, which tells you how completely the homes and the course are woven together. Living here means the fairway is part of the address — price it, and enjoy it, accordingly.

Schools in Stonebridge, Ottawa (7708)

Adrienne Clarkson Elementary School (JK–6, 170 Stoneway Drive) sits inside Stonebridge itself — triple-track (Regular English, Early French Immersion and Middle French Immersion), on a large property beside Stonecrest Park with shared soccer pitches, baseball diamonds, play structures and a community rink; the Children’s Village at Stoneway runs an extended-day program inside the school. St. Cecilia Catholic School (3490 Cambrian Road) is the Catholic elementary option at the community’s southwest edge, beside the Minto Recreation Complex. Secondary options named for the area are John McCrae Secondary School (103 Malvern Drive), St. Mother Teresa High School (440 Longfields Drive) and St. Joseph High School (3333 Greenbank Road). Commute and Transit from Stonebridge, Ottawa

By car, downtown Ottawa runs about 25–30 minutes off-peak, with Greenbank Road and Prince of Wales Drive as the main routes out and Highway 416 reached via Strandherd Drive. Transit is edge-based: the Frequent Route 75 runs along Greenbank Road on the community’s western edge, Route 70 serves Stoneway Drive, and the 600-series school routes cover the interior streets. The nearest Park & Rides are Strandherd (3680 Strandherd Drive) and Fallowfield, which also carries the VIA Rail station. Interior-crescent buyers should check their exact stop before counting on a walk-to-bus commute — in Stonebridge, the walk to the bus varies street by street.

Parks, the Jock River and Recreation in Stonebridge

The Stonebridge Trail — 24 acres of walking trail along the Jock River on the community’s north edge — is the green spine: wildlife watching, river air, and an off-fairway escape minutes from every crescent. Kilbirnie Park and W.C. Levesque Park are the two major parks, with large fields and play structures; Stonecrest Park sits beside Adrienne Clarkson Elementary with shared fields and a splash pad across Stoneway Drive, and the Stonebridge Community Association — Barrhaven’s most active — readies the Kilbirnie outdoor rink each winter and runs the craft shows, the Artists of Stonebridge “Paint the Town” art show and sale, and kids’ sports leagues. At the southwest edge, the Minto Recreation Complex – Barrhaven (3500 Cambrian Road) adds a 25-metre pool, two NHL-sized arenas, a gymnasium with a walking track, a fitness centre and the Quinn’s Pointe artificial-turf field — the suburb’s biggest recreation complex, at Stonebridge’s doorstep.

The Denis Bergin House — Stonebridge’s 1892 Landmark

Stonebridge’s oldest landmark stands at 3697 Jockvale Road, on the neighbourhood’s western edge: the Denis Bergin House. Denis Bergin emigrated from Tipperary, Ireland in 1839, returned to Canada for good in 1842, and farmed 150 acres here with his wife and their ten children — seven sons and three daughters. When the family’s first frame house was destroyed by fire, they built the present house in 1892: a two-storey Gothic Revival farmhouse, wood-frame and clapboard, resting on a two-foot-thick foundation of locally quarried stone and finished with elaborate gingerbread trim on its steep gables. The Bergins worked this land for more than a century — Denis’s son Joe lived in the house until 1974 — and in 1991 the former City of Nepean designated it a heritage property. That is why an 1892 Irish farmhouse still stands at the edge of Stonebridge today, a reminder that long before the fairways, this was Jock River farm country.

Housing Stock in Stonebridge — Executive Detached, Towns and the Bungalow Niche

Stonebridge was master-developed by Monarch from 2000 across roughly 700 acres and some 3,400 homes, with the bulk built through the 2000s by Minto, Mattamy, Monarch, Uniform, Tamarack, Cardel and Phoenix. The stock reads in three tiers. At the top, executive detached homes — many of them four-bedroom, double-garage builds backing onto the fairways on quiet winding crescents. In the middle, townhouses on those same crescents: the realistic entry into the golf-course community. And the tier buyers underestimate until they tour one: the bungalows, main-floor-primary homes on generous frontages that give downsizers single-level living inside a golf-course community — a combination that barely exists elsewhere in Barrhaven. The Links, a Monarch-built stacked-condo enclave on Urbancrest Private beside the club, is the pocket’s one condo exception. Two Stonebridge specifics belong in every buyer’s homework. The first is the Stonebridge Special Area Levy — about $48 per $100K of assessed value, a cost line scheduled to end in October 2029; know it before you compare carrying costs. The second is the long-running question of development on parts of the golf-course lands: if a fairway view is why you are buying a specific home, ask about the current status before you sign.

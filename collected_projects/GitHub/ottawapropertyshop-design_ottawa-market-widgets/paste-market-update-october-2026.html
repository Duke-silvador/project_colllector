This Ottawa real estate market update reads October 2026 as a month of small relief on the surface and a stubborn gap underneath. At the city level, the Ottawa housing market eased a little from September — the median home sold in 25.5 days instead of 27, and sellers held 97.5% of their asking price. Against last October, though, the Ottawa market stats stayed soft: 1,048 sales, down 10.7%, with inventory up to 4.4 months. What October really shows is a city of separate markets — three property types and seven submarkets moving at different speeds — and Ottawa home prices feeling that split unevenly.

Summary of Findings

  • Sales: 1,048 homes sold, down 10.7% from last October — though the month itself firmed slightly from September.

  • Prices: average $678,512 (down 4.5%) and median $622,000 (down 3.9%) year over year; the composite benchmark price sits at $616,900, down 1.3%.

  • Sale-to-list: sellers captured 97.5% of list price — unchanged from September, a shade under last October’s 97.9%.

  • Speed: a median of 25.5 days on market, faster than September’s 27 days and close to last October’s 25.

  • Inventory: 4.4 months of supply citywide, up from 3.7 a year ago, on 4,582 active listings.

  • The pressure point is apartments: 7.4 months of inventory and a benchmark of $377,500, down 5.3% year over year.

  • The suburbs carried the month: 753 sales — 71.9% of all Ottawa sales — with the firmest conditions in Suburb East, at 3.5 months of inventory.

Recommendations to Ottawa Buyers

  • Apartments offer real negotiation room. At 7.4 months of inventory, this is the deepest buyer’s leverage in the city right now.

  • Do not expect the same leverage on a well-priced suburban freehold. Those homes are still selling in roughly 25 days, at 97.5% of asking.

  • Refresh your pre-approval. The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate to 2.5% on October 28 — borrowing costs have moved since many pre-approvals were written.

  • Watch November 5. The federal budget lands this week, and its housing and staffing details matter more in Ottawa than almost anywhere else in Ontario.

Recommendations to Ottawa Sellers

  • Price to the 97.5% reality. The average Ottawa home sold for 97.5% of its list price in October. An asking price set above the recent comparable sales mostly buys extra days on market, not extra money.

  • Your property type sets your market. A suburban single-family home at 4.0 months of inventory plays by different rules than an apartment at 7.4 — price and plan for the segment you are actually in.

  • Apartment sellers need the sharpest pricing and the most patience. With 7.4 months of inventory and a 31.3% sales-to-new-listings ratio, buyers have choices — and they know it.

  • The suburbs still move. Well-priced freeholds in the suburban markets sold in about 25 days at the median in October; condition and presentation still pay there.

For everyone, the citywide headline is the least useful number in this report. Recent sales and competing listings in the same neighbourhood and the same property type are what set a realistic price, a realistic offer and a realistic timeline — and as the market heads into winter, those local differences are what will decide outcomes. The full breakdown follows.

Ottawa Market Stats — the Citywide Picture

The headline tension is between the two comparisons. Month over month, October steadied: the sales-to-new-listings ratio held at 44.0%, the sale-to-list ratio did not move, and homes sold a day and a half faster than in September. Year over year, the gap is the story — sales down 10.7%, dollar volume down 14.6% to $711,080,780, and months of inventory up from 3.7 to 4.4, as active listings climbed 6.4% to 4,582 while new listings slipped 1.2% to 2,382.

October did not change the market’s direction; it confirmed a market that is cooler than last year without being stalled — homes priced to the current numbers are still selling in under a month. Where the coolness sits, though, is anything but even, as the breakdowns by property type show next.

Source: Ottawa Real Estate Board (OREB), MLS® Residential Market Activity and MLS® HPI reports for October 2026, prepared by the Canadian Real Estate Association.

Sales and Inventory by Property Type

The flow behind the inventory numbers matters. Single-family homes drew 1,202 of October’s 2,382 new listings and carried 2,250 active listings at month end — the deepest shelf of the three types, yet still only 4.0 months of supply, because 558 of them sold. Townhouses took 740 new listings against 350 sales, holding 1,247 actives and the tightest supply of any type at 3.6 months. Apartments show the imbalance plainly: 367 new listings arrived while only 115 sold, leaving 853 actives — 7.4 months of supply, the widest gap between what is listed and what is selling anywhere in this report.

Read against the benchmark prices, single-family homes remain the firmest of the three types: a benchmark of $699,200, down just 0.8% year over year. Townhouses sit in the middle — a benchmark of $539,800, down 4.3%: a segment softening faster than detached homes, but still moving.

Apartments are the clearest pressure point in this Ottawa real estate market update — a trend seen throughout 2026. Apartment sales fell 23.3% year over year, and the sales-to-new-listings ratio was just 31.3%. The apartment benchmark of $377,500 is down 5.3% year over year and 0.9% from September. Across all types, the composite benchmark is $616,900, down 1.1% from September and 1.3% year over year.

Ottawa Home Prices by Property Type

What buyers actually paid in October brackets the benchmarks. Single-family homes averaged $823,204, with a median of $749,950 — the average pulled up by the high end — down 3.8% and 2.4% year over year. Townhouses averaged $528,732 against a median of $532,814, the median sitting above the average, down 5.0% and 6.5%. Apartments averaged $430,733 against a median of $375,000, down 1.5% and 4.8% — the wide gap between the apartment average and its median shows how much of that segment’s volume trades at the lower end.

The benchmark trend tells the same story in like-for-like terms. Two-storey homes are the only benchmark above a year ago — $758,100, up 0.6% — while one-storey homes slipped 3.1% year over year to $615,700. Measured over three years, single-family benchmarks are still up 3.0% and one-storey homes up 4.1%, while apartments sit 8.8% below where they stood three years ago. The split in this market is not one month’s news; it is a three-year divergence.

Selling Speed — Days on Market and Sale-to-List

The speed split mirrors the inventory split. Townhouses sold closest to asking in October, at 98.0% of list in a median 25 days. Single-family homes matched the citywide sale-to-list ratio of 97.5% and moved a touch quicker, at a median 24 days. Apartments took a median 34 days — up from 28.5 a year ago — and sold for 96.9% of list, the deepest average discount of the three types. Every type sold a little further below asking than last October, and every freehold type still moved in under a month at the median.

Regional Market Comparison — Where Ottawa Actually Moved

Ottawa’s suburban markets continued to drive activity in October, accounting for 753 sales, or 71.9% of citywide transactions. However, sales were below last October’s levels in all three suburban areas — and with most transactions concentrated in these markets, their slower year-over-year pace helps explain why Ottawa’s overall sales remained soft despite the modest improvement from September.

The suburbs generally maintained firmer conditions than the central and rural markets. Ottawa Suburb East had the lowest months of inventory among the seven submarkets at 3.5, with a sales-to-new-listings ratio of 51.0%; months of inventory stood at 3.7 in Ottawa Suburb West and 4.1 in Ottawa Suburb South. Conditions were weaker in Ottawa Centre and Ottawa Rural East, with 6.6 and 7.3 months of inventory respectively, and months of inventory in Rural East also increased from September — October’s citywide easing did not extend to every area. Ottawa Rural South and Ottawa Rural West recorded 4.1 and 4.4 months of inventory, but their smaller sales totals of 57 and 66 mean monthly changes there should be interpreted cautiously.

The Year So Far — 2026 Year to Date

Through the end of October, Ottawa has recorded 11,335 sales in 2026, down 7.3% from the same ten months of 2025, against 25,352 new listings, up 3.4%. The year-to-date average price is $695,598, down 0.8%, and the median is $635,000, down 1.6%. Homes have sold for 98.1% of list this year, at a median of 24 days on market — three days slower than last year’s pace. October fits its year: cooler, slower, and split by segment.

Looking Ahead — Jobs, the Budget and the Rate Hike

Employment remains a key concern for Ottawa heading into winter. An analysis by the Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives, reported by the Ottawa Citizen, showed roughly 15,000 fewer people employed locally since March 2025 — a larger loss than in any other large Ontario municipality over the same period — and pointed to federal public-service reductions as an important contributor. The employment picture varies depending on the comparison period: September’s seasonally adjusted employment estimate for the Ontario side of the Ottawa–Gatineau census metropolitan area remained above its year-earlier level, although employment declined from August. Earlier periods of federal workforce reductions were accompanied by weaker Ottawa housing sales and higher inventory; that history adds to concerns about further cuts, although it does not tell us how much of today’s housing market softness is directly related to employment.

The federal budget, scheduled for November 5, will therefore be particularly important for Ottawa. Further details on staffing may help households assess their employment outlook, while housing measures could affect their ability to buy. The Canadian Real Estate Association’s pre-budget submission called for changes to housing finance and mortgage insurance, including support for ownership of individual units in small multiplexes — whether the government adopts those proposals will be another area to watch.

Households are also weighing an uneven national economy and a recent increase in borrowing costs. Statistics Canada reported essentially unchanged real GDP in August and an advance estimate of a 0.1% decline in September, while the Bank of Canada raised its policy rate to 2.5% on October 28, citing persistent inflation pressures — the first policy rate increase in three years. Affordability measures may also be influencing where buyers look: GOHBA reported 521 new-home sales in September, up 81.5% year over year, with sales from January to September up 53.8%, and expanded, limited-time HST relief for qualifying new homes may be temporarily strengthening their appeal relative to resale properties. While these figures do not establish that buyers are shifting from resale to new construction, the contrast makes new-home activity important to understanding Ottawa’s broader housing demand.

Questions Buyers and Sellers Ask About the Ottawa Housing Market

How many homes sold in Ottawa in October 2026?
1,048 residential properties sold through the Ottawa Real Estate Board’s MLS® system in October 2026, down 10.7% from October 2025. For the year to date, sales stand at 11,335, down 7.3%.

What are Ottawa home prices doing?
The average sale price in October 2026 was $678,512, down 4.5% year over year, and the median price was $622,000, down 3.9%. The MLS® HPI composite benchmark — the cleaner like-for-like measure — was $616,900, down 1.3% year over year.

Is Ottawa a buyer’s market or a seller’s market right now?
At 4.4 months of inventory citywide, Ottawa sits in balanced-to-soft territory — but the honest answer depends on the segment. Suburban freeholds, at 3.5 to 4.1 months of inventory, remain comparatively firm, while apartments at 7.4 months of inventory are in clear buyer’s-market conditions.

How long does it take to sell a home in Ottawa?
The median home sold in 25.5 days in October 2026 — faster than September’s 27 days and close to last October’s 25 days. Single-family homes were a touch quicker, at a median of 24 days.

Why are Ottawa apartments under so much pressure?
Apartment sales fell 23.3% year over year to 115, while listings kept coming — pushing months of inventory to 7.4, up from 5.8 a year ago, and the sales-to-new-listings ratio down to 31.3%. The apartment benchmark price of $377,500 is down 5.3% year over year, and apartments have been the market’s pressure point throughout 2026.

How much do Ottawa homes sell for compared with their asking price?
In October 2026, homes sold for an average of 97.5% of their listing price — unchanged from September and slightly below the 97.9% recorded a year earlier. Pricing close to the market is what the current numbers reward.

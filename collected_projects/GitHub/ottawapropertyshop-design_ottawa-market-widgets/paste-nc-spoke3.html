What the Federal Rebate Actually Is

Ottawa is in Ontario, so a new home here carries 13% HST: a 5% federal part (the GST) and an 8% provincial part. The federal First-Time Home Buyers' GST rebate — legislated in Bill C-4 and in effect March 12, 2026 — returns 100% of that 5% federal part on a first new home: up to $50,000 on a home at $1,000,000, phasing out in a straight line to $1,500,000. It applies to purchase agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 — a five-year runway, not a one-year window.

Two boundaries define it. First, it is a new construction program: the home must be newly built (or substantially renovated) and bought from a builder, or built by you on your own land. A resale home never qualifies, at any price. Second, it is a first-time buyer program — and "first-time" has a precise legal meaning that is not "have you ever owned anything, anywhere, ever." The next section is that meaning, test by test.

The Four Tests: Who Counts as a First-Time Buyer

Every GST rebate new construction claim by a first-time buyer is tested the same four ways. Fail one and the federal rebate is gone — pass all four and the full 5% comes back, to $50,000.

• The four-year rule — in the current calendar year and the four calendar years before it, you cannot have lived in a home that you owned. The same test runs on your spouse or common-law partner: if either of you owned and lived in a home inside that window, neither of you is a first-time buyer.

• Age and status — you must be 18 or older and a Canadian citizen or permanent resident when you sign the purchase agreement.

• A new home, to live in — the home must be your primary place of residence, or the primary residence of a qualifying relation. A home bought to rent out or flip does not qualify for this rebate (rental purchases use a different rebate family entirely).

• The agreement dates — the purchase agreement must be signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, construction must begin before 2031, and the home must be substantially complete before 2036.

The Phase-Out: What You Get at Each Price

Below $1,000,000 the math is simple: the rebate is the whole 5%. A $500,000 first new home carries $25,000 of GST, and $25,000 comes back. An $800,000 home carries $40,000, and $40,000 comes back. At $1,000,000 the rebate reaches its $50,000 ceiling. Above $1,000,000 it phases out in a straight line — every dollar of price above $1,000,000 takes ten cents of rebate with it — until it reaches zero at $1,500,000. CRA's own example makes the midpoint easy to remember: a $1,250,000 home receives $25,000, exactly half.

How It Stacks With Ontario: The Other 8 Percent

The federal rebate is only half the first-time buyer story in Ontario. Ontario mirrors it with a first-time buyer rebate of the 8% provincial part — the full 8% on a first new home to $1,000,000, worth up to $80,000, phasing down on the same straight line to $1,500,000. Put the two together and a qualifying first-time buyer gets the entire 13% back on a home to $1,000,000 — up to $130,000 — exactly the figure the Ontario enhanced rebate advertises, reached by a different road.

How do the two roads interact for a first-time buyer signing inside the Ontario enhanced window (agreements April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027)? By the rule the mechanics guide in this series explains in full: the federal side is covered once, never twice. The Ontario top-up fills whatever the federal rebates leave behind; it does not duplicate them. And one warning carried over from that guide, because it is the most repeated error in this market: there is no federal 13% rebate. The 13% is always two parts — a federal 5% and a provincial 8% — and any pitch that adds a second federal cheque on top is double-counting the same money.

Where the first-time buyer stack really earns its keep is outside the Ontario window. Sign before April 1, 2026 or after March 31, 2027 and a repeat buyer falls back to the standard rebates — at Ottawa prices, the Ontario $24,000. How the Money Moves: Assign It at Closing, or Claim It Yourself

Route A — assign it at closing (what most buyers do). You sign the rebate application — CRA Form GST190 — and hand it to the builder. The builder credits the rebate amount against your closing balance and claims it from CRA after closing. On an $800,000 first new home, your price money is $800,000, not $904,000: the $40,000 federal rebate and the $64,000 Ontario rebate never leave your account. The risk to read before you sign: most builder agreements make you liable to repay the rebate if CRA later denies the claim, even where the price was advertised after-rebate. Your lawyer reads that clause — for a first-time buyer it can be the largest single paragraph in the agreement.

Route B — claim it yourself after closing. You pay the full price plus HST at closing, then file the same forms with CRA yourself and the rebate is paid to you. Same money, later, with a financing gap in between — most buyers only take this route when the builder will not accept an assignment. Owner-built homes always claim directly. Either way, the eligibility is tested the same way: the four tests above, applied to your agreement date, your history and your residence.

Who Gets What

• A first-time buyer couple — both partners pass the four-year rule together, and the stack applies in full: on a home to $1,000,000, the whole 13% comes back. One claim per home — the rebate follows the purchase, not the number of buyers.

• A buyer whose partner owned before — if your spouse or partner lived in a home they owned inside the four-year window, the first-time buyer rebates are closed to you both, even if you personally have never owned. Inside the Ontario window the enhanced rebate may still carry you — it has no first-time condition — which is why the agreement date matters so much for couples in this position.

• Buying for a relation — the home can be the primary residence of a qualifying relation (an adult child, for example) rather than your own; the residence test follows the person who will live there. The buyer claiming must still pass the first-time tests.

• Repeat buyers — no first-time rebate, in any year. Your rebate story is the Ontario enhanced stack inside its window, or the standard rebates outside it — both mapped in the mechanics guide in this series.

The Dates That Matter

Three dates run this program, and none of them is the March 31, 2027 cliff that dominates the Ontario headlines. March 20, 2025 is the floor: agreements signed before it are outside the program entirely. 2031 is the ceiling: agreements must be signed before 2031, with construction begun before 2031. 2036 is the completion line: the home must be substantially complete before 2036. Inside those rails, the first-time buyer stack simply runs — no annual window to catch, no deadline quarter where builders pressure you to sign before a rebate dies. That calm is worth money by itself: it means a first-time buyer can choose the right lot and the right builder instead of the fastest signature. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the federal first-time buyer GST rebate, in plain terms?

A federal rebate — legislated in Bill C-4 and in effect March 12, 2026 — that returns 100% of the 5% GST on a first new home: up to $50,000 on a home at $1,000,000, phasing out to $1,500,000. It covers purchase agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031.

Who counts as a first-time home buyer for this rebate?

Someone who, in the current calendar year and the four calendar years before it, has not lived in a home they owned — and whose spouse or common-law partner passes the same test. You must also be 18 or older, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and the home must be a new build that becomes a primary residence.

My spouse owned a home before we met. Do I still qualify?

If they lived in that home during the current year or any of the four years before it, no — the four-year test runs on both partners, and one owned home inside the window ends it for both of you. The Ontario enhanced rebate, inside its own window, has no first-time condition and may still carry the purchase.

How much do I get back on an $800,000 new home?

All of the tax. The GST on $800,000 is $40,000, returned in full by the federal rebate; Ontario's first-time buyer rebate returns the 8% provincial part, $64,000. Total back: $104,000.

What happens between $1,000,000 and $1,500,000?

Both first-time buyer rebates phase out in a straight line. At $1,250,000 you receive half of each — $25,000 federal and $40,000 Ontario. At $1,500,000 both have phased to zero.

Does the rebate apply to a resale home?

No. The federal first-time buyer GST rebate covers new construction only — a newly built home bought from a builder, an owner-built home, or a substantial renovation. A resale home is outside the program at every price.

Can I stack this rebate with the Ontario enhanced HST rebate?

They combine, but the federal 5% is covered once, never twice. Inside the Ontario window, the Ontario top-up fills whatever the federal rebates leave behind, so a first-time buyer reaches the full 13% on a home to $1,000,000 either way. Outside the window, the first-time buyer stack is the route that remains.

How do I claim it — do I pay the GST first?

Usually not. Most buyers assign the rebate to the builder at closing: you sign CRA Form GST190, the builder credits the amount on your closing statement, and the builder claims it from CRA. If you claim it yourself instead, you pay the full HST at closing and file the same form with CRA after closing.

Can I buy the home for my adult child and still qualify?

The home can be the primary place of residence of a qualifying relation — an adult child, for example — rather than your own. The buyer making the claim must still pass all four first-time buyer tests.

When does this program end?

Purchase agreements must be signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, construction must begin before 2031, and the home must be substantially complete before 2036. There is no 2027 cliff in this program — that deadline belongs to the Ontario enhanced rebate.

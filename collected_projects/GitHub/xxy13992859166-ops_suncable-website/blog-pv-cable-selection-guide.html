PV Cable Selection Guide 2025: How to Choose the Right Solar DC Cable

Updated October 2025 | By JunlinChicheng Technical Team

Choosing the right solar PV cable is one of the most critical decisions for any photovoltaic installation. Incorrect cable selection can lead to energy loss, safety hazards, and costly replacement projects. This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about PV cable types, specifications, and certification requirements.

Key Takeaway: For most utility-scale and commercial solar projects, H1ZZ2-K single-core cable is the preferred choice in North America, while PV1-F dual-core cable dominates European and Asian markets. Always verify local code requirements before ordering.

PV1-F vs H1ZZ2-K: Understanding the Difference

These are the two most common PV cable standards worldwide, each designed for different regional requirements:

SpecificationPV1-F (IEC 62930)H1ZZ2-K (VDE 0250)
Core ConfigurationDual-core (Red + Black)Single-core (Black)

Typical UseString wiring, module interconnectsArray wiring, combiner box connections

When to Use PV1-F Cable

PV1-F is the go-to choice for solar installations in Europe, the Middle East, Southeast Asia, and Australia. Its dual-core design (red positive, black negative) simplifies installation by reducing the number of cables needed. This cable type is particularly popular in residential and commercial rooftop systems where space is limited.

When to Use H1ZZ2-K Cable

H1ZZ2-K is the standard for North American markets and is increasingly popular in Latin America, Africa, and large-scale utility projects. The single-core black design is required by the NEC (National Electrical Code) in the United States. Many EPC contractors prefer H1ZZ2-K for its flexibility and ease of handling in long string runs.

Certification Standards Explained

Understanding certifications is essential when sourcing PV cables for international projects:

  • TÜV Rheinland: Required for EU market compliance. Tests include UV resistance (2,000+ hours), thermal aging, and mechanical stress. Most European projects require TÜV-certified cables.
  • UL Listed (UL 4703): Mandatory for US installations under NEC Article 690. UL certification ensures the cable meets rigorous safety standards for outdoor use.
  • CE Marking: Required for European market access. CE confirms compliance with EU directives for low voltage equipment.
  • CSA Certified: Required for Canadian market installations. Similar to UL but follows Canadian electrical codes.
  • SAA (Australia): Required for Australian solar projects. AS/NZS 5022 compliance is mandatory.
Pro Tip: Always request certification documents before placing orders. Many projects require proof of certification for customs clearance and electrical inspections.

How to Size Your PV Cable Correctly

Cable sizing directly impacts system efficiency and safety. Here are the key factors:

1. Current Rating

The cable must handle the maximum current of your PV string without overheating. For a typical 10A string, 4mm² cable is sufficient. For higher current strings (15A+), use 6mm² or larger.

2. Voltage Drop

Keep voltage drop below 1-2% for optimal performance. Longer cable runs require larger cross-sections. As a general rule: runs under 50m can use 4mm²; runs over 100m should use 6mm².

3. Ambient Temperature

In hot climates (above 40°C), derate your cable capacity by 10-15%. This is especially important for desert installations in the Middle East and North Africa.

4. Mechanical Protection

Underground installations require additional mechanical protection. Consider using conduit or armored cable for burial applications.

MC4 Connectors: What You Need to Know

MC4-style connectors are the industry standard for PV cable interconnections. Key considerations:

  • IP68 Rating: Essential for outdoor durability. Prevents water ingress and maintains connection integrity.
  • Compatibility: Not all MC4 connectors are compatible. Ensure your connectors match the cable diameter (typically 4mm or 6mm).
  • Quality Matters: Cheap connectors can fail after 3-5 years, leading to arc faults and fire risks. Always choose TÜV or UL certified connectors.
  • Pre-terminated Cables: Consider factory-terminated cables with MC4 connectors for faster, more reliable installation.

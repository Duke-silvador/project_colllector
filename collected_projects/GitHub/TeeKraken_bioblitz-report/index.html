Bioblitz Report

Turn your bioblitz's iNaturalist sightings into a tidy nature report to print and hand to the landowner. Your file stays on your computer; nothing is uploaded.

1. Get your sightings from iNaturalist
  1. Sign in to iNaturalist (an account is needed to export) and open the export page.
  2. Under Create a Query, pick out your event's sightings. If your bioblitz had an iNaturalist project, paste the project's observations page address into the box. Otherwise choose the place and the dates of the event.
  3. Under Choose columns, leave the ticks iNaturalist already sets. Then, under Taxon Extras, also tick these two, which start unticked:
    • taxon_family_name, so the list can be sorted by family
    • taxon_species_name, so subspecies count once, under their species
    • optional: taxon_hybrid_name, so hybrids named to species level (like a hybrid oak) count as species
  4. Press Create Export. When it's ready, download the file. You can add the .zip here as it is, or unzip it and add the .csv inside.

The columns this app reads: scientific_name, common_name, iconic_taxon_name (the group, like birds or plants), quality_grade, observed_on, the privacy columns geoprivacy, taxon_geoprivacy and coordinates_obscured, and the extras above. It ignores everything else, including names, usernames and map points.

Already exported without the extras? Go back to the export page, set up the same query, tick the missing columns and create a new export. Your earlier export stays in iNaturalist's list too.

3. Fill in the details

The date is filled in from your sightings. Change any of these as you like.

Which sightings to count

"Research Grade only" needs the quality_grade column, which isn't in this file. To use it, export again from iNaturalist with quality_grade ticked (under Basic).

Sensitive species

A report about one named property can give away where a rare plant or animal lives, even without a map. So sightings iNaturalist keeps hidden (marked obscured or private) are left out, along with every sighting of a species iNaturalist treats as sensitive.

Only do this if you're sure the landowner should know, and the report won't be shared further.

4. Pick notable finds

The app can't know what's special on this land, so you choose. Tick the species to feature at the top of the report and add a short note to each, like why it matters or where it was seen.

Areas

Areas is a generative art project exploring color theory and grid composition systems. The artwork celebrates the interplay between digital exactness and an homage to the human gesture.

The algorithm generates both dark and light palettes using color theory and HSL color space.

Shapes are laid out within a grid structure and then filled with randomized variation, creating overlapping shapes and unexpected gaps. The image is texturized with pixel feedback to create a paint like quality.

Color Theory

Hue Saturation Lightness (HSL) Color System:

H: a color, in degrees between 0-360
S: mixing between middle gray and full intensity of the hue
L: Creates shades and tints of the Hue between full black and full white.

Complimentary colors are separated by integer divisions of 360 - e.g. 180, 120, 90

Analogous colors are separated by smaller intervals - e.g. 10-30

Delicate Tension. No 85
Wassily Kandinsky

Kandinsky often cited the importance and influence of music on his artwork.

This type of chord cannot be played on a piano with heavily modifying the tuning of the instrument. This tuning system is often called "Just Intonation" and was used by minimalist composers like La Monte Young and Terry Riley (pictured).

Each time a new block is drawn, a new note is created.

The pitch of the note is based on the vertical position of the block in the image.

The volume of the note is modulated by a square wave, which turns the note on and off. The period (duration of the on + off) is determined by the blocks width.

The horizontal position of the block determines the stereo placement of the new note.  

As the notes continue to combine over time, the piece moves from a discrete system to a smeared wall of sound, subtly pulsing and changing.

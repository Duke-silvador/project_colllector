Research

Where physics meets
efficient computation.

01

Tensor networks & reduced-order modeling

Studying low-rank representations of turbulent fields using tensor-network methods, including matrix product states, to understand how physical information is retained under compression.

02

Computational turbulence

Working with direct numerical simulation data and flow statistics, with research spanning isotropic turbulence and turbulent reacting flows.

03

Quantum scientific computing

Exploring quantum algorithms for partial differential equations and fluid dynamics, alongside MPS-based state preparation and variational quantum methods.

Explain my research to…

Turbulence is messy — like swirls in a river or flames in a fire. Computers need tons of memory to describe all that mess. I find clever shortcuts that describe the same mess using way less memory, kind of like folding a giant map so it fits in your pocket.

I develop compression methods that shrink massive fluid simulation datasets by orders of magnitude while keeping the physics accurate. My work combines high-performance computing (Python, Julia, SLURM clusters), advanced linear algebra, and quantum-inspired algorithms, with collaborators at Los Alamos National Laboratory and international quantum physics groups.

I study tensor-network (MPS, MERA, PEPS) encodings of 1024³ DNS fields of forced isotropic turbulence and turbulent non-premixed jet flames, assessing how bond-dimension truncation affects low- and high-order statistics (gradient PDFs, strain-rate invariants, Q–R joint statistics), and explore near-term quantum algorithms for fluid-dynamics PDEs.

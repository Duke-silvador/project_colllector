Understand the numbers

Gross Yield vs. Cap Rate

A property's gross rental yield can look attractive before expenses. Its capitalization rate reflects operating costs such as taxes, insurance, HOA/CDD, maintenance and management. These estimates update when you edit the calculator below.

GROSS RENTAL YIELD · BEFORE EXPENSES—

Scheduled annual rent divided by purchase price, before vacancy or expenses.

CAP RATE · AFTER OPERATING COSTS—

Net operating income divided by purchase price; excludes mortgage payments.

Important: A cap rate is not your cash-on-cash return. Financing costs and the cash you invest affect your actual cash flow. All figures are MODEL estimates based on your entries, not live MLS or rental market statistics.

Florida Gulf Coast considerations

Four Checks Before You Invest

Coastal property ownership requires more than a rental-income estimate. Verify these items for the exact address before relying on a projection.

Wind & Flood Insurance

Request property-specific quotes and review flood zones, deductibles, roof age and coverage exclusions. An average insurance rate is not a quote.

HOA, Condo & CDD Costs

Check assessments, association budgets, reserves, special assessments and any CDD charges. Costs may change after purchase.

Rental Restrictions

Confirm city/county rules, condo documents, HOA lease minimums, registration requirements and any restrictions on short-term rentals.

Taxes & Maintenance

Estimate taxes after the purchase rather than relying only on the seller's tax bill. Budget for repairs, vacancy and major replacements.

For 1031 exchanges, legal structure, timing and eligibility should be reviewed with a qualified intermediary and tax adviser.

Address-specific due diligence

Know Your Investment Area

Different Gulf Coast locations and even neighboring communities can have very different carrying costs and rental rules. Before making an offer, ask for the following information about the actual property:

Rental feasibility

Verify minimum lease terms, registration rules, occupancy limits, association approvals, and local restrictions. Never assume a home can operate as a short-term rental.

Insurance and resilience

Obtain current wind, homeowners and flood insurance quotes for the address; review building age, roofs, flood exposure, deductibles and past claims when available.

Taxes, HOA and assessments

Estimate post-purchase property taxes; review association budgets, reserves, CDD charges, pending special assessments and any transfer or application fees.

Market evidence

Check dated comparable sales, realistic rent estimates, current listings and independent property records. Treat sample numbers as illustrations unless a date and source are identified.

1031 exchange reminder: Federal exchange rules can impose identification and completion deadlines, including commonly applicable 45-day and 180-day periods. Eligibility and timing depend on the transaction. Consult a qualified intermediary and tax adviser before selling or acquiring replacement property.

Costs a Simple Yield Can Miss

Verify local rental restrictions, minimum stays, vacancy, management costs, maintenance, taxes after ownership changes, wind and flood insurance, special assessments and major-replacement reserves.

Waterfront property may also require seawall and dock maintenance. Gross yield is not cash flow, and cap rate excludes financing and income taxes.

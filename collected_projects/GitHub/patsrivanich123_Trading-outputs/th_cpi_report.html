Thailand CPI Nowcast

Predicting headline & core CPI before the official TPSO print — full project report. Data anchor: September 2026 · built October 2026.

What this is. A model that nowcasts Thailand's monthly headline and core CPI before the official release, using only data that is published before the print. It is built on the real TPSO methodology, feeds the volatile components from live sources (fuel, electricity, fresh food), and leaves the sticky "core" on a seasonal rule because it has no live feed. Every predictor is pre-print; every weight is either the official basket weight or calibrated backward from the data.

3 · How Thailand actually builds its CPI

Compiler: Trade Policy & Strategy Office (TPSO), Ministry of Commerce (unusually, not the central bank). Confirmed from the ILO methodology descriptor (read directly), the TPSO 2566-base note, and the live data API.

FeatureDetail
Index formulaDutot (ratio of arithmetic-mean prices) at the elementary level; Modified Laspeyres aggregation above. (Our first sketch wrongly assumed Jevons — corrected.)
Base year2023 = 100 (B.E. 2566), live since Jan 2025; rebased ~every 4–5 years off the NSO Household Socio-Economic Survey.
Basket464 items · 7 groups · 52 provinces (Bangkok + 4 regions) · COICOP-based.
Collection frequencyFuel: daily · fresh food: weekly · everything else: monthly · rent: 6-monthly. This non-uniform frequency is the key to a nowcast.
Core definitionHeadline excluding fresh/raw food and energy (~69.7% of the basket).
AdjustmentsNo seasonal adjustment, never revised (first print is final). ~26% of the basket is administered/price-controlled (IMF).
ReleaseMonthly, first week of the following month (~5th–7th).

Why a pre-print nowcast is even possible: a quarter of the basket is administered (fuel, electricity, fares, tuition), so it's observable or scheduled in advance; and fuel/fresh-food are collected daily/weekly, so by month-end they're effectively known.

4 · Model architecture

A weight-exact decomposition on the official TPSO blocks. Each block's month-over-month move is now-cast, blended by weight, then chained to a level and converted to the year-over-year figure the market trades.

5 · The four blocks in detail

5.1 · Fuel (8.9%) — the gold standard

The CPI motor-fuel index is the monthly arithmetic mean (Dutot) of EPPO daily pump prices. We pulled the full daily series and regressed the published CPI on it to calibrate the weight backward:

Target (published CPI)Recovered slopeOfficialR²
CPI motor-fuel (52200)0.891.00 (pass-through)1.00
CPI energy (92000)0.560.680.98
CPI headline (00000)0.0810.0890.93

Regressing headline on the pump price alone recovers a fuel weight of 0.081 vs the official 0.089 — the model rediscovers the basket weight from prices. Fuel alone explains 93% of monthly headline variance.

5.2 · Electricity (3.7%) — a scheduled step, not a regression

Electricity is flat within each 4-month ERC Ft round and steps only on pre-announced tariff/subsidy changes. A naïve regression on the headline tariff flops (R²=0.05) because the government subsidy overlay — also announced — drives the effective household price (e.g. the −7.8% Sep-2026 drop was the deepened 200-unit ≤3 THB cap). 78% of its MoM variance sits at the scheduled round boundaries (Jan/May/Sep). So it's modelled by a calendar lookup — the most pre-print-certain block of all.

5.3 · Raw food (17.2%) — the hard one, cracked by backward-calibration

Raw food is a ~100-item aggregate where each fresh item is idiosyncratically noisy. A naïve equal-weight basket of DIT prices gave R²=0.00. The fix — calibrate the item weights backward (ridge regression of CPI raw-food on the DIT item prices):

OUT-OF-SAMPLE R²0.46vs 0.00 naïve
OOS correlation+0.69
Top fitted weightseggs +0.42, pork +0.32not the flashy veg

Why it's not as clean as fuel: fuel is one administered price (mechanical); food is a noisy crowd. An 11-item proxy recovers ~half the signal; the rest needs the full CPI survey. Eggs and pork dominate because they're high-weight and move coherently, while vegetables are idiosyncratic noise that washes out.

5.4 · Core (69.7%) — seasonal, and we proved it

Core's biggest moving piece is prepared food (17%) — but DIT does not survey cooked/restaurant food, so it has no live feed. The model is a pure seasonal rule:

πcoret = β₀ + β₁·Sm(t) + β₂·Xt−1 + ε  → fitted: 0.038 + 0.783·S + 0.006·Xt−1  (β₂≈0) → collapses to seasonal π = μm(t) + ε

Backtests: core seasonal out-of-sample y/y MAE ≈ 0.10pp (seasonal-only actually beats the regression with the pass-through term). Window test — predict 2024 from 2022+2023, 2025 from 2022+2023+2024 — gives y/y MAE 0.12pp (more training years → smoother).

We tried to beat seasonal with real scraped observables — lagged DIT food (cost-push to prepared food), USD/THB (imported goods), and gold (personal effects). Result: every model had negative out-of-sample R² — none beat the seasonal mean. The scrapeable bits are too small-weight and core MoM is essentially white noise (std 0.14pp, ~zero autocorrelation; micro prices reset only every 4–7 months). Core staying seasonal is the ceiling, not laziness.

7 · Key findings

  • ~30% of the basket (fuel + electricity + raw food) carries nearly all the headline volatility and has genuine pre-print feeds. The other ~70% (core) drifts and has none.
  • Fuel is the edge. It's one administered price, pass-through ≈ 1, observable daily — alone it explains 93% of headline MoM and catches the big regime shifts.
  • Raw food needs weight calibration to be usable (R² 0.00 → 0.46); eggs & pork, not vegetables, carry the signal.
  • Electricity is deterministic — a calendar of announced ERC Ft rounds + subsidies, no regression needed.
  • Core is irreducibly seasonal — its movers are unobservable before the print; real scraped inputs (FX, gold, food) don't beat the seasonal mean.
  • Methodology correction: elementary index is Dutot (arithmetic), not Jevons (geometric).

10 · Caveats

  • October 2026 is only observed through the first days of the month for fuel — the nowcast assumes the latest pump level holds (the diesel refinery relief & LPG freeze expire Oct 31, a November risk).
  • Raw-food calibration leans on a 2025–2026 window dominated by the March–April-2026 fuel cap break; the recovered weights are indicative.
  • Electricity's effective household price depends on the representative consumption block, which TPSO does not publish — the subsidy overlay is modelled from announcements.
  • The current-model headline backtest is 10 OOS months (bounded by DIT food history starting Jan 2025); the energy-observed backtest covers 33 months.

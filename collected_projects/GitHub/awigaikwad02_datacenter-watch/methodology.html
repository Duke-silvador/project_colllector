Methodology and sources

How The Rack Report is built.

Everything on this site comes from public news feeds and a small hand-checked project list, processed by an open script you can read. Here is exactly what happens, and where it can go wrong.

1Collect

Google News RSS searches (U.S. edition) and optional Google Alerts feeds.

2Tag

Written rules (no AI) assign a topic, places, states and organisations to each headline.

3Detect

Headlines naming a site, a stage and a U.S. place are attached to projects in a permanent registry.

4Publish

JSON files are committed to GitHub and served by GitHub Pages.

How to read the data

  • Everything is rule-based. No AI is used anywhere. Every topic, place, company and project status comes from written rules in an open script: keyword lists, a place gazetteer and a few matching rules, each tested against real headlines. No AI model or language-model service reads, summarises or classifies anything, and none is called by the site or its updates. The same headline always gets the same tags, and every tag can be traced to the rule that set it.
  • A headline is a lead, not a fact. Counts, maps and charts describe what the news reported. A detected project means "headlines describe a project here", not a confirmed facility. Curated projects (rack-shaped pins) are entered and checked by hand.
  • Topics say what a story is about; each headline gets one. General means no rule matched.
  • Places are as precise as the headline: a town, a county, a state, or only the outlet's home state ("source"). Projects known only to the state are shown as Location unavailable, never as a pin.
  • Operators (the companies tab) own, develop or run data centers. Investors, builders (contractors, engineers, architects), suppliers (power, cooling, chips) and utilities are tracked separately and can be filtered in the Newsroom; they never create a project on their own.
  • Status follows what the headlines report as happening now: announced, under construction, operational, or cancelled / blocked. Plans in the future tense ("could break ground", "opens in 2026") do not move a status, and a status never moves backward except to Cancelled / Blocked.
  • Confidence is high when a project has a named operator, a town or county and two or more outlets; low projects are hidden on Rack Radar by default.
  • Freshness: the "Updated" badge shows when the data last changed; it turns amber after 3 hours.

Sources and refresh schedule

A GitHub Actions workflow refreshes the data once an hour. Because GitHub's scheduler can skip or delay scheduled runs by hours, the workflow keeps itself running: each run refreshes hourly for almost six hours and then starts the next one, with an hourly backup schedule and a watchdog that restarts it if the data is ever more than 3 hours old. If Google rate-limits most of the searches, that hour is skipped and retried an hour later. A feed that answers with a rate limit or server error is retried up to three times with increasing waits, and the run fails loudly if every feed comes back empty. It runs scripts/fetch_news.py, which reads the searches listed in data/feeds.json: Google News RSS queries for data center announcements, construction, power, policy and regional news, all using the U.S. edition of Google News. Any Google Alerts RSS feeds added to that file are read too.

An hour with no new or changed headlines makes no commit, so the "Updated" time in the header is the last time the data actually changed; it turns amber when that is more than 3 hours ago. Open pages check for new data every 10 minutes and update in place.

Loading the latest run…

We store only the headline, the publisher name, the publication time and the link. Article text is never copied; every headline links to the original publisher through Google News. The recent feed keeps the last 90 days of headlines when that fits in 2.5 MB, otherwise the last 30 days; the Newsroom loads older months from the archive when you pick a longer range. Projects are kept for good in a registry, each with every headline about it. Every headline is also written to a permanent monthly archive in data/archive/ that is never trimmed; the Newsroom's Include archive switch searches it.

On topic only

A headline is kept only if its title or description says data center (or data centre, datacenter), hyperscale or colocation, or names a tracked company together with a campus, facility, site, megawatt or gigawatt. Stock-market outlets that write about data center companies without reporting on projects (for example Simply Wall St, Yahoo Finance, The Motley Fool, Seeking Alpha, MarketBeat, Benzinga and Zacks) are on a blocklist in data/feeds.json.

U.S. only

Every gazetteer place carries a country code. A story is dropped when every place it names is outside the United States. Stories from a short list of non-U.S. outlets (BBC, The Guardian, The Telegraph, The Irish Times, ABC Australia, CBC, The Globe and Mail, The Times of India, The Economic Times, The Straits Times, South China Morning Post and others) are dropped unless the title itself names a U.S. place or state. Because many foreign stories name no place we recognize, the script also drops stories with clear foreign markers (for example "data centre" spelling, foreign currencies or country names) and stories from a short list of non-U.S. outlets. Stories that name no place at all are kept. This means a few foreign stories can slip through and a few U.S. ones can be dropped.

Topics, places and companies

  • Topics come from keyword rules in the script, checked in this order: Cancelled / Blocked, a piece the outlet labels as opinion, New Announcement, Construction, Expansion, Land Acquisition, Operations, Markets / Earnings, Deals / Investment, Economy / Jobs, Environment (water, noise, air), Power / Utility, Community (residents, protests, backlash), AI Infrastructure, Politics (elections, parties), Policy / Permits (zoning, permits, moratoriums, rules, lawsuits, approvals), and Analysis / Opinion for explainers and studies. The first match becomes the headline's topic; anything else is General. The rules are tuned so General stays under 10% and Policy / Permits under 20% of headlines. Cancelled / Blocked covers projects that are withdrawn, scrapped, denied, voted down, halted or paused, and moratoriums that pass; headlines that only call for or debate a pause are not counted. Cancelled or blocked sites are grey on the map, never count as announced projects and are never picked as the Overview's major story.
  • Places come from a gazetteer (scripts/gazetteer.json) of U.S. states, counties and cities plus foreign places used only for filtering. Each U.S. place carries coordinates and a state, which becomes the headline's two-letter state code. The title and the feed's description are both scanned. Every U.S. county and parish is in the gazetteer at its Census centroid, plus the townships that appear in the news and every U.S. town that the news names at least three times (from GeoNames, CC BY 4.0). A county or town name shared by several states (Lansing, Jackson) is used only when the story or the outlet names the state. When a story names no place, a local outlet's home state (listed in data/feeds.json) is used. Each headline records its location_precision: city, county, state or source (the outlet's home state).
  • Organisations are matched by name and common aliases (for example AWS for Amazon), case sensitive and as whole words. Names that are also ordinary words (Stream, Prime, Page, Element, Edged, Ascent, Galaxy) only count next to a word that makes the company clear ("Stream Data Centers", "Galaxy Digital"). Operators are the companies that own, develop or run data centers; "Stargate" counts toward OpenAI and Oracle, with SoftBank as investor, and named campuses such as Hyperion, Fairwater, Colossus and Project Rainier count toward their owners. Investors (Blackstone, Brookfield, KKR...), builders (Turner, Mortenson, DPR...), suppliers (Vertiv, Schneider Electric, Nvidia...) and utilities and grid operators (Entergy, Dominion, ERCOT...) are tagged separately and can be filtered in the Newsroom.
  • Stories group headlines about the same event. Two headlines published within 5 days of each other join when their titles share at least half of their words (Jaccard similarity of the title words, ignoring common words and the outlet's name), or at least 30% when they also name the same city or county, or the same company in the same state. Groups chain through shared headlines, but two groups that name different states or places never merge. Each story is shown with its headline that gives the most precise place, else the earliest. The Newsroom shows one card per story with its other sources behind an "N sources" button, Rack Radar shows one site per story at the most precise place any of its headlines gave, and company counts are stories, not headlines.
  • Figures such as "1.2 GW" or "$10 billion" are read from the headline. Capacity figures above 20 GW are ignored because they describe grid-wide totals, not single projects.

Curated and detected projects

Curated projects (rack-shaped pins on Rack Radar) are entered by hand in data/projects.json from public reporting, with exact coordinates where known. Each one has a verified flag that stays false until it has been checked against current sources.

Detected projects (ring pins) live in a permanent registry. A headline that mentions a data center or campus, a stage (announced, under construction, operational, expansion or cancelled) and a U.S. place is attached to an existing project when it belongs to the same story, names the project (Stargate, Hyperion and other code names), or names the same company in the same county or city, or names the same project ("Project Sail") in the same state. A headline that names only a builder or supplier can join the one project already tracked at its place but never starts one. Otherwise it starts a new project, but only when it is placed at a city or county and names a tracked company, a code name or a MW or dollar figure. A project's status only moves forward (announced, under construction, operational), and only on a headline that reports the stage as happening now: "could break ground", "plans to open" or "opens in 2026" do not count, and neither does a solar farm or factory that opens. The exception is that a cancellation marks it Cancelled / Blocked and a later construction or opening headline revives it; every change is kept with the headline that caused it. Each project has a confidence level: high needs a tracked company, a city or county and two or more outlets. Rack Radar hides low-confidence and cancelled projects by default. Each project also has its own page under /projects/. The pin is placed at the city or county named. Projects known only to the state are marked Location unavailable and are not pinned. They are hidden by default; with the "Show projects with location unavailable" filter on, each state gets one dashed bubble showing how many such projects it has (folded into the nearest cluster circle as a small "~N" figure when zoomed out to the whole country), and selecting a bubble lists its projects so each can be opened. Headlines that name a company and only a state are grouped into one "site not yet named" project per company and state. Detected projects are leads to check, not confirmed facts; corrections go in data/project_overrides.json.

Known limitations

  • Keyword tagging misses context. A headline about a vote against a project can still be tagged as an announcement. Mistakes are fixed by improving the rules and adding the headline to the test suite, never by an AI model.
  • Places can be ambiguous (Georgia, Washington, Aurora). The gazetteer favors the U.S. meaning.
  • Story grouping compares title words, so coverage of one event written in very different words can still appear as separate stories.
  • The archive is append-only. It was re-tagged once with the current rules; headlines those rules reject stay in the archive files marked filtered and are hidden on the site.
  • Google News results vary, and paywalled or regional outlets may be missing.
  • Counts and charts describe headlines, not the industry as a whole.

Report a wrong pin or headline

Open an issue on GitHub with the project name or headline, what is wrong and a source link. Corrections to curated projects go straight into data/projects.json; recurring tagging mistakes are fixed in the rules so they stay fixed on the next refresh.

Why a platform, not an adapter

A shielded A→B swap needs a venue that prices, matches, and settles privately and non-custodially. Moving idle balances into yield needs the same primitives plus metering and accounting. Those are not two separate adapters — they are two applications of one execution/settlement substrate. ex_net generalizes exactly what adapters need: move assets, price/swap, provide liquidity/yield, settle privately. So it sits beneath Armada's Adapters tier, and Swaps and yield become thin integrations on top — not peers.

Trade-off, stated up front. A shared substrate under multiple adapters is a bigger surface than one independently-deployed adapter, and it needs its own audit — a deliberate deviation from Armada's per-adapter audit discipline. The pool stays immutable and untouched; ex_net operates at the adapter/execution tier only.

Lineage — ex_net (2017) → matcher (2019–20)

Vulcanize's 2017 ex_net whitepaper[1] specified a bonded, federated venue for the privacy-preserving, cross-chain exchange of crypto-assets where assets never leave the trader's custody (they sit in parent-chain payment channels) and the federation only matches, prices, and authorizes settlement. It gave the vocabulary still in use — traders, liquidity providers, validators; bonding; per-block auditable snapshots; liquidity proofs; identity proofs; and the observation that a "decentralized exchange" as a noun cannot prevent operator front-running.

The matcher[24] is the concrete engine: a private Vulcanize repo (commits 2019–2020) with three branches — master (spec + skeletons), ian_branch (a fuller matcher.py), and the most-developed xsleonard_branch (Steve Leonard: algorithm.md, matcher2.py, a runnable matcher-sim.py). Together they are a working proof-of-concept of the ex_net matching layer.

The matcher — what it actually is

A per-block batch-auction matcher over order curves, across multiple trading pairs, on a payment-channel hub, with per-order counterparty filtering. From the design docstring: "reducing free options and cryptographically binding both parties based on their stated intentions." Concretely:

  • Order = a curve, not a point. An order specifies price-varying liquidity over a range (Curve / cRange), flattened into fillable Chunk units — a depth view over one order.
  • Filler-filtered. Each order carries an allowlist (Order_Curve.fillers): empty ⇒ open to anyone; non-empty ⇒ only listed pubkeys may fill. This is the RFQ/private-swap boundary inside one order type.
  • Multi-pair rings. A DFS path/ring clearing (find_path / execute_path) fills an order across multiple pairs (LOL-ABC → LOL-XYZ → XYZ-WTF), giving price improvement transparently.
  • Receipts / vouchers / plasma. Validators issue three receipt types (asset-for-voucher, order-curve-accepted, voucher-transfer) — the ex_net settlement lineage.

source: vulcanize/matcher (private) — xsleonard_branch: algorithm.md, matcher2.py, matcher-sim.py[24]

Frequent batch auction (fair ordering)

The engine clears as a frequent batch auction: discrete per-block clearing at a uniform price per pair, allocation by pro-rata + a deterministic lottery (_distribute_chunks → shuffle) — explicitly not first-in-queue price-time priority. There is no priority / cancel / market speed queue in the engine. This is the Budish–Cramton–Shim design[23] for defeating latency/HFT front-running, and combined with commit-reveal (T3) it also blocks operator front-running. Fills feel like a CLOB but settle like a batch auction.

CLOB + RFQ in one order type

The atomic order is already a limit order (the Curve is the aggregated book; OrderSet builds a bid/ask depth view), so a familiar limit-order + depth-book UX drops out directly. The one thing that differs (and must be disclosed) is that matching is a frequent batch auction — uniform price, pro-rata/lottery — not continuous price-time priority.

  • Open orders (empty fillers) are the lit book — a public CLOB view.
  • Filler-filtered / blinded orders stay off-book — the RFQ / private-swap side.

So one construction spans a public CLOB and bilateral RFQ, with the privacy caveat that a lit book only ever shows opt-in open orders. Because privacy forces request → quote → fill bilaterally over hidden participants and hidden flow, private note swaps are an RFQ system by nature — a public CLOB over hidden participants is a contradiction. The lit book is the public branch; private note swaps are the P2P branch taken to its conclusion.

Capital efficiency vs. AMMs

The venue is quote-based market-making (maker/keeper prices off-chain, any strategy), settled in channels — structurally more capital-efficient than an AMM, for two reasons:

  • No dead curve. An AMM spreads LP capital across the whole price range (0→∞); the vast majority is never near the market. A quoting maker deploys capital at the touch, sized to actual demand.
  • No forced LVR / impermanent loss. An AMM is a mechanical stale quote arbitrageurs pick off every block; a quote-based maker reprices to live (bounded by the reveal/quote epoch), a direct capital-return advantage. Balance-sheet reuse across pairs from one hub channel raises utilization per dollar locked.

This is the empirically-known result: 0x RFQ, Hashflow, and professional FX/OTC exist precisely because quote-based market-making delivers better prices with less locked capital than AMMs. Passive LPs are still first-class — they can fund hub capacity, post resting liquidity, or deposit into a pooled-maker vault where a keeper runs the market-making; the real difference is who runs the pricing (an active maker off-chain) not whether passive capital can participate.

Settlement into Nitro

Fills settle non-custodially into Nitro state channels: go-nitro's multi-asset swap protocol[6] and SwapChannel[7] over virtual channels[9], with the ForceMove dispute game[5] as the escape hatch. Every fill terminates in a co-signed settlement receipt or a slashable proof of its absence; no receipt ⇒ force-close to the pre-fill state. Net-new wiring: the LP/market-maker vault, receipt-or-slash, and multi-asset outcomes (see Build plan · T4).

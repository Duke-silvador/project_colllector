01 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Racial poverty differences

The reported poverty rates differ across racial and ethnic groups. The paper’s 20.5% White estimate corresponds to White alone, not Hispanic or Latino; the complete label matters for a fair comparison. [2]

These are population estimates, not descriptions of every person in a group. Race and Hispanic origin are separate Census concepts, and some categories overlap. The figures can document unequal outcomes, but explaining those outcomes requires evidence about institutions, history, household circumstances, and access to resources.

02 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Racial income differences

The paper reports per-capita income ranging from $17,892 for Hispanic or Latino residents to $36,504 for the group labeled White in CNYVitals. [12]

Per-capita income divides aggregate income by population, including people who do not work. It is not a typical salary or a median. The paper also records 2014 values, but changes in nominal dollars are not used here to claim increases in purchasing power without an inflation adjustment.

03 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Employment and labor-force participation

The paper’s 2024 group comparisons distinguish unemployment from labor-force participation. For example, it records an 11.7% unemployment rate and 59.8% participation rate for Black residents. [13]

The denominators differ: unemployment is measured within the civilian labor force, while participation concerns whether people are in that labor force at all. Neither statistic alone identifies the barriers people face or the quality and stability of available work.

04 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Changes in the number of residents below poverty

The paper includes 2014 and 2024 counts by group. Use the year selector to inspect those endpoints. A change in the number of people below poverty can reflect both a changing poverty rate and a changing population. [14]

These groups are not mutually exclusive, so the rows must not be added. The paper’s separate demographic table also uses overlapping categories and is excluded from a population-share chart until the precise Census categories are verified. [19]

Education across demographic groups

School outcomes provide another view of unequal conditions, using the school district rather than the city population. The Education page includes verified subject-level comparisons and explains why test proficiency should not be treated as a measure of a group’s potential. [10]

06 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Business ownership: a question for further verification

The paper’s business-ownership notes contain percentages and counts that do not reconcile, and the geographic universe is not established. The draft also speculates about the identities included in an “Other” category. These claims are not used as verified findings. [6]

A sound comparison needs the source’s geography, ownership definition, denominator, and treatment of suppressed values. It is not appropriate to infer the race of owners whose data were withheld for confidentiality. The topic remains part of the research agenda.

Read the evidence in context

ACS figures are estimates. The manuscript did not retain margins of error, so small differences should not be treated as statistically significant. “Source checked” means the displayed values were matched to a source during this review. “Paper transcription” identifies values retained from the manuscript that were not fully reproduced independently.

01 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Education and poverty

The paper’s education finding is one of its clearest patterns: the share of adults below poverty declines across the reported attainment categories. The estimates range from 47.2% among adults without a high school diploma to 10.8% among adults with a bachelor’s degree or higher. [2]

This comparison describes different groups of adults at one point in the survey period. It cannot isolate the effect of schooling from age, disability, work history, family resources, discrimination, or other circumstances. Education may expand options, but the chart does not predict an individual’s future.

02 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Economic disadvantage and third-grade testing

Third-grade English language arts and mathematics results show substantial differences between the district’s economic groups. In the downloaded 2025 data, ELA proficiency is 22.6% among economically disadvantaged students and 65.1% among students who are not economically disadvantaged. Mathematics shows a similar gap. [10]

These results can help identify where educational support deserves closer attention. They do not establish that family income alone caused a student’s score, and they should not be used to judge a child’s ability or a school’s quality without further evidence.

03 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Educational inequality and graduation

CNYVitals’ 2025 export records 901 graduates out of 1,356 enrolled in the reported Syracuse district cohort, producing a graduation rate of 66.45%. [11]

The paper compares this with an Onondaga County figure of 83.65%. Because the county cohort and duration have not been reproduced, that comparison is not presented as a verified gap. Similarly, the paper’s 11.5% dropout figure lacks a recorded year. Graduation, dropout, and continued enrollment are distinct outcomes.

04 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

School outcomes by race and ethnicity

The same source allows readers to compare reported proficiency across student groups. Use the subject selector to switch between ELA and mathematics. The verified export corrects the paper’s ELA figure for American Indian or Alaska Native students to 41.7%. [10]

Group differences call for attention to learning conditions and access to support. Small group sizes can make percentages sensitive to a few students. The source’s combined Asian and Pacific Islander category is retained so the site does not invent a more detailed breakdown.

05 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Internet access and the learning environment

The paper reports that 24.6% of households with income below $20,000 lack an internet subscription, compared with 10.2% of households earning $20,000–$74,999. [9]

A subscription can matter for school communication, homework, job searches, and access to services. This household measure does not tell us whether a student has a reliable device, sufficient connection speed, or a quiet place to study. Those questions require additional evidence.

Read the evidence in context

ACS figures are estimates. The manuscript did not retain margins of error, so small differences should not be treated as statistically significant. “Source checked” means the displayed values were matched to a source during this review. “Paper transcription” identifies values retained from the manuscript that were not fully reproduced independently.

03 / Employment

Work, income & wages

Having a job, earning a wage, and achieving household financial security are related—but different—measures.

01 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Median household income

Syracuse’s median household income is $47,819 in 2024 dollars for the 2020–2024 survey period. The paper reports a mean household income of $67,230. [1] [3]

The median is the middle value, not the arithmetic average. A household can include multiple earners and income other than wages, so household income should not be treated as the salary of a typical worker. Neither measure captures accumulated wealth, debt, or differences in household needs.

02 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Income distribution

The income brackets show more detail than a single median. The paper reports 11.4% of households below $10,000, 16.0% at $50,000–$74,999, and 4.0% at $200,000 or more. [3]

These are shares of households in income brackets, not shares of the city’s total income. They describe a broad spread of resources but cannot, by themselves, establish how much wealth is concentrated at the top.

03 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Unemployment and the working population

The paper’s 7.7% city unemployment estimate comes from the ACS multiyear data, while the 3.9% figure refers to the Syracuse metropolitan area in June 2026. Both are meaningful, but they describe different populations, periods, and statistical programs. [8] [5]

The city draft lists 119,578 people age 16 and older and 62,634 civilian employed residents. The larger figure must not be labeled employed. The metro count includes communities beyond Syracuse and is shown separately below. [8]

04 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Employment does not guarantee freedom from poverty

Among residents age 16 and older in the paper’s poverty table, 3.7% of people who worked full time, year-round were below poverty. The corresponding rates were 29.5% for people who worked part time or part year and 43.6% for people who did not work. [2]

This pattern supports examining hours, continuity of work, and household needs alongside unemployment. It does not show why a person worked fewer hours or did not work; caregiving, education, health, and access to suitable jobs may all matter.

05 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Employment by industry

The metropolitan economy has jobs across education and health services, government, trade, business services, and other industries. This chart uses the revised June 2026 values from BLS’s September release. [5]

Large employment totals establish the scale and composition of the regional economy. They do not establish how many vacancies exist, whether residents can reach those jobs, or whether a worker’s skills match an employer’s requirements. The evidence therefore does not prove that Syracuse has enough accessible jobs for everyone.

06 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Wage inequality

BLS’s May 2025 metro table places the median annual wage at $52,990. The $67,470 value labeled as a median in the draft is the mean. The 10th- and 90th-percentile wages are $34,290 and $112,260, respectively. [18]

Dividing the two thresholds gives a ratio of 3.27. This measures dispersion among wages, not the wealth gap between households. Claims about the national ratio, the top 1%, or groups’ shares of total income are not supported by the selected table and are not repeated here.

07 / EVIDENCE & ANALYSIS

Higher- and lower-wage occupations

Selected occupations illustrate how wage levels differ within the same regional economy. Switch between medians and means to see why consistent definitions matter: a few very high wages can pull a mean above the median. [18]

The employment chart gives a second perspective on scale. The source reports jobs per 1,000 as well as employment counts. For example, home health and personal care aides represent about 2.77% of OEWS employment, not the 27.7% stated in the draft. These selected occupations are not a complete accounting of all jobs. [18]

Read the evidence in context

ACS figures are estimates. The manuscript did not retain margins of error, so small differences should not be treated as statistically significant. “Source checked” means the displayed values were matched to a source during this review. “Paper transcription” identifies values retained from the manuscript that were not fully reproduced independently.

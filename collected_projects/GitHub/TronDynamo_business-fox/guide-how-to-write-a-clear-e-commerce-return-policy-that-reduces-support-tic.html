How to Write a Clear E-Commerce Return Policy That Reduces Support Tickets

A clear return policy does more than protect your business. It sets expectations before a customer buys, which cuts down on support emails and builds trust.

Here is how to write a straightforward return policy for your online store.

1. State the time window clearly Be exact about how many days a customer has to initiate a return. Common windows are 14, 30, or 60 days from the delivery date. Avoid vague phrases like "reasonable time."

2. Define condition requirements State what condition the returned items must be in. For example, specify whether items must be unworn, in original packaging, or with tags attached. If you do not accept returns on certain items—like final sale products, custom goods, or undergarments—list those clearly at the top.

3. Explain who pays for shipping Be upfront about return shipping costs. Do you provide a prepaid label, or does the customer pay for return shipping? If you deduct a restocking fee from the refund, state the exact amount or percentage.

4. Detail the refund method Let customers know how they will get their money back. Do you issue refunds to the original payment method, or do you only offer store credit? Also, state how long it takes to process the refund once you receive the returned item.

5. Outline the step-by-step process Make the return process easy to follow. Tell the customer where to start a return, whether they need to print a label, and how to pack the item.

Where to put your policy Do not hide your return policy on a separate page that is hard to find. Link it in your website footer, on product pages near the Add to Cart button, and in the automated order confirmation email.

How to Write an About Page for Your Local Service Business

Your local service business About page is often the second page potential customers visit, right after your homepage. Most businesses treat it as a history lesson or a place to list awards. Customers do not read About pages to learn when you were founded. They read them to figure out if they can trust you to come into their home or business and do a job right.

Here is a practical, step-by-step approach to writing an About page that builds trust and turns readers into inquiries.

Step 1: Lead with the customer's problem

Start by acknowledging the exact frustration your customers face. If you run a plumbing company, talk about the stress of a leaking pipe on a Sunday morning. If you do residential cleaning, mention the exhaustion of trying to keep a house clean while working full-time. Show the reader immediately that you understand their world.

Step 2: Explain what you do and who you do it for

Be specific about your service area and your specialties. Vague statements like "we provide top-tier solutions for all clients" tell the reader nothing. Instead, use clear language: "We provide residential plumbing repairs and water heater installations across [your city name] and surrounding neighborhoods."

Step 3: Introduce the people behind the work

Local service is personal. Customers want to know who is showing up at their door. Include a real photo of yourself or your team, not stock photography. Share how long you have been doing this work and why you started the business. Keep it straightforward. Mention your licensing or certifications if applicable, as these provide necessary reassurance.

Step 4: Describe your actual process

Take the mystery out of hiring you. Tell the reader what happens after they reach out. For example: 1. You call or fill out our online form. 2. We visit your property to assess the work and provide a clear quote. 3. We complete the job on schedule and clean up the workspace.

When customers know what to expect, anxiety drops and they are more likely to contact you.

Step 5: End with a clear next step

Do not leave the reader wondering what to do next. Tell them how to reach you, whether that is calling your phone number or filling out a contact form. Keep the call to action simple and direct.

How to Write an About Page That Builds Trust With Customers

Your about page is often the second most visited page on your small business website, right after the home page. Visitors land here when they want to know who is behind the product or service. If the page is full of corporate jargon or vague mission statements, potential customers leave without trusting you.

Here is a practical, step-by-step approach to writing an about page that speaks directly to your customers and builds real credibility.

Step 1: Shift the focus to the reader Most about pages start with a lengthy history of the founder and the company founding date. Customers care about your history only insofar as it proves you can solve their current problem. Instead of starting with yourself, start with the customer. Acknowledge what brought them to your site and what they need. For example, if you run a local plumbing business, start by acknowledging the stress of a sudden pipe leak and how quickly you arrive to fix it.

Step 2: State clearly what you do in plain language Within the first three seconds of landing on the page, the reader should know exactly what you offer and who it is for. Avoid clever puns or abstract descriptions. If you sell accounting software for freelancers, say so in the first paragraph. Clarity beats cleverness every single time when someone is trying to understand your business.

Step 3: Tell your origin story briefly People buy from people. Share why you started the business, but keep it concise and grounded in a real human experience. Mention what frustration in the market led you to create your product or service. If you have [your years of experience] in your industry, include that detail to establish authority. Keep this section to one or two short paragraphs.

Step 4: Show, do not just tell Include concrete proof of your work. This can be customer testimonials, years in business, or specific results you have achieved for clients. If you have certification or specialized training that matters in your field, list it here. Let your track record do the heavy lifting instead of adjectives.

Step 5: End with a clear next step Do not leave the reader at a dead end. Once they know who you are and what you do, tell them what to do next. Do they need to book a call, view your products, or contact you for a quote? Give them a single, direct action to take.

How to Write an About Page for Your Independent Online Shop

Your online shop's About page is often the second most visited page on your site after the homepage. Shoppers click here when they are close to buying but want to know who is behind the products. A weak About page is usually a wall of corporate text or a generic mission statement. A good About page builds trust and answers the customer's unstated question: "Can I trust this person with my money?"

Here is a practical, step-by-step framework to write an About page that works for an independent shop.

1. Lead with the customer, not your biography While the page is about your shop, the reader cares most about themselves. Start by acknowledging the problem you solve or the reason you started the shop. If you make durable canvas backpacks, start by talking about how frustrating it is to buy bags that fray after six months of daily commuting. Connect your origin story to their daily needs.

2. Tell the real origin story People buy from independent shops because they want a human connection. Skip the corporate third-person voice and write in the first person. Explain simply when you started, where you work, and why you care about the craft. If you started at your kitchen table with [your starting tool or machine], say so. Specific details make your story believable.

3. Show your process and materials Shoppers on independent sites want to know quality exists behind the photos. Describe how you source your materials or how an item is made. Mention specific details such as [your material type] or [your production time]. This reassures buyers that they are getting a carefully made item rather than mass-produced inventory.

4. Include clear photos Text alone is not enough for an online shop. Include a clear, well-lit photo of yourself at work in your workspace. Seeing the maker behind the goods removes the anonymity of the internet. It turns your shop from a faceless website into a real small business.

5. End with a clear next step Once a reader finishes your story, they should not hit a dead end. Guide them back into your shop. Add links to your best-selling collection, your newest items, or your newsletter signup. Make it easy for them to take the next step.
